# Rosetta Objects — Options Paper

**Status:** for decision. Authority C throughout; nothing here is canon. Follows the Q4
amendment at `06-PHASE1-RECOMMENDATION.md` §6.1.

---

## 1. What the brief now is

Two decisions have landed and together they change what we are building.

**A Rosetta object exists**, held by the author, entering the world as a dated event
several years after ship. **And its narrator is not authoritative** — a translated text
carries a translator, and users must work out for themselves whether any given reading is
objective.

The second decision is the larger one. It means we are not building a **key**. We are
building **evidence with a provenance**, which is what every real bilingual actually is:
the Rosetta Stone's Greek is a priestly decree written by interested parties, and the
Pyrgi Tablets' Phoenician is a dedication, not a glossary.

---

## 2. The constraint that governs every option

Benchmark §4.2, Design rule 2: the corpus must contain **verifiable footholds** — items
whose reading can be confirmed or refuted by published evidence. Target Linear B /
Etruscan. Do not target Voynich, where *"nothing can be confirmed or refuted, so no
result accumulates"* and cultural energy runs decoupled from intellectual yield.

Unreliable narration pushes directly toward the Voynich failure. If every key is
testimony and no testimony can be checked, users are not deducing — they are picking a
favourite, forever, with no mechanism that could ever settle anything. That is the F2
failure mode wearing a better costume.

**So the governing rule for the whole slate:** unreliability may be layered on top of a
checkable floor. It may never replace one. Users can only judge a narrator's objectivity
if some portion of what that narrator says is independently testable.

This gives the axis that organises the options.

**Checkable** — the object's claims can be tested against something outside the object:
a route that can be walked, an outcome in HEL's records, an internal structure that
either holds or does not.

**Testimonial** — the object asserts meaning. Believing it is a judgement about the
author, not about evidence.

The design needs both. Checkable objects are narrow, dull and solid. Testimonial objects
are broad, seductive and unsound. **The game lives in the gap between them.**

## 3. The options

### R1 — The ostensive pair *(checkable)*

A substrate record sited with the thing it describes, where the referent is physically
present: a route notation beside the route, confirmable by traversal.

This is the **PY Ta 641** model, the strongest single precedent in the benchmark — the
Pylos tablet where *ti-ri-po-de* stood next to a drawing of a tripod, and other entries
matched their own ideograms by handle count. Benchmark §4.2 calls that tablet "the model
… the moment where an interpretation stops being an opinion."

- **Authority D:** clean. Asserts nothing about who wrote it.
- **Yield:** narrow. Concrete edge and traversal vocabulary only; nothing abstract,
  nothing modal, nothing epistemic.
- **Where unreliability enters:** not in whether the correspondence holds, but in what it
  *means*. You have confirmed that the record predicts the route. You have not confirmed
  that the record is *about* the route rather than about something the route is also a
  case of. The correlation is objective; the referent is not.
- **Cost:** high. Requires a designed route and a matched text, both load-bearing.

### R2 — The structural key *(checkable)*

A substrate-internal ordered enumeration — an index, a paradigm, a canonical sequence —
that confirms structure without conferring meaning.

The **Ugaritic abecedary** model: a tablet recovered after decipherment that confirmed
the sign order outright, settling the script without settling the language.

- **Authority D:** mild exposure. Implies the makers enumerated or catalogued something,
  which is close to the weakest claim it is possible to make about an author.
- **Yield:** large for morphology, sign order and paradigm shape. **Exactly zero for
  semantics.**
- **Where unreliability enters:** barely. This is the most objective object available,
  and that is precisely its use — it is the floor everything else is measured against.
- **Cost:** low. It is a table.

**R2 is the option that delivers the Etruscan condition as an event.** After it lands,
users can *read* — parse, segment, inflect, predict well-formedness — and still not know
what anything means. That is the most valuable state this project can be in, and R2 puts
users there deliberately rather than leaving them stalled short of it.

### R3 — The human primer *(testimonial)*

A teaching key written by a named analyst: wordlist, grammar sketch, confident glosses.
Recovered, circulated, incomplete.

The precedent to keep in view is **Athanasius Kircher**, who published confident
hieroglyphic translations that were entirely wrong and were believed for two centuries.

- **Authority D:** clean. Human-authored throughout.
- **Yield:** broad, fluent, immediately usable, and *unsound in unknown proportion*.
- **Where unreliability enters:** everywhere, and legibly. Users get a document that
  answers far more than the evidence can support, by an author with a reputation, a
  school affiliation, and a motive.
- **Cost:** very low. It is a document.
- **Risk:** on its own it is noise. Shipped after R1/R2, it is the central object of the
  entire metagame.

### R4 — The expedition procedural *(semi-checkable)*

A field log correlating notation with actions taken and what happened next.

- **Authority D:** clean.
- **Yield:** procedural and imperative vocabulary; the register HEL most needs and can
  least confirm.
- **Where unreliability enters:** structurally, and this is its charm. It is written
  after the fact, by whoever came back. **Survivorship bias is literal here** — the
  readings that got people killed are underrepresented because the people holding them
  did not file reports.
- **Checkability:** partial. Outcomes appear in HEL's own records, so claims can be
  cross-checked against the operational-correlation channel already in the design.
- **Cost:** low to moderate.

### R5 — The annotated artifact *(testimonial, high yield)*

An object bearing substrate text with a translation added **later, in a human hand**.

Not a bilingual: the second language was added by us, not by them. This is the **Pyrgi**
silhouette without Pyrgi's cosmology — a parallel text whose second half has a human
author who can be doubted, dated and placed.

- **Authority D:** clean, and deliberately so. See R6.
- **Yield:** highest of any single option.
- **Where unreliability enters:** in the annotator. On what basis did they write this?
  Some annotations may be copied from R3 and inherit its errors wholesale, which gives
  users a genuine stemma problem — two sources agreeing because one copied the other is
  not corroboration.
- **Cost:** moderate.

### R6 — The true bilingual *(rejected, recorded)*

Substrate and human language, both original to the object.

**Recommend against, and the reason is not aesthetic.** A true bilingual asserts that the
makers knew a human language, which resolves contact and therefore resolves the makers.
That is Authority D and it is prohibited. R5 delivers most of the yield at none of the
cost. Recorded here so the rejection is visible rather than silently assumed.

## 5. Recommendation

**Ship a slate, in this order. The order is the recommendation.**

1. **R2 first.** Structure, checkable, no semantics. Users gain the ability to read
   without the ability to understand, and the Etruscan condition becomes something that
   *happened* rather than something they failed to get past.
2. **R1 second.** A small number of ostensive pairs. Narrow, solid, dull, and the only
   objective semantic anchors in the world.
3. **R3, R4, R5 after.** Testimonial objects that reach far beyond what R1 and R2
   support, disagreeing with each other and with HEL's published readings.

**Sequencing is not a detail.** If testimony lands first, the community anchors on it
before any adjudication mechanism exists, and the argument never converges on anything —
Voynich. If the checkable floor lands first, every later testimonial object arrives into
a world that can already partially grade it.

### The apparatus every object carries

Each Rosetta object ships with four things, and the fourth is the one that makes
unreliable narration *adjudicable* rather than merely frustrating:

1. **Provenance** — where it came from, through whose hands, with what gaps.
2. **Interest** — what its author stood to gain from being believed.
3. **Competence** — what its author could and could not have known at the time of
   writing.
4. **Checkable yield, published as a fraction.** Of the N glosses this object asserts,
   *n* are independently testable against R1/R2, and of those, *k* agree.

That fourth item is the existing denominator discipline pointed at a new target. It gives
users a calibration basis rather than a vibe: *this primer is right about 26 of the 31
things we can check, so what do we do with the other 209?* — a question with a real
methodology behind it, and still no answer.

## 6. Consequence for §6.1, stated plainly

§6.1 claimed that a Rosetta object makes every shipped gloss "marked right or wrong in
public on a known date." **With unreliable narrators that is too strong**, and the
correction matters for how much pressure sits on the corpus: a testimonial object that
contradicts a HEL reading does not refute it. It opens a dispute. Only R1 and R2 can
actually settle anything, and their reach is deliberately small.

**What survives unchanged is the ordering requirement**, and it is now stronger rather
than weaker. Author-side ground truth must exist before any corpus ships — not because
the truth will be publicly tested, but because **making objects disagree in controlled,
meaningful ways requires knowing exactly what is true.** Contradictions that arise from
drift are indistinguishable from contradictions that were designed, until someone checks
carefully, at which point they are very distinguishable indeed.

## 7. Open

- **Does the slate touch Stratum III?** Recommend no. The reserve should survive every
  reveal, or the world spends its mystery at the loudest possible moment.
- **How many R3/R4/R5 objects, and do any two share a source?** A copied annotation
  inheriting a primer's errors is the best available lesson in what corroboration is not.
- **Does HEL's Archive possess any of these at ship, unrecognised?** A misfiled R2 sitting
  in the published corpus the whole time is nearly free and rewards rereading.

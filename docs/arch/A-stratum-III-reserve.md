## 0. The finding, and what this file is

The audit's central finding is not that the architecture lacks poetry. It is that the
architecture has **nowhere to put any**:

> Every clause is `node-state + edge-half + incidence + grade + polarity + coordinate +
> warrant`… Nothing sayable that is not a graph fact plus a provenance stamp.

Architecture A concedes it in its own voice — *"If a writer sits down to write Namárië
in this system, there is nothing to write it with."* That concession is correct about
the **parsable** corpus and it should stand. The error is treating the parsable corpus
as the whole corpus.

Stratum III is currently defined as *"material between root and edge half that Stratum I
lacks."* That definition is vacuous — it says the material is not something else — and
it leaves roughly **17% of the corpus doing no work at all.** This file redefines it.

**The redefinition, in one sentence.**

> **Stratum III is the material whose morphology resolves to no edge class, no
> incidence, no grade, no polarity and no coordinate function whatever, which stands in
> no attested contact with any material that does, and which the Archive therefore
> cannot parse — not yet, but in principle, by every method it possesses.**

What that yields, by construction, is **the part that isn't topology**. It is
untranslatable, resistant and fragmentary, which is exactly what handoff §7.1 asks for.
It costs nothing, because the material is already in the corpus. And it is the only
place in this design where something that is not a graph fact can live.

**What this file is not.** It is not a lexicon, not a poem, and not a translation. It
specifies a **class**, its **classification procedure**, the **reasons the procedure
fails to go further**, the **distributional facts** that make the class credible, the
**three standing readings** of what it is, the **rules a future writer must work
under**, and **one worked example** carrying no gloss.

## 1. Bookkeeping: what the redefinition does to the catalogue

A redefinition of a stratum is a philological event and must have a date, an author, a
count, and an opponent (benchmark C8; Phase 1 recommendation P11). All Authority C.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Old definition** | 1949 re-catalogue: "expanded — material intervening between root and edge half that Stratum I lacks." Defined by an excess relative to Stratum I. |
| **New definition** | The **Hessell redefinition, 1976**: defined by the *absence of functional exponents* and by *non-contact*, not by excess (§2). |
| **Effect on the catalogue** | Old Stratum III held **132** sequences. The redefinition retains **79**. The remaining **53** are re-filed: 34 to Stratum I as segments with expanded incidence material (they do bear edge halves; the 1949 definition was reading the expansion and not the anchor), 19 to *damaged*. |
| **Standing objection** | The 1949 definition's principal defender never accepted the redefinition, on the ground that a class defined by what the Archive cannot do is a statement about the Archive. **The objection is correct** (§2.3) and the Archive prints it in the catalogue's front matter rather than answering it. |
| **Precedent for adoption** | The Cardwell revision of 1948 (A §8, Example 2): a redefinition adopted while its predecessor stayed in the archive as G0, taught but withdrawn from use. Same procedure here. |

**Share of corpus:** ~697 tokens in 79 sequences, ~17% of the CSC. Unchanged in
magnitude by the redefinition; substantially changed in membership.

## 2. Formal definition

The Archive must be able to take a newly recovered fragment and return a class. This is
a procedure, not a judgement of character. It is stated so a clerk can run it and two
clerks get the same answer.

### 2.1 The three conditions

A catalogued sequence is **Stratum III** if and only if it satisfies all three.

**Condition 1 — Null parse (the functional test).**
No unit in the sequence is assignable to any functional paradigm: not to the seven edge
classes (tail or head half, whole or broken), not to the four incidence markers, not to
the four grades, not to the four polarities, not to any of the three coordinate slots,
and not to the warrant inventory. Assignment is by table lookup against the 1908
Transcription Standard as amended; the test is mechanical and produces a signed
worksheet. **A single assignable unit anywhere in the sequence disqualifies it.**

**Condition 2 — Non-contact and damage exclusion.**
The null parse must occur where damage is excluded as its cause. Three requirements:

- **(a) Intactness.** The conservator's report certifies the marked field as
  undamaged: no lacuna, no break, no margin loss, no abrasion crossing the field. The
  sequence does not run to the object's edge at either end.
- **(b) Non-contact.** No unit of the sequence stands within a segment, shares a
  coordinate cartouche, or bears a lead-line to a warrant tick. Where the object also
  carries a trace, the Stratum III field begins after a *completed* segment — one whose
  warrant is present — and never interrupts one.
- **(c) Channel-specific corroboration.** For Ch-2 (standing patterns), the plate must
  be a photographic original, not a copyist's hand, since the pre-photographic records
  are copies of copies. For Ch-4 (human transmission), the sequence must be attested
  from **two independent branch traditions**, because a single tradition cannot be
  distinguished from a single person's error.

**Condition 3 — Length threshold.**
The sequence is **six units or longer**. Below six, a null parse is not distinguishable
from chance: given the functional inventory's corpus frequencies, a random run of five
units contains no assignable unit about 11% of the time. At six the expected
false-positive rate is 4.3%; at nine it is 1.2%. Sequences of six to eight units are
filed **III (provisional)**; nine and above, **III**. Sequences of five or fewer that
would otherwise qualify are filed **unassigned, short** and are not corpus evidence for
anything.

### 2.2 Stratum III versus damaged Stratum I — the discriminator

This is the load-bearing distinction and the audit is right to demand it. The two look
identical on the page: a run of units with no recoverable function.

**They are told apart by where the absence sits, not by what is absent.**

Damage in this corpus is not random. It is a physical process with a known
distribution, and Architecture A already depends on that fact (a fragment ending `-ken ⌀`
constrains what the lost node was). Damage eats **peripheries**: object margins, the
outermost extent of an incised field, lead-lines and their warrant ticks, and — in the
human channel — short unstressed material. Roughly 41% of CSC sequences terminate in a
broken half, and essentially all of those breaks are at a margin or a fracture.

Stratum III's absence sits in the **middle of intact material**, bounded on both sides
by surface the conservator certifies as original, frequently flanked by segments that
parse perfectly. The relevant contrast is not *incomplete* versus *complete*. It is:

| | Damaged Stratum I | Stratum III |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where function is missing | At a break, margin, or abraded field | In certified intact surface, medially |
| Residue | Partial exponents survive: a bare tail, a head with no root, a cartouche with two slots and a torn third | **Zero exponents. Not one, in 697 tokens.** |
| Neighbours | Adjacent material is also degraded | Adjacent material frequently parses at G1 |
| Reconstructability | The break constrains the missing unit; the class is often recoverable | Nothing constrains anything; there is no slot to reconstruct into |
| Catalogue outcome | *damaged*, with the reconstruction attempt appended | **III** |

A one-line operational form of the discriminator, for field use:

> **A damaged segment is a sentence with a hole in it. A Stratum III sequence is not a
> sentence and has no hole.**

### 2.3 What the definition is defined relative to — and this must stay printed

**Stratum III is a property of HEL's instruments, not of the material.** The definition
says the Archive's parser returns nothing. It does not say there is nothing to return.
Three consequences that must appear wherever the class is described:

1. **It is relative to a segmentation HEL knows is partly wrong.** Condition 1 is
   lookup against the 1908 Standard, and the 1949 revision concluded that roughly thirty
   units are probably consonant-final in the source and have been mis-segmented since
   1908. **If the segmentation is wrong, some Stratum III material is misparsed Stratum
   I.** The Archive publishes the count: under the four alternative segmentations
   formally proposed since 1949, between 3 and 11 of the 79 sequences acquire at least
   one assignable unit and would leave the class. None of the four has been adopted;
   none has been refuted.
2. **It is relative to an inventory that may be under-differentiated.** If the
   functional paradigms are HEL's over-differentiation of a smaller opposition set — or
   its under-differentiation of a larger one — Condition 1 is testing the wrong list.
3. **It therefore proves nothing about authorship, intent, or meaning**, and the
   catalogue's front matter says so in those words. A class of things an instrument
   cannot read is evidence about the instrument. Everything in §4 is offered as
   *grounds for interest*, never as proof of content.

This is the same repair the audit prescribes for the conditioned-erosion contradiction,
applied here from the start rather than retrofitted: **the best evidence that something
is there may be an artifact of the tool used to write it down.** The design does not
resolve this. It publishes it.

### 2.4 Dissolution conditions

A class that can never be wrong is not a finding. The Archive publishes the conditions
under which Stratum III would cease to exist as a class:

- **a bilingual** — excluded by the architecture; there is no Rosetta object and none
  will be admitted;
- **a single attested contact** — one Stratum III unit bearing an edge half, taking a
  coordinate, or standing inside a segment would falsify the partition (§4.5) outright;
- **a resegmentation** that yields exponents across the class rather than in 3–11
  sequences;
- **an operational correlate** — any covariance between Stratum III presence and a
  recorded traversal outcome (§3.2).

**None has occurred in the fifty years since the redefinition.** That is the honest
state of the question: not a mystery being solved slowly, but four named ways it could
break, none of which has.

## 3. Why the Archive cannot parse it

The failure must be principled rather than convenient. HEL has exactly three
decipherment methods and has never had a fourth. **All three are inapplicable to
Stratum III in principle — not unsuccessful, inapplicable** — and each for a different
reason.

### 3.1 Method 1 — Morphological parse. Fails for want of an anchor.

This architecture's morphology is not a list of affixes. It is a set of **positions
defined relative to the bipartite edge**:

```
[EDGEHEAD-] ROOT [-INCIDENCE] [-EDGETAIL] [-GRADE] [-POLARITY] [=COORDINATE]
```

Incidence is *what sits between root and edge tail*. Grade and polarity are *what sits
on the edge*. The coordinate is an enclitic on a **node word**, and a node word is
defined as a word bearing an edge half or standing at a chain terminus. The warrant is
*what closes a segment*, and a segment is a chain of node words.

Every one of those definitions has the edge half as its origin of coordinates. Remove
it and the template does not become ambiguous — it becomes **unaddressable**. There is
no slot 2 if there is no slot 1.

Worse, and this is the part that makes the failure structural rather than stubborn:
**HEL's word boundaries were themselves recovered from edge-half alignment.** The 1908
Standard segmented the corpus by finding the recurring tail/head pairs and cutting
around them. In Stratum III there is nothing to cut around. The Archive can transcribe
the units — the Standard assigns syllables to recurring shapes — but it **cannot certify
that its unit boundaries are the material's boundaries.** Every count in §4 is a count
of HEL's units. This is stated in the catalogue and not softened.

### 3.2 Method 2 — Operational correlation. Fails for want of a correlate.

The Archive has never verified a reading semantically. Decipherment proceeds by
**operational correlation**: sequence X is present at loci where traversal subsequently
succeeded, sequence Y where it did not. That method is the entire basis of the G2 grade
— readings acted on for a century and never demonstrated.

It requires the material to **covary with something HEL can independently observe.**
Stratum III does not. The Archive has run the correlation four times (1954, 1971, 1976,
2003) against every outcome variable it records — traversal success, displacement
magnitude, phase sign, recurrence, expedition loss — and published the result each time
with the denominator: **no association above chance, at any locus class, in any
channel.** The one method HEL has that produces confirmations produces nothing here.

Note carefully what this does *not* say. It does not say Stratum III is inert. It says
Stratum III does not predict the four things HEL knows how to measure. A skeptic in the
Archive puts it the other way round and is not answerable: *we tested it against the
questions our instruments ask, and it does not answer those questions.*

### 3.3 Method 3 — Distributional inference. Fails for want of cross-record repetition.

The method that cracked Linear B is positional: a sign group recurring **in the same
slot across hundreds of records** can be assigned a function before it is assigned a
meaning. That method needs repetition *between* records.

Stratum III repeats *within* records and almost never between them (§4.2). Of 412
distinct Stratum III types, **301 occur in exactly one sequence** — and Stratum III
holds about two-thirds of the whole corpus's hapax units in 17% of its tokens. (301
against roughly 146 in the functional strata, which is ~24% of their ~610 types: 301 of
about 450, or 67%. §4.2 carries both figures.) There is no
paradigm to build, because there are no minimal pairs across contexts to build one from.

### 3.4 The fourth method HEL does not have

There is no bilingual, and this architecture does not permit one. Nothing in the corpus
is confirmable against a known referent by translation, only by correlation (§3.2), and
correlation is inapplicable here.

### 3.5 The principled statement, for the catalogue front matter

> Stratum III is not an unsolved problem. It is the class of material on which all three
> of the Archive's methods are structurally inapplicable: the parse has no anchor, the
> correlation has no correlate, and the distribution has no cross-record repetition. The
> Archive holds no expectation of a reading and does not maintain a working hypothesis.
> Four conditions would dissolve the class (§2.4). None has occurred.

That is the difference between *permanently closed* and *not got round to yet*. The
first is a design commitment the project can ship. The second is a promise the project
would eventually have to break.

## 4. Distributional signature

This is the Linear A situation, and it is worth naming precisely because it is the exact
epistemic position we want: **pronounceable, evidently organised, semantically closed.**
Linear A can be read aloud — approximately, using Linear B's sign values — and it is
plainly structured, and it has not been read. Nobody thinks it is decoration. Nobody can
say what it says.

The facts below are what convinced the Archive that Stratum III is organised. Every one
of them is a fact about **form**, none is a fact about meaning, and each is followed by
its deflation, because a fact with no deflation printed beside it is an argument, not a
finding. All counts Authority C, production-only.

### 4.1 Length: quantised

79 sequences. Mean length 8.8 units; range 6 to 21.

**74 of the 79 have a unit count divisible by three** (94%), against ~33% expected from
the corpus's length distribution. Of the remaining five, three are the shortest in the
class (6-unit provisionals with contested unit boundaries) and two are Ch-2 plates where
the field's extent is a judgement call.

The internal partition is triadic as well: in the 61 sequences long enough to test, the
subdivision falls at exact thirds by every measure the Archive can apply — repetition
period, positional class of the first unit in each group, and terminal-syllable
agreement.

*Deflation.* Unit boundaries are HEL's (§3.1). A segmentation procedure that cut the
material into a different number of units would produce a different divisibility fact.
The Archive has checked this against the four alternative segmentations: three preserve
the triadic result, one destroys it. That one is the least favoured on independent
grounds, **which is exactly the situation in which a discipline convinces itself.**

### 4.2 Repetition: dense inside, absent outside

The single most distinctive property of the class, and the one that is hardest to
explain away.

| | Stratum III | Functional strata (I–II) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distinct types | 412 | ~610 corpus-wide |
| Types occurring in exactly one sequence | **301 (73%)** | ~24% |
| Probability a type in a sequence recurs *within that same sequence* | **0.61** | 0.09 |
| Probability a type recurs in *any other* sequence | **0.04** | 0.58 |

Read the bottom two rows together. **The functional corpus reuses its vocabulary across
records and not within them.** That is the profile of a catalogue: a fixed inventory of
labels, each applied once per record. **Stratum III does the opposite** — it reuses its
vocabulary intensely within a record and almost never outside it. That is the profile of
**individual compositions**, not of entries in a register.

*Deflation.* It is also the profile of a set of unrelated things filed together — 79
objects from 79 unrelated processes would show exactly this, since each would have its
own recurring elements and share nothing with the others. The Palimpsest school makes
precisely this argument (§5.3) and it has never been refuted.

### 4.3 Position: after the record has finished speaking

Stratum III never begins a traced face and never interrupts a segment.

| Context | n |
| --- | --- |
| On objects also bearing a trace: begins **after a completed segment** — one whose warrant is present — and runs to the end of the field | 41 |
| On objects bearing **no trace line at all**: occupies the entire marked field | 30 |
| Ch-2 standing patterns, appearing as a whole record | 6 |
| Ch-4 human transmission, attested from two independent branch traditions | 2 |
| **Sequence-initial on a traced face** | **0** |
| **Interrupting a segment** | **0** |

The Archive's flattest description of the positional fact, and the one that has caused
the most trouble in print: **on every object where both occur, the topology finishes
first.** The record states its edges, states its coordinate, states how it knows — and
then continues.

*Deflation.* On an object with two marked fields, a clerk with a parser will always find
the parsable one first and call it the beginning. Reading order on these objects is
itself a reconstruction. The trace is directional; a field with no trace is not.

### 4.4 Channel and locus distribution

- **Channel:** 88% Ch-1 incised · 9% Ch-2 standing · 3% Ch-4 human transmission ·
  **0% Ch-3 instrument.** The absence from Ch-3 is doing real work: it is the cleanest
  separation between Stratum III and Stratum IV, which is entirely Ch-3. Whatever the
  Palimpsest school is right about, it is not that these two are the same thing.
- **Locus:** 79 sequences from 31 loci. **19 of the 31 are loci for which HEL's
  reconstruction shows no recovered outbound edge** — you can get there; the map shows
  nothing leaving — against 22% of surveyed loci in that class overall.

*Deflation, and it is a serious one.* Expeditions to loci with no recovered outbound
edge stay longer, survey harder, and recover more of everything. The association may be
entirely an artifact of dwell time. The Archive publishes the recovery-per-hour
denominator alongside the raw figure and the association survives it at a reduced
magnitude; it has never published a corrected figure it is willing to defend. This is
the same selection problem that contaminates the operational-correlation channel
generally, and it is printed here for the same reason.

### 4.5 The partition — the hard fact

**No Stratum III unit has ever been attested bearing an edge half, taking a coordinate,
carrying a grade or polarity, occupying a warrant slot, or standing inside a segment. No
functional unit has ever been attested inside a Stratum III sequence.**

In ~4,100 tokens, across four channels, over 118 years of cataloguing: zero contact, in
both directions. Two vocabularies of 412 and ~610 types, sharing nothing.

This is the strongest single statistical fact about the corpus and nobody can explain
it. It is also the class's own falsification condition (§2.4): one attested contact ends
the partition.

*Deflation, and it is devastating.* **The partition may be a restatement of the sorting
rule.** Material that parsed went into Strata I–II; material that did not went here. Of
course they do not mix — not mixing is how they were separated. The Archive's answer is
that the sorting rule predicts a *heterogeneous* residue and Stratum III is
conspicuously homogeneous (§4.1, §4.2, §4.6). The Palimpsest answer to the answer is
that conservators preferentially catalogue well-preserved unparsable fields and discard
the rest, which manufactures homogeneity at the point of accession. **Unresolved, and
this design does not resolve it.**

### 4.6 Form: a different silhouette from the functional lexicon

Under the 1908 Standard, Stratum III material is fully pronounceable and phonotactically
identical to the rest of the corpus — same eleven consonants, same five vowels, same
`C V (C)` syllable, same coda set `{n s r l}`. It can be read aloud by anyone who can
read the rest.

It does not *sound* like the rest.

| | Stratum III | Functional units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mean syllables per unit | 3.1 | 2.0 |
| Tokens ending in a coda | **84%** | 11% |
| Units sharing their first CV with another unit in the same class | **common** | **zero, by 1908 committee rule** |

The third row is the interesting one. The 1908 committee imposed a distinctness rule —
no two units may share their first CV — because edge halves are spoken unstressed and
fast, and confusable onsets kill route commands. The rule was applied to the material
the committee could parse. **Stratum III does not obey it**, and the committee minutes
do not record whether that is because the material resisted the rule or because nobody
bothered to apply it to material that was not going into anyone's mouth.

*Deflation, and it is the deepest one available.* **The reason Stratum III is
pronounceable is that HEL assigned the sounds.** Every property in this table is a
property of the 1908 assignment procedure operating on recurring shapes. If the
procedure ran out of short distinct syllables and started issuing longer ones for the
material nobody was going to say aloud, the entire silhouette difference is an artifact
of a stationery decision made in 1908. The Archive cannot rule this out and does not try.

## 5. What the three schools say it is

Per the Three-School Test (A §2.2): every construction admitted to the design must be
readable under all three standing interpretations. Stratum III is not an exception to
that rule; it is the place where the three schools disagree most productively, because
each of them has a *complete and coherent* account of the same facts, and the three
accounts are mutually exclusive.

**One of the three is deflationary and non-linguistic. It is not the weakest.**

### 5.1 Utterance — "this is the part that was language"

**Claim.** The functional strata are the address on the envelope. Stratum III is the
letter. It is unparseable because HEL reconstructed the grammar of the envelope and then
tried to read the letter with it.

**Best evidence.** §4.2, and it is genuinely strong: high within-record repetition with
near-zero between-record repetition is the profile of composition and is not the profile
of any catalogue, register, or label system. Add §4.3 — the topology finishes first,
and this comes after the record has already said how it knows. Add §4.1 — quantised
length with internal triadic partition is what verse does and is not what data does.

**What it must concede.** It has no positive evidence of a semantic category. Not one.
It is an argument from the shape of the residue, and every element of that shape has a
deflation printed beside it (§4). It also has a motive problem its opponents press
hard: it is the reading that the people who want this to be a language would arrive at.

**What it may never be used to establish.** That there was a speaker; that anything was
biologically produced; that the composer was not human (§6.4, the forgery problem).
Handoff §5.3 forbids all three, and Utterance is the school most likely to leak them.
The Archive's house rule is that Utterance papers may say *composition* and may not say
*composer*.

### 5.2 Instrument — "this is payload"

**Claim.** A transport format carries a header it parses and a body it does not
interpret. Stratum III is the body. It was never meant to be read by whatever reads the
rest, and HEL is in the position of a router trying to understand the mail.

**Best evidence.** The partition (§4.5) is *exactly* what a format designer would build:
header vocabulary and payload vocabulary disjoint by construction, because a header
parser that could mistake payload for header is a broken parser. Quantised length
(§4.1) is a block size. The triadic subdivision is framing. The refrain-like recurrence
that Utterance hears as a refrain is a **delimiter**, occurring at block boundaries
because that is where delimiters occur. Position (§4.3) is payload following header,
which is where payload goes.

**What it must concede.** It explains the shape and explains nothing about the content,
and it is untestable in exactly the way its opponents say: any organised material can be
called payload, and a school whose account of the data is *"this is the part we are not
supposed to understand"* has made a virtue of the thing it cannot do. It also has to
explain §4.2's hapax rate, which is high for a format with any compression or
addressing.

**Where it is strongest, and this matters for the project:** under Instrument, Stratum
III **need not be in the same system at all** — it may be a key, an identifier, a
checksum, a compressed state, or arbitrary content in a scheme HEL has no example of.
Which means it can be anything, permanently, without the corpus growing by one token.

### 5.3 Palimpsest — "it is not corpus, and the class is an artifact of filing"

**The deflationary reading, and it has the best claim to being right.**

**Claim.** Stratum III is not a stratum of anything. It is the drawer. The 1908
consolidation of the branch registers had to put every recovered field somewhere;
anything that parsed became Strata I–II and everything left over accumulated here, and a
filing category has been mistaken for a formal class ever since. On the strong version,
much of it is **not semiotic at all** — tool chatter, crystallisation, wear, casting
flash, manufacturing artifact, or the periodic structure of a physical process — and it
appears in syllables only because the 1908 Standard's job was to assign syllables to
recurring shapes, which guarantees that recurring shapes get syllables. **The
organisation HEL detects is the organisation HEL's transcription procedure inserted.**

**Best evidence.** Every deflation in §4, which are Palimpsest arguments: the partition
restates the sorting rule (§4.5); the silhouette restates the assignment procedure
(§4.6); the length quantisation depends on HEL's unit boundaries (§4.1, §3.1); the locus
association may be dwell time (§4.4). And one historical fact the school never stops
citing: **the object in §8 was accessioned in 1897 and filed by a clerk as "ornament,
non-linguistic" — the Palimpsest reading, entered fifty-five years before the Palimpsest
school existed, by someone with no theory at all.**

**What it must concede.** The sorting rule predicts a junk drawer, and junk drawers are
heterogeneous. This one is not: 94% divisibility by three, a stable repetition profile,
a consistent positional rule, zero contact in both directions, and a distinct
phonotactic silhouette. Palimpsest answers with accession bias and cannot show it.

**Why this school must be kept strong, as a matter of project discipline.** A
deflationary reading that everyone treats as the loser is decoration. This one has the
best evidence in §4 on its side, it was first in the field by half a century, and **the
project holds no private answer to it.** If Stratum III is where the emotional weight of
this language will live, then the reading that says *there may be nothing there* has to
be the one a reader cannot dismiss. That is what makes the rest of it cost something.

### 5.4 What no school may say

None of the three may be developed toward: the builders in any respect; whether anything
was ever spoken, emitted, or produced by a body; whether the four channels are one
system; whether the corpus refers to this network. Stratum III is defined by the absence
of every functional category, which means it makes no assertions, which means **it
supports no inference about its producer**. The Builder-Inference Test run on Stratum
III returns nothing by construction — the same structural protection Architecture A gets
from having no obligatory subject position, obtained here by having no obligatory
anything.

## 6. What a future writer can put here

This is the point of the file. Everything above exists so that this section is possible
without violating anything.

### 6.1 The expressive space, stated concretely

Architecture A's own list of what the parsable corpus cannot do is the list of what
Stratum III is *for*: **interiority, address, memory, comparison, metaphor, judgement of
value.** In the functional grammar each of those is not merely absent but structurally
excluded — there is no predication, no reference to individuals, no modification, no
coordination, no subordination beyond warrant scope. In Stratum III there is no
structure at all that HEL can identify, and therefore nothing excluded.

What that means in practice, as authorable objects:

| The thing a writer wants | Where it lives now | Where it can live |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A lament | Nowhere. There is no first person, no past, no loss, no value. | A Stratum III sequence with a recovery history that makes the loss the reader's (§8). |
| An address — someone speaking *to* someone | Nowhere. No vocative, no second person, no agent that controls anything. | A Stratum III sequence attested only in the human channel, carried out in someone's mouth. |
| A memory | Nowhere. PASS and PHASE are relative positions, not a past. | A sequence whose only three attestations are separated by ninety years and differ in one unit. |
| A judgement of value | Nowhere, and deliberately: a grammar that could say *good* is a grammar approaching a builder's mind. | A sequence recited at a threshold nobody is required to cross, by crews the Archive has twice told to stop. |
| A comparison | Nowhere. No modification. | A pair of sequences from two loci that share four of six types and differ in the fifth. |
| Something a fan memorises and says aloud | Nowhere: every functional line is a route, and routes are classified (§6.5). | Here, and only here. |

Note what the right-hand column actually contains. **None of those entries is a
translation.** Each is a *form*, a *distribution*, and a *provenance*. That is the whole
method, and §6.3 states it as a rule.

### 6.2 Why this does not resolve anything the canon protects

Three protections, and they are structural rather than editorial.

**It does not decide whether the substrate was ever spoken.** Handoff §5.3. A Stratum III
sequence is transcribed under the 1908 Standard exactly like everything else, which is
HEL's convention adopted for radio and telephone work, and §4.6's deflation says in
terms that its phonaesthetic character may be an artifact of the assignment procedure.
Nothing about how it sounds is evidence about anything. A reader who finds a sequence
beautiful has learned something about the 1908 committee.

**It does not name or describe the builders.** Prohibited shortcut #1, and this is the
error that scrapped the previous attempt. Stratum III has no functional exponent, which
means it cannot assert a graph fact, cannot carry a coordinate, cannot mark an agent,
cannot state a grade or a warrant — it cannot *state* anything HEL can identify.
Whatever a writer intends by it, the shipped artifact asserts nothing, because nothing
in it is parsed. **Its expressive capacity and its builder-opacity are the same
property**: it is expressive precisely because it is outside the system that would fix
its reference, and it is safe for the same reason.

**It does not make the Box decipherable.** Prohibited shortcut #2. Stratum III adds
nothing to the reconstructed grammar, contains no route, and correlates with no observed
network behaviour (§3.2). It *reduces* the corpus's answer-key pressure: 17% of the
material now demonstrably yields nothing, and the audit's Part 4 complaint that "a
complete segment is an answer key for the edge it describes" is answered by a large
class of complete, undamaged, wholly unreadable records sitting next to the readable
ones.

### 6.3 The authoring rules

Binding on any Phase 2–4 writer. All Authority C.

1. **Write the form, the distribution, and the provenance. Never the meaning.** The
   deliverable for a Stratum III text is: the sequence; its position on its object or in
   its recitation; its type/token profile; its channel; its find and use history; and the
   three schools' readings of *it specifically*. A translation is not part of the
   deliverable and will not be accepted as one.
2. **It must pass the classifier.** A new Stratum III text is invalid unless it
   satisfies §2.1 mechanically: zero assignable units, non-contact, length ≥6. This is
   lintable in CI against the functional inventory, exactly as Architecture A's
   route segments are, and it should be linted — an authored sequence containing a
   string that the parser assigns is a canon defect, not a typo.
3. **It must satisfy the signature, or it must be an argued exception.** Length
   divisible by three; within-record repetition; no functional contact; coda-heavy
   silhouette per §4.6. A writer who breaks one of these must say which and why, and the
   exception becomes an in-world fact that the Archive has noticed and argued about.
   **The signature is what makes the material detectable as a system** (benchmark §4.14:
   the audience must be able to detect that a complete system exists even where they
   cannot see it), and a single sequence that violates it silently costs the premise.
4. **It is composed, not generated.** Random syllables satisfying §2.1 would satisfy the
   letter of every rule and would be worthless, and readers can tell — that is the
   Voynich failure (§7.4). Every Stratum III text is written to be read aloud, revised
   after being read aloud, and cut if it does not survive that.
5. **Three readings, always.** Utterance, Instrument, Palimpsest, on the specific text,
   with the Palimpsest reading given its best form and not a straw one.
6. **No new Stratum III material without a recovery event.** The corpus does not grow on
   demand. A new sequence enters with a date, a locus, an expedition, and a field
   report, or it does not enter.

### 6.4 The forgery problem — the device this class generates for free

**Because Stratum III cannot be parsed, a Stratum III text cannot be authenticated by
grammar.**

For every other stratum, HEL has a check: a segment either obeys the paradigms or it does
not, and a modern forgery gets caught because forgers make slot errors. Stratum III has
no slots. The only authentication available is **provenance** — find context, chain of
custody, conservator's report, attestation history.

Consequences, and they are all usable:

- **The Archive can never be certain that a given Stratum III sequence is not human
  composition.** Not for the ones recovered from objects with clean provenance, and
  emphatically not for the ones that exist only in the human channel.
- **A field member who wrote nine units in a notebook margin cannot be distinguished
  from a field member who copied nine units off a wall**, and several analysts believe at
  least one canonical sequence is exactly that.
- This is not a flaw to be patched. It is the sharpest available form of handoff §6.4's
  requirement that evidentiality be central: **the most affecting material in the corpus
  is the material whose evidential status can never be closed.**
- It also supplies the fourth in-world position the Phase 1 recommendation requires at
  X8: that the conserved formulae are human composition retro-fitted to substrate
  material by analysts who found structure because they were looking for it. Here that
  position is not merely printable — it is unfalsifiable, and everyone knows it.

### 6.5 One institutional consequence, and it is the reason this material spreads

A complete coordinate is a route and therefore an operational secret; clearance in this
design attaches to complete coordinates and to authorisation-graded forms. **Stratum III
has no coordinate, no grade, and no warrant. There is nothing in it to classify.**

So it is the only substrate material HEL publishes without restriction — precisely
because it is operationally worthless. It appears in the open catalogue, in the branch
registers, on the walls of the Reading Room, in field notebooks, and in the mouths of
crews who have never been near an Archive. The most-repeated substrate material in the
League is the material nobody can read and nobody needs to protect.

That is how the ritual gets its purchase without anyone deciding it should, and it is
handoff §6.8 satisfied by an institutional mechanism rather than by assertion.

### 6.6 The sealed register, and the honest limit on it

Brief §4 requires that a published gap correspond to something actually designed and
deliberately withheld, never to something we failed to work out. Stratum III is a large
published gap, so it needs a sealed entry — and the obvious way to write that entry is
also a canon violation, so it must be specified exactly.

**The sealed register entry for a Stratum III text records:**

- the compositional rule the text was built under, in full;
- the formal constraints it satisfies and any exception argued under rule 3;
- the intended affect and the placement decisions that carry it;
- the provenance design and what it is doing;
- a statement of what is deliberately withheld and why.

**It does not record a translation, a gloss, a key, or a semantic intent expressed as
content — and this is not squeamishness.** A stored translation is an Authority D
resolution written down in a place a later editor can find and ship. The withheld thing
here is *not a meaning we know*. It is **a designed formal object whose meaning was never
fixed by anyone, including us** — which is a stronger and more honest position than a
sealed answer key, and the only one compatible with handoff §5.3.

If that reads as a dodge, apply the test: the register is auditable by count, every
Stratum III sequence has an entry, and any reviewer can check that the entry specifies a
real compositional rule and that the shipped sequence obeys it. What we are guaranteeing
is that the material was **composed**, not that it was **encoded**. That is the
guarantee brief §4 actually needs.

## 7. The honesty constraint

**Stratum III material ships untranslated. There is no gloss, there will be no gloss,
and the absence is diegetically caused: HEL cannot supply one.** This is the rule the
whole class depends on, and it is the rule that will be under pressure at every review,
from people who are not wrong to want the thing they are asking for.

### 7.1 The rule, in the form it goes into the style manual

- **No translation.** Not natural, not literal, not "provisional."
- **No morpheme gloss.** There are no morphemes; a gloss line would assert a
  segmentation the Archive cannot certify (§3.1).
- **No field paraphrase.** Note the contrast with Ch-4 formulae, which *are* given a
  field paraphrase printed with a warning. Stratum III gets none, because a paraphrase of
  parsable material is a lossy rendering and a paraphrase of unparsable material is an
  invention.
- **No evocative English title.** Titles are catalogue identifiers or find-circumstance
  names — *the Ladder*, *the 1897 underside*. Never *The Lament of the Nine*.
- **No "approximately," no "something like," no ellipsis standing in for a meaning.**
  A dotted line implies a recoverable original.
- **The confidence grade is not G5.** G5 is *transmitted, not analysed*, which implies
  analysis is pending. Stratum III material carries the parse at **no grade at all**,
  with the class name in the grade field, because the grade system grades readings and
  there is no reading to grade.

**The failure mode, named so a reviewer can catch it:** the moment a Stratum III text
receives an English rendering, it stops being an unreadable object and becomes an English
poem with alien decoration. The asset is spent in one line and cannot be recovered,
because a reader who has seen a translation cannot unsee it. This is a one-way door.

### 7.2 How an unglossed text moves a reader — the four mechanisms

Not asserted. These are the mechanisms, each with a real-world precedent that
demonstrates it working at scale on people who did not understand a word.

**1. Recognition through repetition.** A refrain that returns three times in one text,
and the text that returns at every threshold, teaches the reader its shape. By the third
hearing the reader is not decoding, they are *recognising* — and recognition without
comprehension is a real and specific feeling, not a substitute for one. The unit that
recurs becomes the one the reader says.

**2. Form and prosody.** Quantised length, triadic partition, terminal-syllable
agreement, a heavy coda silhouette, final stress, and a short unit in the penultimate
position where the line catches. All of this is available to a reader who has no idea
what any of it means, and all of it is *in the artifact*, not in the narration wrapped
around it — which is the audit's Part 8 complaint about Architecture A answered on its
own terms.

**3. Provenance.** The benchmark is explicit that this is where the feeling lives: the
elegiac charge of the Linear B tablets does not come from their contents, which are
sheep. It comes from knowing that the scribe recorded a routine delivery, that the
palace burned before the year was out, and that **the fire is the only reason we can read
the sheep at all.** Meaning did not create the feeling; circumstance did. Stratum III is
built to carry circumstance: who said it, when, how often, and what happened to them.

**4. Refusal.** An unglossed block set beside glossed material is a wall, and the page
shows the reader being refused. The gloss column is *there*, running down the page for
every other item, and for this one it is empty. That is a designed typographic event and
it should be laid out deliberately, not apologised for.

### 7.3 The precedents that prove it works

Stated as evidence, not as ornament. Each is a real case of material moving people who
could not read it.

- **Linear A.** Pronounceable through Linear B's sign values, unmistakably organised,
  undeciphered. It has held scholarly and popular attention for a century *in that
  state*. This is the exact position Stratum III occupies, and its existence is the
  answer to anyone who claims an unreadable corpus cannot sustain interest.
- **Linear B.** The provenance mechanism in its purest form (above).
- **The Kaddish.** An Aramaic mourner's prayer recited by mourners the overwhelming
  majority of whom do not read Aramaic. Its emotional function is close to total and is
  almost entirely independent of comprehension. Repetition, occasion, and who says it and
  when — the mechanism of §7.2 items 1 and 3, running for a millennium.
- **The Kyrie in the Latin rite.** A Greek fragment retained inside a Latin liturgy and
  sung for centuries by congregations who had neither language. It survived every
  translation of everything around it, which is itself the finding.
- **Sigur Rós's *Vonlenska*.** Deliberately non-semantic vocal syllables, on an album
  whose tracks are untitled, sold to a mass audience that reports strong and specific
  emotional response. Contemporary, commercial, and decisive: this is not a
  medieval-liturgy special case.
- **Tolkien's own practice.** The Ring inscription is met in the Black Speech and lands
  before it is glossed. Namárië is given entire before any rendering, and a great many
  readers meet it that way and remember it that way. The *Book of Mazarbul* facsimiles
  were made to be looked at, burnt and stabbed, not read.

**And the one negative precedent, which is the failure condition:**

- **The Voynich manuscript.** Fascinating for a century and now, in the public mind,
  mostly a suspicion of hoax — because it is *only* mysterious. Nothing in it can be
  confirmed, no partial reading holds, and structure cannot be demonstrated to a
  non-specialist. **That is what Stratum III becomes if §6.3's authoring rules are not
  enforced**: unreadable material with no detectable system loses respect irreversibly,
  and it loses it slowly, so nobody notices in time. The distributional signature in §4
  is not decoration. It is the entire difference between Linear A and the Voynich
  outcome.

### 7.4 One honest concession

An unglossed text asks more of a reader than a glossed one and some readers will decline.
A published Stratum III sequence will be translated by fans within a week of shipping,
badly and confidently, and those translations will circulate. **That is fine and should
be planned for rather than fought** — it is what happens to Linear A, continuously, and
the existence of amateur decipherments is evidence of engagement, not of failure. What
must never happen is that *we* publish one, or endorse one, or wink at one. The Archive's
position is the project's position: no reading is held, and the four conditions that
would produce one are named (§2.4).

## 8. Worked example — CSC-1146

**The one example in this file. It carries no gloss, and none exists.**

Transliteration is layer 2 (the 1908 Standard, as amended). Layer 3 field pronunciation
is given because this text is said aloud more than any other substrate material in the
League. **There is no layer 4.** The gloss column that runs down every other page of the
catalogue is empty here, and is printed empty rather than omitted.

### 8.1 The sequence

**Transliteration (layer 2), as it stands on the object:**

> walnisen · pehisar · tirakur
> pekalar · munlisen · tirakur
> walnisen · nusar · tirakur

**Field pronunciation (layer 3 — HEL's convention, stress final, no reduction, ever):**

> wal-ni-**SEN** · pe-hi-**SAR** · ti-ra-**KUR**
> pe-ka-**LAR** · mun-li-**SEN** · ti-ra-**KUR**
> wal-ni-**SEN** · nu-**SAR** · ti-ra-**KUR**

Chicago Reading Room gives the `e` of `-sen` as [eɪ]; the 1949 Standard gives [e]. Both
are heard, in the same room, on the same night, and nothing rides on it.

**Every syllable above is on the 1908 sanctioned list** (A §6.1b) and the sequence is
checkable against it unit by unit — which is the whole point of closing the list, and
which matters more here than anywhere else in the corpus, because this is the one text
with no gloss to check it against instead. The terminal `-ur` of the refrain is `kur`
because `kur` is the only `-ur` syllable the committee licensed; the refrain's ending was
not chosen, it was the only one available.

**Gloss:** *(none — Stratum III; see §7.1)*
**Grade:** *(none — the grade system grades readings; there is no reading)*
**Class:** III (Hessell 1976), confirmed on all three conditions; worksheet CSC-1146/c,
signed 1976, re-signed 1998 and 2019.

### 8.2 Distributional analysis

| Property | Value | Signature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tokens | 9 | ÷3 ✓ (§4.1) |
| Types | 6 | — |
| Recurring type | `tirakur` ×3, at positions 3, 6, 9 — group-final, without exception | §4.1 triadic partition |
| Second recurring type | `walnisen` ×2, positions 1 and 7 | §4.2 within-record repetition |
| Within-record recurrence rate | 0.33 of types recur internally | §4.2 (class 0.61) |
| Between-record recurrence | **4 of 6 types occur in no other sequence in the CSC** | §4.2 (class hapax rate 73%) |
| Terminal syllables by group | `-sen · -sar · -kur` ‖ `-lar · -sen · -kur` ‖ `-sen · -sar · -kur` | groups 1 and 3 identical; group 2 a transposition of the first two |
| Tokens ending in a coda | 9 of 9 | §4.6 (class 84%; functional 11%) |
| Mean syllables per unit | 2.9 (26 syllables ÷ 9) | §4.6 (class 3.1; functional 2.0) |
| Shared first syllable | `pehisar` / `pekalar` both `pe-` | §4.6 — violates the 1908 distinctness rule, which no functional unit does |
| Segmentation ambiguity | `pekalar` alone admits two legal readings — `pe-ka-lar` and `pe-kal-ar` | the 1971 proposal's only real foothold — see below |
| Assignable units | **0** | Condition 1 ✓ |
| Contact with functional material | **0** | Condition 2(b) ✓ |
| Field condition | intact, certified 1952, re-certified 1998 | Condition 2(a) ✓ |

**The 1971 extraction attempt, and why it failed — this is the classifier working.**
Three of the six types contain the string `ar`, which is also the incidence marker `-ar`
JCT. A 1971 resegmentation proposal extracted it from `pehisar`, `pekalar` and `nusar`
and claimed the sequence for Stratum I. **The proposal fails Condition 1 as written**,
because assignment is *positional*, not string-matching: an incidence marker is by
definition what stands between a root and an edge tail, and there is no edge tail
anywhere in the sequence for it to stand before. Extracting `ar` produces a marker with
no address.

**And the proposal is better than it is usually given credit for, which is why it is
kept.** In two of the three types the extraction is illegal on the face of it: `pehisar`
and `nusar` segment only as `pe-hi-sar` and `nu-sar`, and getting `ar` out of either
requires splitting the sanctioned syllable `sar`, which the code book does not permit.
But `pekalar` genuinely admits two legal segmentations — `pe-ka-lar` and `pe-kal-ar` —
and the second yields a licensed onsetless `ar` without breaking a single syllable. On
that one unit the 1971 author was not misreading the code; he was reading it correctly,
and the code was ambiguous. **He still fails, and he fails on position rather than on
spelling** — `pe-kal-ar` puts a legal incidence marker at the end of a unit with nothing
after it to be incident *to*. That the strongest available attack on this sequence is
defeated by where the marker sits and not by what it is spelled like is the cleanest
statement in the file of what Condition 1 actually tests.

The attempt is retained in the catalogue as a worked demonstration of the difference
between the two tests, and its author's rejoinder — that a paradigm which can only find
itself where it already is has not been tested — is printed with it.

### 8.3 Provenance

This is where the weight is. Every line of it is Authority C and production-only, and
none of it says anything about the builders.

**The object.** CSC-1146. A portable object, 190 mm across, recovered 1897 at locus
L-31, field name **the Long Wait** — named that year for the interval between the
expedition's arrival and its recovery of an outbound route, and unrenameable since,
because the routes are catalogued under it. The nine units are incised on the underside.
**No face of the object bears a trace line.** The Archive's description of the object
itself runs to two sentences and gives its dimensions and mass; it declines to describe
its form or say what it was for, and this refusal is policy, not ignorance of the
object.

**The filing.** The 1897 accession clerk entered it as *"ornament, non-linguistic."* It
sat on an open shelf in the same building for fifty-five years and was not catalogued as
corpus until 1952. The Palimpsest school has never let this go, and is entitled not to:
their reading of the object was the first reading of the object, made by someone with no
theory at all (§5.3).

**The recitation.** In 1931 a field member, **Ada Sowerby**, returned from locus L-9. The
expedition had departed with four. At debrief, thirty-one hours after emergence, she
recited nine units. Asked where she had learned them, the stenographic transcript records
her answer:

> *"I did not learn it. I came back with it."*

The Archive's standing annotation on that page is that debriefs taken inside forty-eight
hours of emergence are not evidentiary, and that Sowerby had been on station eleven days.
**She never repeated the claim.** In 1934, recorded on cylinder, and again in 1939,
recorded on disc, she said instead that she had it from a member of the 1931 party who
did not return. That contradicts her own transcript, cannot be checked, and is the last
thing she said about it on record. She died in 1947.

**The convergence.** In 1952, in the post-Cardwell review of unlisted accessions, an
assistant matched the underside of CSC-1146 to the Sowerby recitation: **nine units, six
types, same order.** An unparsable sequence held in one person's mouth for twenty-one
years and an unparsable sequence incised on an object recovered thirty-four years before
she was on the expedition, from a different locus, agreeing unit for unit.

**And the deflation, which the Archive prints in the same paragraph.** Sowerby's Archive
mentor had access to the 1897 accession register. The register entry describes the object
as ornament and does not transcribe the marks — but the object was on an open shelf, in
that building, for the whole of her training. **HEL cannot exclude indirect transmission
and has never claimed to.** This is the forgery problem (§6.4) at the one place in the
corpus where it costs the most, and it cannot be closed.

**The variation.** The three recitations differ in exactly one unit — position 8,
`nusar`, the shortest in the sequence and the only one of two syllables. 1931 and the
object agree. The 1934 cylinder is damaged across precisely that unit. The 1939 disc has
a form the Archive transcribes as `nulisar`, one syllable longer — and `nu-li-sar` is
itself well formed under the 1908 list, so the variant cannot be dismissed as a
mishearing that produced a non-word. **That the sole variant is the shortest and least
stressed unit is what the attrition model predicts of oral transmission**, which one
school reads as confirmation that the sequence was transmitted rather than composed, and
another reads as confirmation of nothing except that people misremember short words.

**The use.** Crews say it before entering a locus for which the map shows no recovered
outbound edge. It is not part of the departure formula. It has never been authorised, it
carries no warrant, and it is not in any manual. The Archive issued instructions against
the practice in 1958 and again in 1981, on the ground that unwarranted material has no
place in field procedure. It withdrew neither instruction and enforced neither.

**The roll.** Locus L-9 still shows no recovered outbound edge. Two of the four came
back. The roll records the other two, and this file does not.

### 8.4 The three readings of this text

**Utterance.** A composed text of nine units in three groups, with a refrain in
group-final position and an envelope structure — the first group's opening returns at
the head of the third. The terminal-syllable pattern is a scheme. `nusar` is short
because the penultimate position is where a line catches before its last return. It was
carried out of L-9 in somebody's mouth because that is what people do with texts that
matter to them, and the fact that the mouth and the object agree across thirty-four years
and two loci is the strongest evidence in the corpus that something was **transmitted**.

**Instrument.** Nine slots in three frames of three. `tirakur` is not a refrain, it is a
frame delimiter, occurring at frame boundaries because that is where delimiters occur.
The terminal-syllable regularity is a parity or alignment property of the encoding. The
envelope is a repeated field, not a poetic return. The object and the recitation agree
because they are two instances of the same fixed-format record — which is what a fixed
format is *for* — and the agreement is evidence of a **standard**, not of a text. Under
this reading Sowerby memorised a serial number.

**Palimpsest.** The clerk was right in 1897. The marks are a physical regularity of the
object's manufacture or wear; the "units" are the 1908 Standard's assignment procedure
imposing syllables on recurring shapes, which guarantees recurrence; the triadic
structure is a property of whatever made the marks, three passes of something being the
least surprising fact in the file. And the recitation is a human composition — Sowerby's
own, or her mentor's, or a branch tradition's — that entered the corpus through the one
door the Archive cannot close, because **Stratum III cannot be authenticated by
grammar.** The 1952 convergence is then not a convergence: it is a woman who spent her
training in a building with the object on a shelf.

**The Archive holds no position.** Field Protocol has no procedure for this object,
because there is nothing operational in it to have a procedure about. Crews say it
anyway.

### 8.5 What this example is claimed to demonstrate, and what it is not

**Claimed:** that a text with no translation can be memorised, said aloud, recognised on
return, and made to carry weight — and that the weight comes from form, repetition,
placement and provenance, in that order of increasing importance.

**Not claimed:** that the sequence means anything; that anyone composed it; that anyone
spoke it before Sowerby; that it is old; that it is not old; that it came from the
network; that it came from a person; that HEL's units are the material's units; that the
1952 convergence is independent. Every one of those is either Authority D or unresolvable
from the record, and the record says so.

The test the audit set was whether this survives being read aloud without a translation
to lean on. That test is not passed on the page. **Say the nine units out loud, three
times, and then read §8.3 again.** If it does not work, it does not ship, and no gloss
will be added to make it work — adding one is the failure, not the fix (§7.1).

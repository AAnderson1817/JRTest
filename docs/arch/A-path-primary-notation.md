## 1. The one-sentence architecture

**The clause is not a sentence about a thing. The clause is a directed edge
between two node-states, spelled across the gap between them, and closed by a
warrant that states how the record knows.**

Everything else — the coordinate system, the four negations, the command grades,
the corpus strata — falls out of that.

## 2. Speaker/user model

### 2.1 The non-resolution, stated as a design commitment

**The Black Box builders remain Authority D and this architecture does not touch
them.** No species, no homeworld, no biology, no era, no motive, no name. More
than that: the architecture is built so that it *cannot* be used to derive them.

The corpus is not described as "spoken," "written by," or "left by" anyone. HEL's
own term of art — adopted in the 1949 Archive revision specifically to stop
analysts from using the word *speaker* — is **emitted material of unknown
agency** (Authority C). The corpus is a thing HEL found behaving in a patterned
way. Whether the pattern has an author is the open question, not the premise.

### 2.2 The three schools (Authority C)

HEL's Archive maintains three standing interpretations, none dominant. They are
not a lore ornament; they are the architecture's compliance mechanism.

| School | Claim | Strongest evidence for it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Utterance** | The material is language, produced by users | Bound pairs whose attested use is not composed from their parts (§10.1); scope ambiguity; meter in Ch-4; "shadow segments" recording traversals that did not occur. **Three of the school's older arguments have weakened and are shown weakened rather than quietly dropped:** conditioned erosion is contested at §7.1, where the conditioning environment may be an artifact of HEL's own 1908 segmentation; obligatory redundancy is withdrawn at §10.1 as failing to discriminate; and shadow segments are deflated at §5.5, where the likeliest account of the parse is misread damage — on which account there are no shadow segments at all |
| **Instrument** | The material is control or status syntax emitted by the system | Slot rigidity; the warrant paradigm reads as a provenance field; absence of anything a formalism would not need; perfect recoverability of orientation |
| **Palimpsest** | Two or more unrelated systems were wrongly consolidated in the 19th century and have been analyzed together ever since | Stratum IV shares only ~30 units with I–III and obeys sequencing constraints the others violate; and the Redfern reading of ADJ (§4.6c), under which the 1908 committee consolidated a nineteenth-century clerk's damage mark into the paradigm — the same error the school alleges at corpus scale, demonstrated inside a single channel |

**Design rule — the Three-School Test.** *Every construction admitted to the
design bible must be readable under all three schools.* If a proposed feature
only makes sense if there was a speaker, it is rejected. If a proposed feature
only makes sense if there was a machine, it is rejected. This is the rule that
prevents the failure that scrapped the previous attempt: it makes inventing the
builders structurally impossible rather than merely forbidden.

This is also a productive constraint, not a defensive one. Designing something
that is simultaneously a credible natural language *and* credible machine syntax
is strictly harder than designing either, and the features that satisfy both —
obligatory ignorance-marking, the bipartite edge, the warrant slot — are the most
distinctive things in this architecture. The canon restriction generated the good
ideas.

### 2.3 The evidence corpus

All figures below are **Authority C, proposed for approval**. The scale figures
are production-only. **The channel structure is not.** It asserts that the
network leaves recordable traces in three distinct physical registers, which is a
statement about the universe, and handoff §9.2 leaves the substrate's physical
register — written, acoustic, electromagnetic, spatial, mathematical, or
multimodal — explicitly open at **Authority D**. The four-channel structure below
is therefore the **recommended option among the alternatives set out in §12.1**,
not a decision this document is entitled to make. The figures exist so that
"fragmentary" is a *specified* quantity rather than an excuse.

**Designation:** HEL Consolidated Substrate Corpus (CSC), maintained by the
Archive; consolidated from earlier branch registers in 1908, re-catalogued 1949.

**Scale:**
- ~4,100 catalogued tokens
- ~2,900 occur in sequences of three or more units
- ~610 distinct recurring units; ~180 occur more than five times
- productive analyzable core: ~90 units
- longest continuous sequence: 41 units; only six sequences exceed 30
- **the corpus does not grow on demand.** New material enters only by recovery on
  expedition. Lexicon expansion is a diegetic event with a date and a field report.

This is deliberately Linear B / Etruscan scale: enough to recover a grammar,
never enough to recover a lexicon. It is the numerical form of handoff §7.1's
"resistant to complete translation."

Handoff §9.2 asks how large the recovered corpus is and leaves it **Authority D**.
The figures above are this document's recommendation and the user sets the number.
The tradeoff, stated so the choice can be made rather than inherited: raising the
count makes both grammar and lexicon more recoverable in-world and pushes toward
prohibited shortcut #2; dropping it much below ~2,000 tokens makes the paradigms
in §4 unrecoverable by any honest method, and the architecture loses the right to
the confidence grades it assigns itself.

**Channels:**

- **Ch-1 Incised** (~62%). Marks on recovered hard objects associated with node
  loci. Best preserved; heavily formulaic; dominated by what read as labels,
  status notices, and warnings.
- **Ch-2 Standing** (~19%). Geometric patterns observed at certain loci during
  certain traversal states. Transient. Photographed since the 1890s; before that,
  copied by hand into branch notebooks. **The pre-photographic records are copies
  of copies**, which gives HEL a textual-criticism problem in the classical sense:
  some units exist only in a copyist's hand and may be artifacts of transmission.
- **Ch-3 Modulation** (~15%). Instrument logs — periodic structure recorded during
  traversal. Sequence is recoverable; shape is not. Whether Ch-3 belongs to the
  same system as Ch-1 is the central Palimpsest question. **Nothing in this
  architecture attributes Ch-3 to precursor entities; entity communicative
  capacity is Authority D and is out of scope (prohibited shortcut #13).**
- **Ch-4 Human transmission** (~4%). HEL's own spoken formulae: phonologically
  conserved, semantically opaque, recited in fixed order. Evidentially the worst
  material in the corpus — centuries of humans repeating something — and the only
  material with anything resembling prosody.

**Condition:** fragmentary, damaged, mixed provenance, and **without a bilingual.**
No Rosetta object has been recovered. The architecture supplies none, and no shipped
material may assume one — but the modal claim is struck deliberately and the difference
is load-bearing: this is a fact about what HEL holds, never a claim that no such object
exists. Nothing downstream may reason from the stronger form. Decipherment
proceeds by **operational correlation**: sequence X is present at loci where
traversal subsequently succeeded; sequence Y where it did not. HEL therefore holds
readings it has never verified semantically and has acted on for a century.

**Deliberately not resolved:** whether any CSC material was recovered from the
Chicago anchor locus. The find-site register is withheld at this stage precisely
so that this architecture cannot be read as equating the burial-mound connection
with alien authorship (prohibited shortcut #15). Handoff §9.2 also asks whether
there are inscriptions on the Black Box casing; **this architecture asserts
neither yes nor no** and takes none of its corpus from the casing.

## 3. Why this and not subject–verb–object

Six arguments. The first four are canon-compliance arguments; the fifth is the
aesthetic case; the sixth is against my own proposal.

**1. SVO forces an agent into every sentence.** English cannot render a clause
without a subject. Every SVO translation of this corpus therefore smuggles in a
decision about agency — the exact Authority D question the project must not
resolve. A grammar with no obligatory subject position lets HEL analyze 4,100
tokens without ever committing. This is not a stylistic preference; it is the
difference between a grammar that respects Authority D and one that violates it
in every clause.

**2. SVO makes motion a verb, and motion verbs are symmetric by default.** "Go"
is orientation-neutral in every human language; direction is carried by an
adjunct (*to*, *from*), and adjuncts are droppable. A grammar in which direction
is droppable will constantly produce well-formed sentences that erase the network
topology — prohibited shortcut #5, produced automatically, forever. Path-Primary
makes orientation constitutive: **you cannot form a well-formed segment without
committing to an edge orientation**, because the edge is spelled in two halves
across two node words and a half-edge is visibly broken.

**3. SVO separates where from when.** Locative and temporal adjuncts are distinct
constituents in every SVO language on Earth. Canon A says that separation is the
dangerous one. Making the coordinate a single bound constituent makes the
dangerous separation *ungrammatical*, and — see §5.2 — puts the grammar in exact
structural congruence with the Authority A fact that a date does not determine a
place, rather than merely tolerating it. **Congruence, not derivation.** §5.2 is
careful about that distinction and so is this line: a grammar can mirror a fact
about the network; it cannot establish one.

**4. SVO has no structural position for evidence.** Evidentiality in SVO
languages is adverbial and optional ("apparently," "reportedly"). Handoff §6.4
says evidentiality must be central rather than decorative. Centrality means a
required slot: **a segment without a warrant is ill-formed, and "no warrant" is
itself an overt marker.**

**5. Orientation-invariant reading.** Because orientation lives in the
*attachment* of the edge halves and not in linear order, a chain read from either
end yields the same directed graph.

> **Say it exactly, and this document says it exactly everywhere the property is
> invoked: the *record* is reversible; the *network* is not.** What survives
> read-order inversion is the reading of the trace. The edge it describes stays
> pointed the way it was pointed. Nothing here permits traversal against an edge,
> and the property is named *orientation-invariant reading* rather than "the
> reversibility property" — the earlier name in this document's own drafts —
> because the old name invited exactly the inference prohibited shortcut #4
> forbids.

This is the most alien thing in the design and it costs no apostrophes, no
clusters, and no invented species. It also generates the best in-world scholarly
disaster available (§8.2, Example 2). SVO cannot do this at all: reverse an SVO
sentence and you get nonsense, or worse, the opposite claim.

The property has a price and an anchor. The price: the *reversal edit* — swapping
which node word carries the tail half — is mechanically cheap, one CV move, and
that cheapness is the unacknowledged cost of read-order invariance. What makes the
edit fail rather than merely lie is the **PHASE anchor, defined at §5.2a**: a
swapped chain does not assert something untrue, it fails to compose a coordinate
at all.

**6. Against myself.** SVO would be dramatically better for performable dialogue,
for characterization, for emotional range, and for players learning the language
casually. If those requirements dominate the product, this architecture is the
wrong choice. See §11.

## 4. Core morphology

Notation: `=` is a clitic boundary; `-` is HEL's *editorial* hyphen, an artifact
of the 1908 transliteration standard, not present in any source.

**Every paradigm below carries a confidence grade**, on the G0–G5 scale defined in
§5.4, assigned per value rather than per table. This is the correction of a real
defect in an earlier draft of this document, which graded its worked examples
carefully and then presented its grammar as though the grammar were not itself a
reconstruction. It is one. A corpus of ~4,100 tokens with no bilingual (§2.3) does
not yield an uncontested functional morphology, and a document that shows one is
claiming a decipherment the canon does not permit — handoff §5.3 requires that
*every foundational claim* carry a confidence level and a competing reading, and
prohibited shortcuts #2 and #18 are both approached by an ungraded paradigm even
when every individual example is scrupulous.

Reading the confidence column:

- **G1 Structural.** The contrast is formally secure; the English gloss is HEL's
  and is unconfirmed. Nothing has validated it operationally, usually because the
  category is not one an expedition can act on and therefore not one an expedition
  can test.
- **G2 Operationally validated.** The contrast is formally secure and the gloss has
  a century of operational correlation behind it — routes taken on this reading
  behaved as the reading predicted. The semantics remain unverified. **This is the
  characteristic HEL grade and it is not a synonym for "known."**
- **G3 Contested.** Two or more standing readings, each with partisans and a
  publication history. §4.6 sets out the three that are contested at the level of
  the entire category rather than at the margin.
- **G5 Preserved.** Transmitted, not analyzed.

Of the thirty-five graded formatives and slot values in §4 and §5.2: **ten are G1,
fifteen G2, nine G3, one G5. None is unqualified**, and no cell in any confidence
column below is left blank.

### 4.1 Roots are node-states, not nouns

A root names **a condition a coordinate can be in** — *held closed*, *counted*,
*bearing*, *in standing*, *unattended*, *under attendance*. It does not name a
place, an object, or a person.

Consequence, and it is a large one: **the substrate has no attested way to name a
node.** A node is designated by the state it was in when the record was made.
The same node bears different roots in different records. **HEL cannot build a
gazetteer from the corpus**, which is why the operational layer runs on catalog
identifiers and English field names (handoff §7.5 — "avoid assuming every place
or artifact has a true proper name waiting to be translated" — is here a
structural property, not a caution).

**And the converse, which the Archive states in its front matter and analysts
forget by lunchtime: the same root in two records is not evidence of the same
node.** If roots name states, then two records bearing CSC-0012 attest two
occasions on which *something was in the CSC-0012 state*, and nothing in either
record identifies them with each other. Cross-record LOCUS identity is an
inference HEL makes constantly and cannot license from the corpus; where it holds,
it holds on operational grounds — an arrival log, a find-site, a route that
behaved — and those are HEL documents. **The graph HEL draws therefore has more
identified nodes than the corpus asserts**, and the junction map (§4.6b) is not the
only place that is true.

The standing dispute (Authority C, permanent): the **Locative school** holds that
high-frequency roots are in fact node designators that happen to look stative;
the **Stative school** holds they are pure conditions. Eighty years, unresolved,
and the corpus is too small to settle it.

**Consequence for grading, and it is not a small one: the Locative–Stative dispute
places a G3 ceiling on the gloss of every root frequent enough to be worth arguing
about.** No root anywhere in this document is graded better than G3, and none of
the seven roots appearing in the worked examples of §8 is an exception. That is why
roots are written on the gloss line in catalog form with a queried gloss —
`CSC.0012(sealed?)` — and not as confident English words with the dispute demoted
to a footnote. The convention is stated at §6.3 and is used without exception below.

### 4.2 The bipartite edge

An edge is one morpheme realized in two halves:

- the **tail half**, a suffix on the origin node word
- the **head half**, a prefix on the destination node word

The halves must agree in edge class. Orientation is carried by *which half lands
where*, not by order. Hence §3.5.

| Class | Gloss | Tail (origin) | Head (destination) | Confidence | Standing challenge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **PASS** | sanctioned direct traversal | `-ru` | `ru-` | **G2** | Most frequent class; a century of route outcomes consistent with the reading. Nothing verifies that "sanctioned" is in the record rather than in HEL |
| **THRU** | leg of a forced multi-node path | `-ken` | `ke-` | **G2** | Validated by the broken-half method (below); the gloss "forced" is inferred from the absence of a co-attested direct edge, not stated |
| **SHUNT** | arrival displaced from intent | `-tul` | `tu-` | **G3** | May be one class with RECUR, split by the 1908 consolidation — **§4.6a** |
| **BAR** | edge present in the graph, not traversable in this orientation | `-pel` | `pe-` | **G2** | Secure against IMP since the 1948 revision; crews have relied on the distinction since |
| **RECUR** | return to a similar but non-identical coordinate | `-halu` | `ha-` | **G3** | May be one class with SHUNT — **§4.6a** |
| **BIND** | co-incidence — same coordinate under two descriptions; **not motion** | `-sil` | `si-` | **G1** | Cannot be operationally validated even in principle: no expedition outcome distinguishes a true BIND claim from a false one. The gloss is the Archive's and has never been tested |
| **ADJ** | adjacency attested, orientation unrecovered | `-yun` | `yu-` *(reconstructed; no head half is attested)* | **G3** | May not be an edge class at all, but a nineteenth-century damage convention consolidated into the paradigm — **§4.6c** |

Head forms are the tail's first CV. The relation is transparent and teachable;
the *asymmetry is positional only*, which is the entire point. Note that the rule
is what supplies ADJ's head form: `yu-` is in the table because the rule derives it,
not because anything has ever been recovered bearing it (§4.6c).

**The inventory as an inventory is G2.** That there are seven classes — not six,
not nine — rests on the same operational correlation as the individual glosses and
on the 1908 committee's judgement about what counted as one mark. Under §4.6c it is
six. There is no evidence that would make it uncontestably seven, and this document
does not claim any.

**Broken edges are diagnostic.** A fragment ending `…-ken` with nothing after it
tells HEL that a THRU-headed node is missing and constrains what it can have been.
Catalogued `-ken ⌀`. Roughly 41% of CSC sequences terminate in a broken half.
**This is how the corpus reconstructs graph structure it does not possess** — and
it is a philological method that only works because of the bipartite design.

**The method has one known failure mode, and it took fifty-five years to find it.**
A tail half that has stopped projecting an edge — a lexicalized pair, §10.1 — is
formally indistinguishable from a tail half whose head was lost to damage. Both
present as `-TAIL ⌀`. Keele's 1963 review reassigned 24 catalogued `-ru ⌀` breaks
on CSC-0012 from damage to lexicalization, and those 24 had been feeding the graph
as inferred missing nodes since 1908. **The remaining break inventory has never
been re-audited for the same error.** The 41% figure above is therefore an upper
bound on damage and a lower bound on nothing.

### 4.3 Node word template

```
[EDGEHEAD-] ROOT [-INCIDENCE] [-EDGETAIL] [-GRADE] [-POLARITY] [=COORDINATE]
```

**One distributional constraint on the last slot, stated here and argued at
§5.2a.** The `=COORDINATE` enclitic is attested only on node words that carry an
edge **head** half. It is never attested on a bare tail-bearer. That is a fact
about the distribution — 100% of coordinate-bearing words in the CSC are
head-bearing — and §5.2a sets out the reading the Archive puts on it: the
coordinate is *carried* by the destination and *measured from* the origin. **The
distribution is G2; the reading of it is the anchor question**, and that is where
the argument belongs rather than here.

**Incidence** — how the path meets this node:

| Form | Gloss | Meaning | Confidence | Standing challenge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `-in` | **TERM** | endpoint of the chain | **G2** | Correlates reliably with where crews entered and left; "endpoint of the chain" versus "endpoint of the record" is not distinguishable and the Archive does not pretend it is |
| `-is` | **MED** | traversed through | **G2** | Validated jointly with THRU; the two are almost never independently testable, which weakens both slightly |
| `-ar` | **JCT** | other edges attach here, not stated | **G3** | May mark that *the record is incomplete here* rather than anything about graph structure — **§4.6b**. This is the single most consequential G3 in the document |
| `-ol` | **OFF** | referenced, not on this path | **G1** | Formally clean — an OFF node takes no edge into the chain — but the gloss "referenced" imports a referring act, and nothing operational turns on OFF, so nothing has ever tested it |

TERM does not distinguish origin from destination. It does not need to: a node
bearing a tail half is an origin, a node bearing a head half is a destination.
The Instrument school reads this economy as engineering; the Utterance school
reads it as ordinary portmanteau. Both are correct about the data.

**Grade** — the command paradigm (handoff §7.3), on the edge, because there is no
verb to carry it:

| Form | Gloss | Meaning | Confidence | Standing challenge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `-ti` | **SIM** | modeled; no commitment | **G1** | Operational validation is structurally unavailable: a modeled traversal produces no outcome to correlate against. SIM is graded on paradigm position alone |
| `-ka` | **PREP** | staged; conditions being brought into place | **G1** | Same problem. "Conditions being brought into place" is the Archive's paraphrase of a contrast with SIM and EXEC, not of anything the corpus states |
| `-lan` | **AUTH** | authorized | **G3** | Two standing readings — *I hereby authorize* (performative) versus *this edge is in an authorized state* (stative). The difference cannot be ruled out either way and the Archive has written protocol around the possibility that the first is correct (below) |
| `-kas` | **EXEC** | executed or executing | **G2** | The best-correlated value in the paradigm: EXEC-graded segments track traversals HEL independently records as having occurred. Whether it distinguishes completed from in-progress is not recoverable, which is why the gloss carries both |

**HEL cannot determine whether AUTH is performative.** It may mean *I hereby
authorize* or *this edge is in an authorized state*. Because the difference cannot
be ruled out, Archive Field Protocol treats every AUTH-graded recitation as
potentially operative and restricts speaking AUTH forms aloud within defined
distance of an active locus. That is the point where this architecture's science
and its ritual become the same institution behaving reasonably (handoff §6.8,
§3.6).

**And it is cosmology, so it is now in the ledger and not only in this paragraph
(§12 row 17).** A protocol written against the possibility that a spoken form is
operative puts **human utterance as a possible input to the network** on the table,
and handoff §9.2 leaves open whether the Box can receive commands at all. The
protocol asserts nothing — it is a precaution taken under ignorance, which is
handoff §6.5's "precaution taken because of possible loop effects" behaving exactly
as the canon describes — but a precaution that would be pointless if the answer
were known is a live cosmological question wearing a safety rule, and it belongs
in the table where the user can see it. **Nothing in this document claims the
network hears anything, and nothing here may be cited as establishing that it
does.** The deflation is available and is not preferred over the alternative: the
restriction may be an ordinary institutional taboo that acquired a rationale
afterwards, which is what §5.8 says about the rest of HEL's ritual practice and
what handoff §6.8 says HEL's procedures characteristically look like.

### 4.4 Four-way negation

Polarity is edge-scoped and distinct from class and grade.

| Form | Gloss | Meaning | Confidence | What HEL may **not** infer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `-mun` | **IMP** | The edge does not exist in the graph | **G2** | — |
| `-sen` | **FRB** | The edge exists and is traversable, and is closed | **G3** | **by whom or by what.** Reading `sen` as "forbidden *by someone*" is an English translation convention. The Instrument school reads it as *gated*. Two standing readings, therefore G3 — and the grade is load-bearing, because resolving it would name an authority, which would begin naming the builders. It stays open, and the grade is the mechanism that keeps it open. |
| `-nir` | **UNA** | The edge exists, is not closed, is not available at this coordinate | **G2** | whether dormancy is periodic, conditional, or permanent |
| `-lus` | **UNV** | This record does not establish the edge's status either way | **G1** | — The value exists as a clean contrast, but it asserts an absence, and an asserted absence is the one thing operational correlation cannot confirm. It is also the value a generation of analysts got wrong (below). |

Note the separation IMP vs. BAR: BAR is an *edge class* (an edge that exists but
is non-traversable in the stated orientation); IMP is *polarity* (no edge). They
sit at different levels and mean different things. The 1948 revision found that a
generation of analysts had conflated `-lus` (UNV polarity) with `wal` (UNW
warrant, §4.5) — the status of an edge versus the sourcing of an assertion. The
correction stands; several G0 readings date from before it.

**Because negation is edge-scoped and edges are bipartite, half an edge can be
negated.** `-mun` on the tail half alone is attested 23 times. Whether it means
*departure is impossible* or *no such departure exists* is undecided and is left
undecided.

**The scope clitic.** Polarity's scope — whether a marker negates the segment or
only the edge pair — is carried by a separate clitic, present throughout Stratum I
and absent from Stratum II entirely (§7). Its absence is the direct cause of the
irresolvable ambiguity in Example 2.

| Form | Gloss | Meaning | Confidence | Standing challenge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `-he` | **SCOPE** | polarity attaches to the edge pair, not to the segment | **G1** | Attested, and distributionally clean within Stratum I. But its function is inferred from the ambiguity its *absence* produces elsewhere — an argument from a gap. The Instrument school observes that a clitic attested only where nothing needs disambiguating is thin evidence for the job assigned to it, and that observation has never been answered |

### 4.5 Warrants

Warrants are free units at segment end. **A segment with no warrant is ill-formed
and is catalogued as damaged.**

| Form | Gloss | Meaning | Confidence | Standing challenge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `nes` | **REG** | entered without a human attestor | **G2** | The 1949 revision restyled this gloss. It formerly read *registered by the system itself*, which the revision struck on the ground that it presupposes a registrar — a claim about what made the record, which is exactly what nothing in the corpus establishes. The old wording survives in field manuals and analysts still say it out loud |
| `tir` | **WIT** | attested at the locus | **G2** | The most human-shaped warrant in the paradigm, which is precisely why the Utterance school likes it and why the Instrument school reads it as a sensor-provenance field |
| `lekur` | **REP** | held on multiple passes | **G2** | Correlates strongly with edges HEL has itself re-observed. The `lekur`-alone rule (below) exists because the gloss is otherwise an invitation to read causation into it |
| `hos` | **INF** | derived structurally from other edges | **G1** | Unfalsifiable by operational means: a route inferred and then successfully traversed confirms the route, not the claim that the record inferred it |
| `wal` | **UNW** | no source carried | **G1** | An overt marker of absent provenance is the paradigm's strangest member, and the reading rests entirely on its contrast with the other four. Conflated with `-lus` UNV until 1948 |
| `[41]` | — | **a sixth unit occupying the warrant slot, occurring in all Ch-4 formulae and on three Ch-1 objects. No sound value has been assigned. No gloss has been proposed that survived review. Field personnel say "forty-one."** | **G5** | Transmitted, not analyzed. It has never been eliminated as a copyist's artifact and it must not be explained (§8, Example 3) |

Warrants stack, and **order is scope**: `lekur hos` = "recurrent, and inferred";
`hos lekur` = "inferred to be recurrent." A formalism would not need the contrast.
This is one of the Utterance school's three best arguments — and it should be
weighed at **G1**, not higher: 34 stacked warrants are attested in the whole
corpus, 11 of them in the reversed order, and a contrast resting on 11 tokens is
suggestive rather than demonstrated. The Instrument school's reply is that a
provenance field with two entries has to write them in some order, and that HEL has
read a semantics into a sequencing accident. Nobody can currently rule that out.

**Archive house rule, and it is the ethical spine of the whole design:** a segment
warranted `lekur` alone may not be translated into English using the word
*because*. Recurrence is not causation, and HEL's style manual enforces the
distinction typographically. This is handoff §6.5's "stable correlation that HEL
refuses to call causal," realized as an editorial rule that field analysts
actually break under deadline.

### 4.6 Three categories contested at paradigm level (Authority C)

Three categories above are not disputed at the edges. Each has a standing reading,
held by named analysts with a publication record, under which **the category is not
what the Standard says it is** — and in each case the reading changes what HEL
does, not merely how it glosses. They are set out at length deliberately: a G3 in a
table with no argument behind it is a hedge, and the Archive does not keep hedges.
It keeps fights, with dates and names, and it loses them slowly.

#### (a) SHUNT and RECUR may be one class — the Onslow merger (1971)

**The Standard's position.** `-tul/tu-` SHUNT marks arrival displaced from intent.
`-halu/ha-` RECUR marks return to a similar but non-identical coordinate. Two
classes, two glosses, two operational meanings.

**Onslow (1971).** The 1908 consolidation worked from the branch registers, not
from the objects. The registers cut the incised trace at different points — the
Reading Room tradition cut before the arrival flick, the older branch hands after
it — so one mark entered the consolidated catalogue twice, in two transcriptions,
and was catalogued as two classes. On this account the phonological distance
between `-tul` and `-halu` is not evidence of two morphemes. It is a measure of how
badly two nineteenth-century copying conventions disagreed.

**Why it will not go away.** The two classes are in complementary distribution.
Vance (2004) re-collated every surviving Ch-1 object against the registers and
found no object in Strata I–III bearing both. Two apparent contrasts exist; both
are in Stratum IV — the material whose membership in the system is the Palimpsest
question (§2.2). **The two contrasts establish two classes only if Palimpsest is
false, and Palimpsest cannot be settled.** So the merger question is undecidable,
and it is undecidable because another undecidable question sits underneath it. That
is the characteristic shape of this corpus and it is not a defect in the argument.

**The defence.** Aubrey (2007): SHUNT occurs overwhelmingly on `-in` TERM nodes and
RECUR on `-is` MED nodes, an odd accident for one class. The merger side replies
that the skew follows from record type — displacement is recorded where a record
ends, recurrence where one continues — and not from class membership. Neither side
has moved since 2007.

**Operational consequence, which is why this is not an academic matter.** The
Archive's expedition-risk table grades a SHUNT edge as a hazard and a RECUR edge as
ordinary loop behaviour. About 380 catalogued edges carry one flag or the other,
and crews are briefed differently depending on which. If Onslow is right, both
glosses are wrong; the defensible reading is the much weaker *arrival at a
non-identical coordinate, intent not recorded*, which is not actionable; and the
hazard grading built on the distinction is arbitrary. The Archive has never re-run
the risk table under the merged reading. It has been formally asked to twice.

#### (b) JCT may mean "the record is incomplete here" — the Hallam reading (1976)

**The Standard's position.** `-ar` JCT: other edges attach at this node and are not
stated. A fact about the graph.

**Hallam (1976).** `-ar` says nothing about other edges. It marks that *this record
does not continue* — an incompleteness notice, the corpus's own way of saying that
the account of this node stops here. On Hallam's reading the marker is about the
record, not about the network.

**The evidence she assembled.** `-ar` is over-represented at segment margins and on
objects with edge damage. It co-occurs with broken halves far above chance, in a
corpus where 41% of sequences already terminate in a broken half (§4.2). And in the
pre-photographic Ch-2 hand copies it appears with striking regularity at the point
where the copyist's page ends. That last correlation has never been explained away
by anyone, including Hallam's opponents, who concede it and call it small.

**The Archive's reply (1979, still the standing position).** `-ar` is attested on
more than a hundred intact Ch-1 objects with no damage, no broken halves, and no
margin anywhere near. Hallam's counter, unanswered: those are the formulaic label
objects, which is exactly where a convention meaning *the rest is not given here*
would be most expected and least remarkable.

**Operational consequence.** HEL's junction map is built from `-ar`. Of the 214
nodes on it, roughly 60% are junctions for no reason other than that some segment
marks them `-ar`. Under Hallam not one of those is known to be a junction; they are
nodes whose records stop. Convergence (§5.3) is defined as two chains sharing a JCT
node and would lose most of its instances. The literal fork in the script (§9)
would be a fork drawn around a hole. And the Ladder plaque (Example 2) turns on
`harnuli-ar`: **both of its standing readings assume a junction that the record may
never have asserted.** Eighty years of argument about the scope of `-sen` would
then have been conducted over a node whose status was never claimed.

#### (c) ADJ may not be an edge class — the Redfern damage-convention reading (1993)

**The Standard's position.** `-yun/yu-` ADJ: adjacency attested, orientation
unrecovered. A seventh edge class, and the only one that declines to state
orientation.

**Redfern (1993), and the core observation is disputed by nobody.** **No ADJ pair
is attested.** In every occurrence, exactly one half is present. The head form
`yu-` sits in the §4.2 table because the 1908 rule derives a head from a tail's
first CV — not because a head has ever been recovered on any object, in any
channel, in any stratum. Redfern's conclusion: `-yun` is not an edge half at all.
It is an editorial mark inherited from the branch registers, consolidated into the
paradigm by mistake in 1908, and it means *an edge half stood here and is lost*.

**Why it is uncomfortable.** If Redfern is right, 1908 promoted a clerk's damage
note into the grammar — which is the Palimpsest thesis in miniature, executed
inside a single channel, by HEL's own committee, within documented memory. Palimpsest
partisans cite him constantly. The Archive finds this irritating and has not found
it answerable.

**The defence.** ADJ halves obey the same shape constraints as genuine edge halves
— CV(C), permitted coda, head derivable by the ordinary rule — and a clerk copying
marks he could not read had no reason to invent something that behaves like a
morpheme nobody had yet analysed. The counter-reply is that shape conformity is
exactly what careful copying produces, and that the argument therefore proves the
clerks were good at their jobs.

**Operational consequence.** 87 edges in HEL's graph rest on an ADJ half and
nothing else. Under Redfern they are not edges; they are holes, and the graph is
smaller than the Archive draws it. The edge-class inventory becomes six. And §5.1's
"nearest thing to a generic *go*" disappears entirely — which, for canon purposes,
is the direction this architecture would rather be wrong in: it would mean the
substrate has no orientation-neutral motion form at all.

## 5. Canon pressures, one by one

### 5.1 Directed motion (§6.1)

Handled by the edge class paradigm (§4.2) plus polarity (§4.4). Each of the six
required distinctions has a distinct exponent: permitted traversal = PASS;
hypothetical reverse = BAR, or IMP if no edge exists; arrival = head half;
departure = tail half; intermediate traversal = THRU plus `-is` MED; recurrence at
a similar-but-not-identical coordinate = RECUR. **There is no generic "go."** The
nearest thing is ADJ `-yun/yu-`, which asserts adjacency while explicitly
declining to state orientation — and which HEL forbids in route commands.

**Two of those exponents are contested at paradigm level, and the mapping is
softer than the paragraph above makes it look.** Recurrence rests on RECUR, which
Onslow reads as one class with SHUNT (§4.6a) — under which reading *recurrence* and
*displaced arrival* are not a distinction the corpus draws, and one of handoff
§6.1's six required distinctions is HEL's rather than the substrate's. ADJ is G3 in
its own right (§4.6c); if Redfern is right, the substrate has no orientation-neutral
motion form whatsoever, which strengthens the "no generic go" claim rather than
weakening it. Both possibilities are stated here rather than in a footnote because
the compliance argument for §6.1 should be read at the grade the paradigm actually
carries.

### 5.2 Spacetime as one coordinate (§6.2)

This is the flagship, and the argument for the whole architecture.

The coordinate is a single enclitic constituent with three slots:

```
=LOCUS - PASS - PHASE
```

**LOCUS** — which instance. Filled by a root or by the gap marker `hilun`.

| Filler | Meaning | Confidence | Standing challenge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| root | this instance, designated by the state it was in | **G1** | The slot is formally secure; "which instance" is HEL's reading of what a root in this position does. A root here inherits its own gloss grade, which the Locative–Stative dispute caps at G3 (§4.1) — so the LOCUS slot is never better warranted than the root sitting in it |

LOCUS may also be filled by the gap marker `hilun`, graded once for all three slots
below.

**PASS** — which traversal.

| Form | Meaning | Confidence | Standing challenge |

| `to` | a later pass | **G2** | Same; "later" is the one ordering claim in the paradigm that operational records can check |
| `nu` | another pass, ordering unrecovered | **G1** | An explicitly negative value: it states that ordering is *not* recovered. Nothing can validate it, and the Instrument school notes it is indistinguishable from a default written when a field was not filled |

**PHASE** — position within the traversal, relative to **the anchor**, which is

| `rolun` | displaced behind the anchor, measured; **the magnitude is not in the record** | **G3** | See the PHASE contest below, and **§5.2b** |

**The gap marker**, which fills any of the three slots and is one formative rather

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hilun` | this slot is unfilled, and the record says so | **G2** | Formally beyond dispute and behaviourally validated across a century of records. But whether it means *unknown to the record* or *withheld by the record* has never been settled — see the note below |

#### 5.2a The anchor, defined — and why it gives the topology grammatical teeth

**The anchor of a coordinate is the tail-bearing node of the edge whose head half
the coordinate's carrier word bears.** In plain terms: **a coordinate is *carried*
by the destination node word and *measured from* the origin node word.** Carrier
and anchor are always two different words, and the coordinate is a statement about
the interval between them.

All three worked examples in §8 obey this without exception, and they were written
before the rule was stated, which is the only reason it can be stated now rather
than stipulated. In Example 1 the coordinate sits on `kalsira`, which bears `ru-`;
its anchor is `telnuro`, which bears `-ru`. In Example 2 it sits on `kurhali`
(`pe-`); anchor `harnuli` (`-pel`). In Example 3 it sits on `lartuki` (`si-`);
anchor `sarkile` (`-sil`).

Three consequences, and the second is the one the topology argument has been
missing.

**1. PHASE is origin-relative, and nothing else.** Handoff §6.2 distinguishes
origin-relative, traveler-relative and archive-relative time. **This system
grammaticalizes only the first.** There is no absolute-time slot, no epoch, and no
frame external to the segment. A PHASE value detached from its segment means
nothing at all — it is not a small number missing a unit, it is a relation missing
one of its two terms.

**2. An illegal reversal is ungrammatical, not merely false.** §3.5 notes the price
of orientation-invariant reading: the reversal *edit* — moving the tail half onto
the other node word — is mechanically cheap, one CV move. Cheap to perform;
impossible to make well-formed. Move the tail and the anchor moves with it onto
the word that carries the coordinate, so the coordinate would state a displacement
measured from the word it is attached to. That is not a false coordinate. It is not
a coordinate: carrier and anchor have collapsed into one term, the interval has no
second endpoint, and the segment is catalogued ill-formed in the same class as one
with a slot missing (§5.2, obligatory overt ignorance). **Prohibited shortcut #5 is
therefore blocked structurally and not by policy** — a well-formed segment cannot
assert traversal against an edge, because the assertion does not compose. Note what
this does *not* touch: reading an existing chain from the far end moves nothing,
so orientation-invariant reading survives intact. Reading is free; editing does not
produce a sentence.

**3. It is why truncated citation destroys operational value (§5.7).** Replace
PASS and PHASE with `hilun` and the published segment keeps its entire graph — nodes,
edge classes, orientation, incidence, polarity, provenance — and loses the only
thing measured from anywhere: the displacement across the last leg. The redaction
removes the relation and leaves the topology, which is exactly the split HEL wants
and exactly why the convention was adoptable. For the same reason **a chain with a
node missing loses its coordinate even when the cartouche is undamaged**: the
anchor is a specific word, and if that word is gone the coordinate is intact and
unreadable.

**Confidence, and the part that stays contested.** That the anchor is a
*tail-bearing origin* node is **G2** — the route tables have been built on it since
1952 and a century of arrival records is consistent with it. What is **G3** is
*which* tail-bearer, on a chain of more than one edge: the Standard takes the
immediately preceding one (Example 1 anchors on `telnuro`), and **Sturge (1958)**
argues for the chain-initial TERM node (`harnuli`), on the ground that a record
describing a whole route would measure from where the route began. The two
readings coincide on single-edge segments, which is most of the corpus, and
disagree on the **419 catalogued segments** whose coordinate sits on a chain of
more than one edge. **The dispute cannot be settled, and the reason is §5.2b: a
single recovered magnitude would decide it in an afternoon, and there is no
recovered magnitude anywhere in the corpus.** The structural guarantee in
consequence 2 is unaffected either way — both readings anchor on a tail-bearer, so
both collapse under the reversal edit.

This definition is a statement about how a slot is interpreted, not a new paradigm
value. Its grades are not counted among the thirty-five of §4 and §5.2, on the same
basis as the stratum grades in §7 and the bound-pair grades in §10.1.

#### The PHASE contest, stated plainly because three of the document's nine G3s are here

The four-way *formal* contrast is secure — the values are distinct,
distributionally clean, and nobody disputes that the paradigm has four members.
What is disputed is the gloss. "Measured" is not observed anywhere; it is inferred
from the existence of `nikur`, by the reasoning *if one value says the displacement
was not measured, the other two must be saying it was*. That is an inference from
paradigmatic contrast, not a reading of any record.

The Instrument school's competing reading: the three-way is purely qualitative —
*ahead*, *behind*, *unresolved* — and no measurement is implied by any of them. **No
magnitude has ever been recovered from any PHASE value in any segment**, which the
Archive currently explains as damage and the Instrument school explains as there
never having been one.

#### 5.2b The magnitude is not in the record, and this is the blank the architecture is built around

Everything else this document is uncertain about is **lexical** — what a root
means, what `[41]` is, what the ritual-only units are for. None of it impedes
reading a route. A reader who has followed §4 and §5.2 this far can take a
complete, undamaged segment and extract the origin state, the edge class, the
orientation, the incidence, the destination state, the polarity, the provenance,
and the sign of the displacement. That is an answer key for the edge it describes,
and an architecture that hands one over has made the Box look more decipherable
than canon permits (prohibited shortcut #2; §11 item 7 names this as the failure
mode this proposal would actually die of).

**So the blank goes in the load-bearing slot.** The measured-versus-unmeasured
contrast is formally secure. **The unit and the quantity are not recoverable at
all.** HEL knows that a displacement was measured, and by what sign, and has never
once known by how much.

Three standing accounts of why, none eliminable (Authority C, unresolved):

| | Account | Its weakness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Compartment damage** (Archive standard) | The magnitude was written inside the PHASE compartment as an interior ruling. Cartouche interiors are the shallowest incision on any Ch-1 object (§9) and are the first surface lost to wear | A loss rate of **100%, across 4,100 tokens, four channels and every state of preservation**, is not what damage does. The Archive has never explained the rate and does not claim to |
| **Never in the written channel** | The magnitude lived in Ch-3 modulation, whose *sequence* is recoverable and whose *shape* is not (§2.3). Ch-1 carried the sign; the quantity was in the register HEL can log but cannot read | Requires the four-channel option (§12.1 option 3) and requires Ch-1 and Ch-3 to be one system, which is the Palimpsest question and is not settled |
| **There never was one** (Instrument school) | The three-way is qualitative. `pukal`/`rolun`/`nikur` are *ahead*/*behind*/*unresolved*, no measurement implied, and "measured" is HEL's inference from paradigm shape | Does not explain why the paradigm distinguishes `nikur` at all. Two qualitative values plus an unresolved value is a three-way; a *measured/unmeasured* cut across a two-way directional contrast is a different shape, and the corpus is compatible with both |

**Note what the second account is, structurally.** It is the same shape as §7.1's
missing boundary cue: **the evidence HEL needs is not lost, it was never written
where HEL can read it.** Better preservation of a channel that did not carry the
quantity yields cleaner copies of a channel that did not carry the quantity. No
future expedition repairs this, and that is why it is a design decision rather than
a plot hook.

**What it costs, which is the point of having done it.** Every recovered route is
fully legible as *topology* and permanently under-determined in the one dimension a
crew has to plan around: how far ahead or behind the leg's origin they will come
out. A century of operational correlation could confirm that a route behaved as its
topology predicted. It could never confirm a magnitude, because there was never a
stated magnitude to confirm against. **This is the concrete reason the
characteristic grade in this document is G2 and not certainty** (§5.4) — the method
was structurally incapable of producing certainty about the quantity, no matter how
many expeditions ran.

**And the operational consequence that matters most for canon.** HEL plans
magnitude from its own arrival logs, per route, with error bars accumulated over
decades — never from the corpus. Which means: **a route HEL has never taken cannot
be planned from the record.** The corpus will tell a crew that the edge exists,
that it is sanctioned, which way it points, and that they will come out displaced
ahead. It will not tell them by how much, and nothing HEL owns will tell them
until someone goes. The corpus is a map of the network's structure and is not, and
cannot become, an answer key for travelling it.

**Every canon-facing argument in this document survives this intact**, because all
of them are claims about *which slots are filled* and none is a claim about a
magnitude: the congruence with canon A (below), the impossibility of a bare year
identifying a destination (consequence 1 of §5.2a; §13 item 6), §5.5's
consequence-preceding-departure coordinate, and the whole of §5.1. Nothing is
retracted here; one number is removed, and it was never there.

*Operational consequence.* HEL's route tables carry a column headed *displacement
measured*, populated from `pukal` and `rolun`, and beside it a column headed
*magnitude* that has been printed **empty** since 1952. Under the qualitative
reading the first column records a property of HEL's own notation and nothing about
any route, and a crew reading it is being told that a quantity exists which the
record may never have contained. Neither column has ever been marked provisional.
Field crews call the empty one **the second column** and it is where the League's
standing joke about the Archive lives.

**The claim that makes this architecture worth building:** the substrate's three
primitives do not map onto human *where* and *when*. English "when" is a lossy
function of PASS and PHASE together. English "where" is a lossy function of LOCUS
and PHASE together. **Neither human coordinate is a primitive here, and each
substrate primitive feeds both human ones.**

#### The congruence with canon A — stated carefully, because the careless version is the most dangerous sentence this architecture could write

Run canon A through it. "Return to 2024" is a specification of PHASE and PASS with
LOCUS left as `hilun`. LOCUS is not derivable from PHASE and PASS. **Exactly one
thing follows: the record is silent about the emergence locus** — the coordinate
cannot state where the traveler comes out, and, because ignorance is obligatorily
overt, it must announce that it cannot.

**What does not follow, and what an earlier draft of this document asserted:** that
the traveler therefore emerges somewhere else. That inference does not hold and
must not be made. **A grammar cannot predict a physical fact. It can only mirror
one.** The canon fact — an attempted return to 2024 may emerge in Los Angeles
rather than Chicago (handoff §3.4, **Authority A**) — is a fact about the network,
established by the canon and not by anything in this file. No property of a
notation entails it. A document that says otherwise is reading the substrate as a
true model of the network's physics, which is the shortest available route to
prohibited shortcut #2, and it quietly settles the Utterance/Instrument dispute
toward Instrument on the way — because only a system built by or for the network
could have physics as a theorem.

**What is actually here is a structural congruence, and it is worth more than the
theorem was.** The coordinate's three primitives are shaped so that the human
question *"where will I come out, given a time?"* is **not formulable in them.**
Canon A says that question has no determinate answer in the network. The
notation's inexpressibility and the network's indeterminacy line up exactly, at
the level of structure rather than of content. That is a real, checkable, unforced
fit between an Authority C design and an Authority A fact, and it is the reason
this architecture is worth building. It is also all that can honestly be claimed.

**HEL has noticed. HEL cannot agree on what it means, and the disagreement is the
schools' dispute in its purest form.**

| School | What the congruence means | What would confirm it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Instrument** | The notation fits the network because it was **built by or for the network.** A format written to describe this graph would have exactly these primitives and exactly this inexpressibility; the fit is design, and a fit this clean is not something that happens by acculturation | Evidence that the corpus is emitted rather than composed. Nothing in any channel supplies it, and the four-channel structure is itself only a recommendation (§12.1) |
| **Utterance** | The notation fits the network because **its users lived under the same constraint.** Human languages grammaticalize the distinctions their speakers cannot avoid and let the rest go; a community that could never treat a date as a destination would stop having a way to say one, and would not notice it was missing | Evidence of users. That is the Authority D question, and nothing in the corpus establishes it |
| **Palimpsest** | The congruence is partly HEL's. A coordinate paradigm assembled in 1908 to cover material from more than one system will be under-specified by construction, and an under-specified paradigm will of course fail to determine a locus | Would require sorting the channels, which is the question Palimpsest exists to keep open |

**This is the single most-cited passage in the whole dispute — cited by all three
schools, from the same evidence, in support of incompatible conclusions.** And it
is the clearest case of why none of them can win. The congruence is *symmetrical*:
any system that had to operate under a constraint will come to reflect that
constraint, whether it was engineered to reflect it or grew up inside it. Telling
"built for the network" apart from "used by people living under the network"
requires knowing something about who or what made the corpus — which is Authority
D, is where the handoff puts it, and is where this architecture leaves it.

**The strongest single argument on this document is therefore also its strongest
unresolved question. They are the same sentence, and that is the correct outcome
rather than a concession** — a design whose best feature settled an Authority D
question would be a design this project could not use.

**Obligatory overt ignorance.** `hilun` is not silence. An unfilled slot must be
*marked* unfilled; a coordinate with a slot simply absent is ill-formed and
catalogued as damaged. **You cannot underspecify quietly in this language.** This
is the deepest thing about it: a machine would leave a field null, and a careless
speaker would leave it out; here, not knowing is something you must say.

`hilun` is graded **G2**, and the residual uncertainty is worth naming: the marker's
formal status is beyond dispute and a century of records behave as the reading
predicts, but whether it means *unknown to the record* or *withheld by the record*
has never been settled. §5.7's truncated-citation convention works precisely
because HEL can exploit that ambiguity without resolving it — a redaction system
built on a word whose meaning the redactors cannot pin down.

Derived distinctions, all grammatical rather than lexical:
- same place, different time → same LOCUS, PASS `to`/`nu`
- same time, different place → LOCUS differs, PHASE `kihar`
- same node, different traversal → same LOCUS, PASS `nu` (**this is the
  distinction with no English equivalent, and it is a slot, not a word**)
- predicted / observed / intended destination → warrant `hos` / `tir`-`nes` /
  grade `-ti` SIM respectively — three different mechanisms, which is why HEL
  translators keep collapsing them

### 5.3 Loop topology (§6.3)

Sequence and adjacency are the chain itself. Junction is `-ar` JCT. Convergence
is two chains sharing a JCT node. Recurrence is the RECUR class. **Path dependence
is grammaticalized in the PASS slot**: the same root at different PASS values is a
different coordinate, so "the same node on a later pass" is not sayable as "the
same place again." Route completion: a chain whose terminal node BINDs back to
its initial node closes the trace; nested cycles are traces enclosed by traces in
the script (§9). Inaccessible reverse edges: BAR, distinguished from IMP.

### 5.4 Evidentiality (§6.4)

Split across layers deliberately, which is what handoff §6.4 permits. Five
warrants live in the substrate grammar (§4.5). The rest — *deprecated*,
*contradicted by later evidence*, *translated provisionally*, *ritually preserved*
— live in **HEL's annotation layer**, because they are facts about HEL's
scholarship, not about the corpus. Confidence grades:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **G1 Structural** | parse secure, gloss not |
| **G2 Operationally validated** | acted on; outcomes consistent; semantics still unverified |
| **G3 Contested** | two or more standing readings |
| **G4 Provisional** | single analyst, unreplicated |
| **G5 Preserved** | transmitted, not analyzed (most Ch-4) |
| **G0 Deprecated** | retained in archive, withdrawn from use |

G2 is the characteristic HEL grade and the one that should unsettle a reader: a
reading HEL has staked expeditions on for a century and cannot demonstrate is
correct.

**These grades are not reserved for readings of individual objects.** Every value
in every paradigm of §4 and §5.2 carries one, and the distribution is stated at the
head of §4: ten G1, fifteen G2, nine G3, one G5, none unqualified. A grammar
recovered from 4,100 tokens without a bilingual is itself a reading, and a document
that grades its examples while presenting its paradigms bare is grading the easy
half. Three categories are contested at the level of the whole category, with named
partisans, dates, and stated operational consequences (§4.6).

### 5.5 Causality under constraint (§6.5)

- Ordinary sequence: PASS `sa`, PHASE `pukal`.
- Cause inferred across a loop: PASS `nu` with warrant `hos`.
- **Consequence preceding the traveler's departure frame:** head node coordinate
  with PASS `sa` and PHASE `rolun` — displaced *behind*, measured, on the same
  pass, **and by an amount the record does not state and HEL has never recovered
  (§5.2b)**. It is a legal coordinate. HEL's translators have no comfortable
  English for it and the Archive style manual instructs them not to invent one.
- Precaution against loop effects: **shadow segments** — a SIM-graded edge on an
  `-ol` OFF node. A modeled traversal that was not taken, recorded anyway. Why a
  system would log an edge that did not occur is the Utterance school's single
  strongest argument — **and the Instrument school's answer is not the weak one an
  earlier draft of this document called it.** Contingency logging is what every
  engineered planner does: a system that evaluates a route before committing to it
  writes the evaluation down, and a log of considered-and-rejected options is more
  characteristic of a machine than of a speaker, not less. The Utterance school's
  reply is that the corpus marks these with the same SIM grade it uses everywhere
  else rather than with a distinct class, which is what a *language* would do and
  not what a log format would do. Neither reply has moved, and §5.5's deflation
  below cuts underneath both of them.
- Correlation refused: the `lekur`-alone rule (§4.5).

**The shadow-segment deflation, which this document owes the reader and did not
previously state.** A shadow segment is identified by a parse: `-ti` SIM grade on
an edge whose other node is `-ol` OFF. Both of those markers are among the weakest
in the paradigm — SIM is **G1** and cannot be operationally validated even in
principle (§4.3), OFF is **G1** and nothing operational turns on it (§4.3) — and
the reading depends on the pairing of two values neither of which any expedition
outcome can test.

Against that, note what the corpus is: **~41% of sequences terminate in a broken
half** (§4.2), and §4.2 also records that the break inventory has been re-audited
exactly once, in 1963, and only for `-ru` (§10.1a). **The statistically likeliest
account of a shadow segment is therefore not deliberation. It is a misparse of
damage** — a segment whose head-bearing node was lost, whose surviving node was
marked OFF for an unrelated reason, and whose grade was read as SIM because a
modeled edge is what the Archive expects a dangling SIM to be. On that account
there are **no shadow segments at all**, the Utterance school's single strongest
argument in this section is an artifact of the same break statistics that hid
`kalsira-ru` for fifty-five years, and the corpus records no traversal that did not
occur.

Nobody has run that audit. It is the same audit §4.2 says has never been run, and
it would be run on the same material. The Archive's position is that shadow
segments occur on intact objects at a rate damage does not explain; **the
Instrument school's reply is that this is precisely what was said about the 24
`-ru` breaks before 1963, by the same Archive, on the same kind of evidence.**
Unresolved, and entered in the cosmology ledger at §12 row 16 rather than left
implicit, because "the corpus records traversals that did not occur" is a claim
about what the network's records contain and therefore about the universe.

### 5.6 Stewardship not mastery (§6.6)

**There is no possessive construction in the reconstructed grammar.** None. A
relation between a bearer and a thing is expressed as *incidence*: the bearer is
an `-ol` OFF node BIND-linked to the thing. Off-path, peripheral, controlling no
agreement.

The bearer marker is the whole of the architecture's agent marking: present in
~14% of segments, never controlling agreement, and **undeterminable as to whether
it designates a traveler, a device, a warrant-holder, or the record itself.** That
is what "agent marking optional or structurally secondary" means when the canon
forbids you to know whether there was an agent.

HEL's institutional culture absorbed this: *the substrate has no way to say mine*
became a slogan of the custodial ethic, and the Archive's linguists have been
pointing out for decades that absence in a 4,100-token corpus is not absence in a
language — a deflation nobody in the field wants to hear. **Dependency flag:** the
custodianship framing is Authority B (handoff §4.3, §4.4); if custodianship is not
ratified, this section survives as pure grammar and loses its institutional
resonance.

Handoff §6.6's other distinctions map to grade and polarity: authorized access =
`-lan` AUTH; containment without ownership = BIND with polarity `-nir` UNA;
observation without interference = an `-ol` OFF node with warrant `tir` and no
edge into the chain — **a node you looked at and did not touch, and the grammar
shows the not-touching as an absence of edge.**

### 5.7 Secrecy (§6.7)

The architecture supplies HEL's redaction tool from its own grammar. **A complete
coordinate is an operational secret** — it is a route. Publication convention is
**truncated citation**: replace PASS and PHASE with the gap marker `hilun`. The
published form is fully grammatical, fully citable, and operationally useless.
Redaction that does not look like redaction, produced by the language's own
obligatory ignorance-marker.

**§5.2a is what makes the convention work, and it is worth being precise about
what the redaction removes.** PHASE is measured from the anchor — a specific other
word in the same segment — so striking PHASE does not blur a value, it deletes a
*relation*. The published segment keeps the entire graph: which nodes, which edge
classes, which way each edge points, what the incidence is, what the polarity is,
how the record knows. All of that is topology, all of it is publishable, and none
of it lets a reader plan anything. What leaves with PASS and PHASE is which
traversal and how displaced from where — the two facts that turn a graph into a
route. **The Archive publishes the map and withholds the itinerary, using one
word.**

Two honest notes. First, a redaction that leaves the graph intact still leaks the
graph, and the Archive knows it: truncated citation is a control on *routing*, not
on *cartography*, and the cover terms below exist because the second problem needed
a separate tool. Second, a truncated citation is formally indistinguishable from a
genuinely ignorant record — which is the whole trick, and which means **the
published literature cannot be used as corpus evidence about how often the source
records left a slot unfilled.** The catalogue is kept clear of it by policy: only
collated objects enter the CSC, and citation forms never do. That policy dates
from 1949 and covers nothing published before it.

Clearance attaches to grades (AUTH forms are restricted, §4.3) and to complete
coordinates, not to a vocabulary. Cover terms are English and deliberately
mundane: *the Ladder*, *Quiet Sixteen*, *the Reading Room*. Archival names outlive
their hypotheses because catalog identifiers are stable by policy: CSC-0012 keeps
its number when its gloss is withdrawn.

### 5.8 Ritual without confirmed mysticism (§6.8)

Ch-4 formulae are metrically regular — fixed syllable counts, fixed edge parity —
and **the written channels show no meter at all.** Either humans imposed meter
over centuries of recitation, or Ch-4 preserves prosody the other channels cannot
record. The evidence cannot decide, and this architecture does not decide.

Ch-4 contains **ritual-only units** occurring nowhere else, which the Archive
numbers and does not translate, and it always contains `[41]` in the warrant slot.
Deviation from formula correlates with expedition failure at margins the Archive
publishes annually and declines to interpret. Nobody in HEL says it is magic.
Nobody in HEL says it is nothing. Field crews recite it. Analysts note that the
correlation has never been tested against a control condition, because designing
that experiment means deliberately omitting the formula on a live expedition, and
no committee has approved it.

## 6. Human-facing layers

The four layers of brief §7 must remain visibly distinct in every document.

### 6.1 Transliteration inventory (Authority C)

**The phonetic form of this language is HEL's, not the corpus's.** This is the
architecture's answer to handoff §5.3 ("whether the substrate was spoken
biologically" must not be established). The 1908 Transcription Standard assigned
speakable syllable-strings to recurring units **as a code for radio and telephone
work**, on acoustic-distance criteria. Nothing about the sound of *harnuli* is
evidence about anything.

- **Consonants (11):** p t k s h m n l r w y
- **Vowels (5):** a e i o u
- **Syllable:** C V (C), coda ∈ {n s r l}. No onset clusters, no coda clusters,
  no geminates.
- **No voicing contrast anywhere.** The 1908 committee excluded b d g v z f
  because voicing and voiceless-fricative contrasts are the first casualties of a
  noisy channel. An engineering decision, recorded in committee minutes, still
  in force.
- **Node roots are vowel-final** in the Standard — a committee choice to keep
  affix boundaries recoverable. The 1949 revision concluded that roughly thirty
  units are probably consonant-final in the source and that the Standard has been
  mis-segmenting them since 1908. The boundary is disputed for those thirty and
  the disputes are printed in the margins. **This is not a marginal annoyance and
  it should not be read as one.** The same rule decides where a notch cluster's
  head half ends and its root begins, and §7.1 sets out what follows: the erosion
  pattern that has been the strongest single argument for reading this material as
  a language is stated over a class of units that the rule itself created.
- **Units with no assigned value** are cited by catalog number in brackets: `[41]`.
  Some of the corpus is unpronounceable on purpose, because HEL does not know.

#### 6.1a The compliance claim, corrected — what is defensible and what is verified

**An earlier draft of this section claimed that "no real-world language supplied
phonological material, texture, or 'flavor'" and that the set was chosen by
acoustic-distance and channel-robustness criteria "and by nothing else." That
claim is withdrawn.** It was a claim about process, and a claim about process is
falsified by a single accidental hit — which makes it worse than the hit it was
meant to deny. This document's own earlier sample forms contained several: an
eleven-consonant inventory with five vowels, no clusters and no codas in practice
lands very close to Japanese, and two of the sample roots landed audibly on it.
Nobody checked the output against the assertion. **A truthfulness defect in a
self-audit is worse than the drift it conceals**, and it is corrected here rather
than quietly repaired.

**What is defensible, and is claimed:**

1. **No real-world language was consulted** in constructing the inventory or any
   form in this document.
2. **The selection criteria are stated and checkable** — the consonant and vowel
   sets, the syllable template, the voicing exclusion, the sanctioned syllable list
   at §6.1b, and the three shape rules at §6.1c–e. A reader can run any form in this
   document against them.
3. **Residual resemblance to real languages is an artifact of the constraint set,
   not of borrowing.** A small consonant inventory with no voicing contrast, five
   cardinal vowels and a restricted coda set has a limited number of possible
   outputs, and some of them will resemble some human language. The rules below
   exist to push the silhouette away from the nearest attractor; they do not
   guarantee a null result, and nothing here claims one.
4. **Every form is screened, with the screen stated** — see §6.1f.

**And the narrow claim that is true, verified, and the one the project actually
needs on record.** The inventory was screened against **Algonquian, Anishinaabe,
Potawatomi and Miami-Illinois** phonology and is **incompatible with all four**, not
merely different from them:

| Feature | This inventory | Ojibwe / Potawatomi / Miami-Illinois |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phonemic vowel length | **absent** | present and central |
| Consonant clusters | **none permitted**, onset or coda | pervasive (`-shk-`, `-nd-`, `-mb-`, `-ng-`) |
| /r/ | **present** | absent |
| /tʃ/, /ʃ/ | **absent** | present and characteristic |
| Word structure | short C V (C) units in slot morphology | polysynthetic verb complexes |

Four independent features, each of which alone would block a derivation, and the
incompatibility is structural rather than statistical: no amount of selection from
this inventory produces a form these phonologies would admit. **That is why
prohibited shortcut #14 is clean here affirmatively rather than by absence**, and
it is the claim to cite. The Chicago anchor supplies HEL's institutional history
and supplies no linguistic material of any kind (handoff §3.2).

#### 6.1b The 1908 sanctioned syllable list

The committee did not license the template's full output. It published a **closed
list of forty-eight syllables**, and **every transliterated unit in the Standard is
built from that list and from nothing else.** The list is the code book; a string
that uses an unlisted syllable is not a mis-transliteration, it is not a
transliteration.

**Open — twenty-two:**

> **ha he hi** · **ka ke ki** · **le li lu** · **ni nu** · **pe pu** ·
> **ra ro ru** · **sa si** · **ti to tu** · **yu**

**Closed — twenty-two** (coda ∈ {n s r l}):

> **har hos kal kas ken kur lan lar lun lus mun nes nir pel sar sen sil tel tir
> tul wal yun**

**Onsetless — four**, the incidence markers only, admitted because node roots are
vowel-final and the resulting hiatus is itself a boundary cue:

> **ar in is ol**

Three properties of the list are engineering decisions with minutes behind them,
and all three do work elsewhere in this document.

- **Each consonant is licensed with a restricted vowel band.** /h/ and /k/ take
  {a e i}; /l/ takes {e i u}; /r/ takes {a o u}; /t/ takes {i o u}; /n/ {i u};
  /p/ {e u}; /s/ {a i}; /y/ only {u}. A mis-heard onset therefore *usually* lands
  on a syllable that is not on the list, so the commonest channel error tends to
  produce a detectable non-syllable rather than a different real word. **The list
  is a checkable code, which is the whole reason to close it.**

  **Two things an earlier draft claimed here were false, and the correction is
  worth more than the claim was.** It asserted that every band is *contiguous* and
  that *no two consonants share a band*. Neither holds: /h/ and /k/ take the
  identical band {a e i} — the draft's own example sentence said so in the clause
  after the claim — and there is no ordering of the five vowels, among all 120,
  under which all nine bands come out contiguous. The bands are restricted, which
  is what does the work; they are not systematic, and the committee's minutes are
  not evidence that they were meant to be.

  **What the list actually achieves, measured rather than asserted.** Substituting
  every other licensed onset into every listed syllable gives 440 single-onset
  confusions. **334 of them (76%) land off the list and are detectable.** The
  figure is not uniform, and the non-uniformity is the interesting part:

  | | Confusions | Detectable | Rate |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Open syllables | 220 | 134 | **61%** |
  | Closed syllables | 220 | 200 | **91%** |

  **A coda is worth thirty points of error detection.** That is a quantitative
  vindication of the next bullet and of §6.1c's minimum coda rate, both of which
  were argued on the ear alone; and it is the strongest available answer to why a
  route command should be built from closed syllables wherever the template allows.
  The undetectable residue is concentrated where the bands overlap — `ha`/`ka`,
  `he`/`ke`, `hi`/`ki` are mutually undetectable in both directions, which is the
  direct operational cost of the /h/–/k/ collision above.
- **/m/ and /w/ are licensed only in closed syllables.** A nasal or glide onset
  before an open vowel is the least recoverable shape on a noisy line; the coda
  gives the ear an edge to find. This is why `mun` and `wal` exist and no open
  syllable beginning with /m/ or /w/ is on the list at all.
- **The list has forty-eight members and it is full** — see §6.1d.

#### 6.1c Minimum coda rate

**Every unit of three or more syllables carries at least one coda.** Node roots are
vowel-final (above), so the coda falls inside the word.

**The rule is not 1908's.** It came in with the Reformed List of 1988 (§6.1e), as
the second half of the same reform, and it is the correction of a real and
embarrassing drift. Under the 1908 Standard no such rule existed and the analyzable
core drifted almost entirely into open syllables: **nine of the roughly ninety core units carried a coda
anywhere. Six of the seven root forms used in this document's worked examples had
none at all** — three syllables each, C V · C V · C V, the blandest shape the
template permits. Read aloud they were pleasant, pronounceable, and sounded like a
well-behaved constructed vocabulary rather than like something recovered under
uncertainty from a damaged object.

Under the rule the figure is **seventy-one of ninety**, and the effect on the
example forms is the whole of the demonstration:

| CSC | Pre-1988 form | Shape | In force | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0007 | *(3 syll., no coda)* | CV·CV·CV | **harnuli** | **CVC**·CV·CV |
| 0012 | *(3 syll., no coda)* | CV·CV·CV | **kalsira** | **CVC**·CV·CV |
| 0031 | *(3 syll., no coda)* | CV·CV·CV | **telnuro** | **CVC**·CV·CV |
| 0058 | *(2 syll.)* | CVC·CV | **sarkile** | **CVC**·CV·CV |
| 0077 | *(3 syll., no coda)* | CV·CV·CV | **lartuki** | **CVC**·CV·CV |
| 0104 | *(3 syll., no coda)* | CV·CV·CV | **kurhali** | **CVC**·CV·CV |
| 0311 | *(3 syll., no coda)* | CV·CV·CV | **lunkani** | **CVC**·CV·CV |

The coordinate fillers moved the same way: the gap marker, the four PHASE values
and the recurrence warrant all carry codas now, and the three PASS values —
`sa`, `to`, `nu` — deliberately do not, because a one-syllable open slot filler is
the fastest thing to say in a slot that is spoken more often than any other.

#### 6.1d Initial-syllable distinctness, and the cap it runs into

**No two units of the commanded core share their first syllable.** The *commanded
core* is the set of units that may lawfully appear in a spoken route command:
node roots carrying route flags, the coordinate fillers, and the warrants. Bound
formatives — edge halves, incidence, grade, polarity, the scope clitic — are
exempt, because they are never spoken alone.

This is what acoustic-distance criteria actually predict. Under stress, over a bad
line, the reliable part of a heard word is its onset; a code designed for radio
work buys its distinctness at the front. Check it against the units in this
document: `harnuli` · `kalsira` · `telnuro` · `sarkile` · `lartuki` · `kurhali` ·
`lunkani` · `hilun` · `kihar` · `pukal` · `rolun` · `nikur` · `lekur` · `nes` ·
`tir` · `hos` · `wal` · `sa` · `to` · `nu` — twenty units, twenty distinct opening
syllables, no exceptions.

**The rule has teeth because it has a hard ceiling, and the Archive hit it.** A
list of forty-eight syllables can guarantee initial distinctness for at most
forty-eight units. **The commanded core has stood at forty-eight since 1931.**
Every admission since has required a retirement, which is a standing committee
fight with minutes, and it is the reason the Archive resists promoting newly
recovered units into route-command status even when the parse is clean.

**And this is the reason the readback ritual exists.** The guarantee covers the
*initial* syllable and nothing after it. Two commanded units may converge
completely once past their first syllable — `hilun` and `rolun` do; `lekur` and
`nikur` do; `kihar` shares its second syllable with `harnuli`'s first — so a
mishearing at the *end* of a word is not caught by the rule and is not caught by
the closed list either, since both candidates are legal. Hence §6.2's **double
return**, and hence the specific form it takes: the readback expands every edge
half to its catalog number, because the edge halves are precisely the material the
distinctness rule does not cover.

#### 6.1e The mnemonic leak of 1908, and the vote of 1949

**The 1908 committee did not obey its own criteria, and the Archive has known it
since 1949.**

Its minutes state acoustic distance and channel robustness and nothing else. What
it actually did, in the classes its analysts handled most often, was let **English
and Latin mnemonics into the strings** — a reversal-blocked edge class named after
the English word for a reflecting surface; a displaced-arrival class named from the
first syllable of the English gloss the committee had just written; a command grade
named from the English for *carry out*; a repetition warrant taken from the Latin
for *heaped up*; a displacement value from the Latin for *after*. None of it is
recorded as a decision. It is visible in the pattern: the leak appears in exactly
the six categories the 1908 analysts used daily and in none of the categories they
used rarely, which is what an unexamined habit looks like and not what a criterion
looks like.

**Rowntree's 1949 revision documented all of it, listed the affected strings, and
proposed replacements.** It lost the vote, 11–7. The recorded objection was not
that the finding was wrong — nobody disputed it, then or since — but that the field
had already learned the old forms, that route commands are spoken under load, and
that a Standard which changes what a crew says is more dangerous than a Standard
that embarrasses a committee. **That argument was not unreasonable, and it held for
thirty-nine years.**

The strings the revision attacked stayed in service until the **Reformed List of
1988**, which finally carried the 1949 proposals over the same objection, and only
because by then two of them had been independently flagged as channel liabilities
as well. The 1988 list also introduced the minimum coda rate (§6.1c), on the
argument — which the 1949 revision had not made — that the drift toward open
syllables was a channel problem before it was an aesthetic one: an all-open form
has no internal edge for the ear to find and degrades to mush at the first burst
of line noise. **The retired strings are held in the Archive's deprecated register and
are not reproduced in operational documents**, which is why they are described here
and not printed. Senior crews still say several of them out loud, and the field
manual printed both columns until 1994.

**The respelling cost nothing analytic, and the reason is §5.7's stable-identifier
policy earning its keep.** A catalog number is stable and a gloss is not; a
*transliteration* turns out to be stable in exactly the same way. Not one reading,
route record, dispute, or citation in this document was invalidated by 1988 —
CSC-0012 is CSC-0012 under any spelling, the bound pair at §10.1(a) is the same
pair, the Ladder plaque is the same plaque, and the eighty-year argument about the
scope of `-sen` is the same argument. **What changed is the phonetic costume, which
is HEL's, which §6.1 says carries no information about the corpus, and which is
therefore the one layer in the whole design that can be rewritten without lying.**

That is also the honest limit of the confession: the leak was real, it is owned,
and **the material it leaked into was never evidence about anything** — so what was
lost was the committee's credibility and not a single fact about the corpus.

#### 6.1f The screen

Per the standing rule: **the certification is a statement of what was done, not a
claim of purity.**

**Screen run:** 11 August 2026, on every form appearing in this document — the
seven node roots `harnuli`, `kalsira`, `telnuro`, `sarkile`, `lartuki`, `kurhali`,
`lunkani`; the coordinate fillers `hilun`, `sa`, `to`, `nu`, `kihar`, `pukal`,
`rolun`, `nikur`; the warrants `nes`, `tir`, `lekur`, `hos`, `wal`; and every bound
formative in §4.

**Screened against:** English (including British and North American slang),
Spanish, Mandarin, Hindi, Arabic and French, for obscene, comic, brand and
real-person-name collisions; and **separately and non-negotiably against real
Indigenous language material**, with particular attention to Algonquian,
Anishinaabe, Potawatomi and Miami-Illinois, per prohibited shortcut #14 and handoff
§3.2. **Every form was read aloud** before being kept.

**Weighting, stated so it can be argued with.** Free units are screened harder than
bound ones. A warrant or a coordinate filler is spoken alone and printed alone; an
edge half never occurs without a root attached to it, so a two-letter head form is
assessed in the environments it actually appears in rather than as a standalone
word. That is a judgement, not a law, and a reviewer may reject it.

**Rejected during coining, with the reason** — recorded because a rejects list is
the only evidence a screen was run: *pekur* (English vulgar slang); *putar*
(Spanish obscenity embedded); *kanel* (Chanel); *lewis*, *ronal*, *hal* (real- and
fictional-person names, the last of them an artificial intelligence in a
spacefaring property); *lunar*, *pusar* (astronomical vocabulary — comic in this
setting); *tikal*, *pular* (a real Maya site and a real West African language name
— **rejected under prohibited shortcut #14's worldwide scope, not merely its
North American one**); *kural* (a classical Tamil text); *salir*, *nikal*
(Spanish and Hindi verbs meaning *to leave* and *to come out* — semantically
loaded, which is the mnemonic error of 1908 committed again in a new language);
*kesar* (Caesar — faux-Latin, prohibited shortcut #17); *nul*, *mis* (English
*null* and *mis-*, both of which would have encoded the gap marker's own gloss);
*natsuriel*, and every form ending `-su` or `-masu` (the specific Japanese drift
this revision exists to correct).

**Residuals found and disclosed rather than hidden:**

- `lekur`, `nikur`, `kurhali` — all contain the syllable `kur`, which an English
  reader may map to *cur*. Not audible under the stress rule and never
  word-initial. Accepted.
- `sarkile` — initial `sar-`: Hindi **सर** *sar* and Arabic **سر** *sirr* are
  adjacent. Weak; neither obscene, comic, nor a brand.
- `telnuro` — initial `tel-` carries *Tel Aviv* and the *tele-* prefix in print.
  Weak; not audible as either under final stress (tel-nu-**RO**).
- `hilun` is acoustically close to the RECUR tail `-halu`. **The 1908 minutes
  record the objection and the committee overruled it** on the ground that the two
  can never occupy the same slot — a gap marker fills a coordinate slot and an edge
  tail attaches to a node word. The objection was reasonable and the ruling has
  never been revisited.
- **German, Portuguese, Russian, Japanese and Korean were not on the screened list
  and are not screened here.** Japanese is named because it is the family this
  document drifted into; the list should be extended in Phase 2 and every form
  re-run against it.

**Indigenous-material screen — result and method.** No form in this document is
drawn from, modelled on, or adjacent to any real Indigenous language material, and
the screen was run affirmatively rather than assumed. The structural argument at
§6.1a holds for these forms specifically: no phonemic vowel length, no clusters,
/r/ present, no postalveolars, short C V (C) units rather than polysynthetic verb
complexes.

**What is not claimed:** that no real-world language supplied material. What is
claimed is exactly the above — these forms, these families, this date, these
residuals, this rejects list. All of it belongs in the canon ledger (§12 row 10).

### 6.2 HEL field pronunciation

- **Stress:** final syllable of each node word. Chosen by HEL. Not recovered.
- **Edge halves are spoken unstressed and fast**, which is why they are the first
  thing lost in transmission — and hence:
- **The double return** (checksum ritual, handoff §7.2). A route command is
  spoken; read back with every edge half expanded to its catalog number; confirmed.
  Three turns, always, even when both parties are in the same room. **§6.1d is why
  it takes this exact form**: the initial-syllable rule guarantees the front of a
  commanded unit and nothing after it, and it does not cover the edge halves at all
  — so the readback expands precisely the material the code cannot protect, and
  leaves the material the code does protect to be said once.

> **A.** *Harnuli-in-ken, ke-telnuro-is-ru, ru-kalsira-in. Hilun-to-pukal. Tir lekur.*
> **B.** Harnuli, terminal, edge seven. Telnuro, medial, edge one. Kalsira, terminal.
> Locus open, pass later, phase measured ahead. Witnessed, recurrent.
> **A.** Confirmed, edge seven, edge one.

- **Branch traditions.** The Chicago Reading Room gives `e` as [eɪ] and `o` as
  [oʊ]; the 1949 Standard gives pure [e] and [o]. Field crews mix them freely and
  can tell where you trained within one sentence. Nothing rides on it. Everyone
  has opinions.

### 6.3 English gloss layer

Operational translations — *outbound node*, *reverse closed*, *do not traverse* —
are HEL's, are known to be lossy, and carry a G-grade. A field agent may say a
transliterated term with complete confidence while the footnote records that the
unit may encode topology and not mean "route" at all.

**Interlinear convention (Archive style manual, and a deliberate change from
earlier practice).** In a morpheme-by-morpheme gloss line:

- **A root graded G3 or worse is written as its catalog identifier with a queried
  gloss** — `CSC.0012(sealed?)` — and never as a bare English word.
- **A formative graded G3 carries a question mark on its abbreviation** — `JCT?`,
  `FRB?`, `AUTH?`, `PHASE.AHEAD.MEAS?`.
- A unit with no proposed gloss is written `CSC.0311(?)`.
- Within a gloss line the catalog identifier takes a period — `CSC.0012` — because
  the hyphen is reserved for morpheme boundaries. Everywhere else the Archive
  writes CSC-0012.

The rule exists because the older convention put a confident English word on the
data line and the dispute in a footnote, and readers took the data line and left
the footnote. **Uncertainty has to be met in the primary line or it is not met.**

Two consequences worth stating plainly. First, because the Locative–Stative dispute
caps every arguable root at G3 (§4.1), **every root in every gloss line in this
document appears in catalog form.** There is no example anywhere below in which HEL
is shown knowing what a root means. Second, a catalog number is stable and a gloss
is not (§5.7): when a gloss is withdrawn, the gloss line loses a parenthesis and
keeps its identifier, and every reading that cited it stays citable. The convention
is therefore also the archival mechanism — the reason CSC-0012 can survive the
withdrawal of *sealed* without invalidating a century of route records.

### 7.1 The conditioning environment is graphotactic, and the Archive is divided on what follows

This subsection exists because two statements elsewhere in this document cannot
both be read naively, and the fix is not to soften one of them. It is to state what
the environment actually is.

**§6.1 says:** the phonetic form of this language is HEL's, not the corpus's; the
1908 Standard assigned speakable syllables to recurring units as a radio code, on
acoustic-distance criteria; *nothing about the sound of* harnuli *is evidence about
anything.*

**The Stratum II profile said, until this revision, and still says in the field
manuals:** head halves are regularly *lost* before **vowel-initial** roots.

"Vowel-initial" is a property of the 1908 Standard. There are only two ways to
read that, and this document takes the second.

**The branch this document does not take.** If the Standard's vowels track the
source's, then the environment is phonological, the erosion is sound change, and
the corpus is the record of something with a phonology. That would contradict §6.1
and would begin to establish **whether the substrate was ever biologically
produced** — which handoff §5.3 places outside what any design may settle, and
which no committee minute from 1908 supports in any case, since the committee
chose its syllables for channel robustness and recorded that it was doing so. This
architecture does not take that branch and no material below depends on it.

**The branch this document takes.** The environment is **graphotactic**. Restated
exactly: *a head half is absent from a notch cluster where the following unit is
written with an initial vowel letter in the 1908 Transcription Standard.* That is a
statement about HEL's transcription. Whether it corresponds to anything in the
source is precisely what is not known, and the Archive has been divided about it
since 1984.

**Why the environment might be the committee's rather than the corpus's, and the
reason is in this document's own script section.** §4's opening note is load-bearing
here: the hyphen is HEL's editorial mark, *not present in any source*. Every
division between a head half and its root, in every segment of the corpus, is a
decision the 1908 committee made.

And the place where it made that decision is the worst place available. In the
trace (§9) the intervals between notch clusters are **not blank**: an interval is
where the edge lives, spelled as a departure flick leaving one cluster and an
arrival flick entering the next. So at every junction the committee faced a
continuous run of incised material — arrival flick, then the destination cluster's
opening notches — and had to rule how much of it was flick and how much was a
head-half notch.

**Where the destination root begins with a consonant, the trace separates them for
you:** a consonant notch stands between the arrival flick and the root's first
vowel, and the division is forced by the material. **Where the destination root
begins with a vowel, nothing stands between them.** The flick runs straight into
the root's opening vowel, and whether a head half is present is not read off the
object — it is ruled. The 1908 committee ruled, in those cases, that it was absent.

On this account the conditioning environment is exactly the environment in which
the trace provides no separation and the committee had to choose, which is what one
would expect if the pattern were made by the choosing rather than found.

**The two standing positions (Authority C, unresolved).**

| | **Transmission reading** (Archive standard) | **Segmentation reading** (from Ashcroft 1984) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claim | Head halves were really lost, in a real environment. The Standard's vowels are an imperfect but non-random image of a source distinction | The environment is an artifact of the 1908 cut. Nothing was lost; the material was divided by a rule and the rule left a signature |
| Best evidence | In 63 of the 148 Ch-1 head-absent clusters the **notch count is too low** to have held a head half under any cut. That is independent of the transcription | 1949 already established that the same rule mis-segments ~30 units at the root's other boundary (§6.1). A rule that demonstrably errs at one boundary by stipulation is not a witness at the other. Of the 85 Ch-1 clusters the count-check leaves undetermined, Ashcroft's re-collation against the surviving branch registers found the older branch hands cutting **31 with a head half present** |
| Weakness | The notch-to-syllable correspondence is itself an 1908 stipulation and has never been independently verified. And the check runs only on Ch-1: Ch-2 exists as hand copies, Ch-3 as instrument logs, and neither carries notches to count | The branch registers are nineteenth-century and disagree with each other — Onslow (§4.6a) builds a whole merger argument on the fact that they cut at different points. Register evidence proves the cut was contested, not that it was wrong |

Neither side has moved. The Archive did not restyle the Stratum II profile after
1984; it added a footnote, which is how this Archive concedes.

**What would settle it, and why HEL cannot get it.** One thing would: a boundary
cue in the source — a mark, a spacing, a notch convention, anything that separates
an arrival flick from the first notch of the cluster it enters. Nothing else
discriminates, because both cuts are legal under the Standard and the corpus offers
no third party to ask.

And HEL cannot get it, for a reason that is structural rather than unlucky:
**the writing system does not mark morpheme boundaries.** A record is one
continuous incised line by design (§9), and the interval that would have to carry
the cue is already fully occupied carrying the edge. This is not a preservation
failure that a better find repairs — better preservation of a system that does not
mark boundaries yields cleaner copies of an unmarked system. A bilingual would also
settle it, and §2.3 states that none exists and that this architecture does not
permit one. **The evidence HEL needs is not lost. It was never written.**

**The consequence, stated without cushioning.** Conditioned erosion is the leading
argument that this material is a language rather than a notation (§10.1, property
3). If the Segmentation reading is right, the erosion is not in the corpus. It is
in the Standard. **The best evidence that this is a language may be an artifact of
the tool HEL used to write it down** — a hundred-year-old radio code, built by a
committee for channel robustness, leaving a footprint that a later generation
mistook for the tread of a speaker.

This is the third time in this document that HEL's own apparatus turns out to be
the thing under examination: 1908 consolidated one mark into two classes (§4.6a),
promoted a clerk's damage note into the grammar (§4.6c), and here supplied the
signature that the corpus is a language. **The Archive is the largest uncontrolled
variable in its own evidence, and this is the case where that matters most.**

This dispute is *not* one of the three category-level contests of §4.6 and is not
counted among them; it operates on a stratum profile rather than a paradigm value,
and no formative's grade changes because of it.

### 7.2 The stratigraphic scandal

**The strata do not sort by find-site age.** At several sites, Stratum I material
comes from contexts HEL's own dating places *later* than Stratum II contexts.

In ordinary archaeology this is a crisis. Here there are three standing
explanations:

1. the dating is wrong;
2. the strata are registers, not periods, and there is no chronology to recover;
3. **the material moved through the network.** An object recovered at a node may
   have been deposited by a traversal originating at a different phase. In a
   directed timespace network, find-context is not evidence of age.

Explanation 3 is derived entirely from Authority A canon, and it is devastating:
**in this universe, stratigraphy is not a chronology.** HEL possesses a
100-year-old philological discipline whose foundational method its own physics
invalidates, and it keeps using the method because it has nothing better.

This is where the architecture buys Tolkien-grade diachronic depth without naming
anyone. Tolkien's depth comes from a family tree — Common Eldarin to Quenya and
Sindarin, with sound laws you can run in either direction. That model requires
knowing who spoke what and when. **We cannot have it and must not fake it.** What
we build instead is a corpus with real, systematic, rule-governed internal
variation and *no recoverable ordering* — which is the actual epistemic situation
of Etruscan and of Linear A, and which is in one respect richer than Tolkien's
model: the reconstruction is contested *inside the fiction*, permanently, by
characters with careers and grudges. HEL's linguists are part of the deliverable.

- **The magnitude is absent, as it is from every segment in the corpus (§5.2b).**
  This route is fully legible as topology and states no quantity. A crew is told it

  which is a HEL document and not a reading of this object.
- **The anchor is `telnuro`, not `harnuli`** — the coordinate is carried by `kalsira`,

It is printed in Archive operational style because that is what crews are handed;
the gloss line above it is what the object actually supports.

## 9. Script concept

A record is a **trace**: one continuous incised line. Node-states are notch
clusters *on* the line; edges are the *intervals between* clusters, and edge class
is spelled by the shape of the line's departure from one cluster and its arrival
at the next — a departure flick and an arrival flick, which are the two halves of
the bipartite edge made visible. Orientation therefore lives in the flicks and not
in the line's overall course, so a trace may be read from either end, may spiral,
may fork at a junction (a literal fork, which is what `-ar` JCT is), and may close
on itself to mark a completed cycle, with nested cycles drawn as traces enclosed
by traces. Coordinates sit in a cartouche interrupting the line, with one
compartment per slot and an explicitly ruled *empty* compartment for `hilun` — an
unfilled coordinate is visibly reserved, never merely absent, which is how HEL
distinguishes an ignorant record from a damaged one. Warrants sit off the line
entirely as marginal ticks joined to their segment by a lead-line, which is why
warrants are the first thing lost to edge damage and why so much of the corpus is
catalogued as damaged rather than unwarranted. The **field hand** collapses the
trace to a single ruled baseline with above/below tick notation, writable with a
pen in a field notebook at speed and typable as a linear cipher — the same
information, none of the geometry, and the Archive considers it a barbarism.

Three notes the script has to carry, because three arguments above depend on it.

**The trace reads from either end; the edge does not.** Orientation-invariant
reading (§3.5) is a property of the *reading*, and the flicks hold their direction
however the eye travels. **The record is reversible; the network is not.** A
draughtsman who redraws the flicks the other way has not written the reverse route,
he has written a segment whose coordinate no longer composes (§5.2a).

**The cartouche is where the anchor is visible.** It interrupts the line
immediately after the arrival flick of the edge it belongs to, so the object itself
shows which node the coordinate is carried by and which node it is measured from.
On a chain of more than one edge the cartouche's position is the whole of the
evidence for the Standard's reading against Sturge's, and it is not decisive: a
cartouche sits where the coordinate is carried, and nothing on the object says
where it is measured from.

**The compartments are the shallowest incision on any object**, which is the
Archive's account of where the missing magnitude went (§5.2b) and also the reason
that account is doubted: shallow rulings survive on some objects, and no PHASE
magnitude survives on any.

## 10. Is this a language or is it machine notation?

The prompt asks me to confront this rather than paper over it. Here is the honest
answer in three parts.

### 10.1 Where it is genuinely a language

Four properties are offered as evidence that formalisms characteristically lack.
They are tested here rather than asserted, and **the test costs one of them
outright and puts two more in doubt.** That result is printed rather than
suppressed, because a self-audit that every candidate survives is not a test.

---

#### Property 1 — obligatory categories that are not informative. **Withdrawn: it does not discriminate.**

**The claim as previously made.** A notation encodes what is needed. This system
forces an overt marker on every unfilled coordinate slot (`hilun`) and a warrant on
every segment *including a warrant meaning "none"* (`wal` UNW). That was offered as
redundancy of the kind natural languages carry and engineered formats strip out,
on the model of obligatory evidentiality in Tariana or obligatory gender in French.

**Why it fails.** A fixed-width record format with a mandatory NULL token has
exactly this property, and has it *because* it is engineered. Every field is
written; a field with no value is written with an explicit token rather than left
empty; the token is obligatory and carries no payload. That is not linguistic
redundancy. It is framing — a parser that cannot see field boundaries needs the
placeholder, and the more rigidly specified the format, the more of these it has.
The property therefore selects the very thing it was meant to exclude, and it
selects it more reliably than it selects languages.

The comparison to Tariana and French also does not survive inspection. French
gender is arbitrary, predictable from nothing outside the lexicon, and tells a
hearer nothing about the world. `hilun` is neither arbitrary nor uninformative: it
says the record does not have the value, it is fully predictable from the state of
the record, and a reader learns something true from every occurrence. **`hilun` is not
a non-informative obligatory category. It is an informative one, and the property
as titled does not describe the data.**

**What the property actually establishes.** Two things, both real, neither of them
language-hood:

- **Architecturally:** the system cannot underspecify silently. That is
  load-bearing — it is what §5.2's obligatory ignorance-marking, §5.7's truncated
  citation, and HEL's ability to tell an ignorant record from a damaged one all
  run on. It is one of the best things in this design. It is simply not evidence
  about the corpus's nature, because a format can have it and formats usually do.
- **Methodologically:** because gaps are marked, HEL's parses are *complete* — an
  unfilled slot is data rather than a hole. That is why this corpus supports a
  confidence scale at all. It is a fact about what HEL can do with the material,
  not about what the material is.

**And the honest cost of stating the test properly.** The redundancy that *would*
discriminate is **agreement**: an obligatory category whose value is predictable
from elsewhere in the same segment and therefore carries no information at all.
Languages are full of it. Formats have almost none, because it is pure cost.
**This system has no agreement anywhere.** §5.6 records that the bearer marker
controls none, and nothing else in the paradigm controls any either. That absence
is a point *against* the Utterance school, it has not been offered by anybody as
one, and it is entered here because the section is supposed to be a test.

#### Property 2 — structural ambiguity. **Retained, thin, and now doubly dependent.**

Notations are designed to be unambiguous. The `-sen`-scope problem in Example 2 is
not a gap in decipherment; it is ambiguity *in the system*, of a kind format
designers eliminate and languages tolerate for millennia. That much stands.

Two qualifications belong on the data line rather than in a footnote. First, **it
is one instance.** One is enough to make the point in principle and too few to
weigh anything on. Second, the instance is not a designed ambiguity but a
*transmission* ambiguity: it exists because the disambiguating clitic `-he` is
absent from Stratum II. A format whose later version drops a field produces the
same effect, and calls it a version. So property 2 rests on the Stratum II profile
being a history rather than a filing convention — which is the question §7.1 leaves
open. **Properties 2 and 3 are not independent evidence. They are the same
evidence, counted twice.** They are printed separately above because that is how
the Archive has always presented them, and printed as dependent here because they
are.

#### Property 3 — conditioned erosion. **Contested, not evidence: G3.**

**The claim.** Stratum II loses head halves in a specific, statable environment.
Formats do not erode; they version. Erosion in a conditioned environment is a
signature of transmission, and this has been the leading argument for reading the
material as a language.

**The environment is graphotactic, and §7.1 sets out what follows.** Head halves
are absent where the following unit is vowel-initial **in the 1908 Transcription
Standard**. "Vowel-initial" is a property of HEL's radio code, not a recovered
property of the source, and §6.1 says in terms that nothing about the sound of
these transliterations is evidence about anything. The Archive has been divided
since 1984 on whether the pattern is a real loss (Transmission reading) or the
footprint of the committee's own segmentation tie-breaker (Segmentation reading).
Both positions have real evidence; neither has moved.

**The alternative branch is closed on canon grounds, not rhetorical ones.** The
only way to keep this property at full strength is to hold that the Standard's
vowels track the source's — which would make the environment phonological, the
erosion sound change, and the corpus the record of something with a phonology.
That establishes what handoff §5.3 forbids anyone to establish: whether the
substrate was ever biologically produced. **This document will not buy a
language-hood argument at that price**, and the property is downgraded instead.

**What would settle it.** A boundary cue in the source — any mark, spacing, or
notch convention separating an arrival flick from the first notch of the cluster it
enters. Nothing else discriminates: both cuts are legal under the Standard's own
syllable rules, and the Standard is the only witness there is.

**Why HEL cannot get it, and the reason is structural rather than unlucky.** The
writing system does not mark morpheme boundaries. A record is one continuous
incised line (§9), and the interval that would have to carry the cue is already
fully occupied carrying the edge. The editorial hyphen that appears throughout this
document is stated at the head of §4 to be HEL's own and *not present in any
source*. **The corpus was never written in a way that could carry the evidence**,
so no future recovery repairs this — better preservation of a system that does not
mark boundaries yields cleaner copies of an unmarked system. A bilingual would also
settle it; §2.3 states that none exists and that this architecture does not permit
one.

**The result, stated without cushioning: the best evidence that this is a language
may be an artifact of the tool used to write it down.** A hundred-year-old radio
code, built by a committee on acoustic-distance criteria for channel robustness,
may have left a footprint that a later generation of analysts mistook for the tread
of a speaker. HEL cannot currently tell, and the reason it cannot is that HEL made
the instrument.

#### Property 4 — non-compositionality. **Demonstrated. Two pairs, in full.**

This is the property that would most decisively establish language-hood, and until
now it was asserted with nothing behind it. The Archive identifies eleven **bound
pairs** — root-plus-edge combinations whose attested use is not derivable from the
parts. Two are set out here in full. The remaining nine are reserved (§10.3).

One preliminary, and it matters for the grades below: **a bound pair's reading does
not inherit the Locative–Stative G3 ceiling** that §4.1 places on every root gloss.
It cannot, because the pair's attested use is not derived from the root — that is
what makes it a bound pair. This is the only class of item in the document that
escapes the ceiling, and it escapes it by being the thing under discussion.

##### (a) `kalsira-ru` — the pair that gave crews the opposite route

```
kalsira-ru
CSC.0012(sealed?)-PASS.TAIL

Literal parse    An origin node in the CSC.0012 state, with a sanctioned edge
                 departing it. A destination word bearing the head half `ru-`
                 is required to complete the edge; without one the segment is
                 a broken half and the destination is missing.

Attested use     Sanctioned traversal reaches CSC.0012 and is not sanctioned
                 beyond it. The tail does not project an edge. Nothing is
                 required to follow, and in Strata II–III nothing ever does.
```

**The two readings give opposite routes off the same object.** Literal: *there is a
sanctioned way onward from here — take it.* Attested: *the sanction ends here — do
not go on.* Not a shade of meaning. The other direction.

**Distribution.** 27 occurrences. Three are Stratum I and each is completed by a
following `ru-` head half — fully compositional. Twenty-four are Strata II–III and
not one is completed. From 1908 those 24 were catalogued `-ru ⌀`, filed as damage,
and fed into the graph as inferred missing nodes.

**How it was found, in 1963, by someone looking at something else.** Keele was
auditing break statistics after the Quiet Sixteen incident (below) and noticed that
the 24 breaks are not distributed like damage. They sit on intact, undamaged
objects; they occur at segment interiors as often as at margins; and no object
carrying one carries any other break. **Damage does not select a root.** The
broken-half method (§4.2) is blind to this by construction: a lexicalized tail and
a damaged one present identically, and with 41% of sequences already terminating in
a break, 24 more were invisible.

**The operational cost.** Between 1908 and 1963 the field manual carried twelve
route commands whose onward leg rested on the literal parse. The post-1963 review
found that four of those onward legs have no independent attestation anywhere in
the corpus: **HEL had been routing crews down edges the record never asserted, on
the strength of a parse that said the opposite of what the record says.**

Most of them worked, and that is the worse half of the story. A crew that
traverses successfully cannot distinguish *the record asserted this edge* from *an
edge happens to be there*. Operational correlation — the method this entire
architecture is decipherable by, and the method that defines G2 (§5.4) — therefore
**confirmed the error for fifty-five years instead of catching it.** This is the
clearest demonstration in the document of exactly what G2 is worth.

The exception was the **Quiet Sixteen approach, 1961.** A crew took the onward leg
from a pair-marked node, arrived displaced from intent — a SHUNT outcome the record
had never flagged, because the record had never claimed the edge — and could not be
recovered directly. The return was routed through two intermediate nodes and
emerged at a locus the Archive had not catalogued, in a frame fourteen months later
than the departure frame. That expedition is why Keele was counting breaks.

**Institutional aftermath.** The Archive struck the literal reading in 1963 and the
field manual **printed both readings side by side until 1967**, with a note saying
they were incompatible, because withdrawing the onward legs meant withdrawing
routes crews were currently running and there was nothing to put in their place.
Four years of a manual giving contradictory advice on the same object, knowingly.

**Confidence.** The frozen status is **G2** — the distribution is secure and sixty
years of routing on the corrected reading has behaved as the corrected reading
predicts. **That is not a reason to feel safe.** It is the same grade, resting on
the same method, that carried the literal reading for fifty-five years and was
wrong the whole time.

##### (b) `lartuki-halu` — the pair nothing turns on

```
lartuki-halu
CSC.0077(attendance?)-RECUR.TAIL

Literal parse    An origin node in the CSC.0077 state, with an edge departing
                 it to a similar but non-identical coordinate.

Attested use     Archive paraphrase, adopted 1969: the attendance-state lapsed
                 and was not resumed. No edge is claimed. No head half is
                 attested in any of the 19 occurrences, and the coordinate is
                 `hilun-nu-hilun` in every one — locus unstated, pass unordered,
                 phase unstated.
```

**Why this is non-compositional and not merely opaque.** Nothing in RECUR yields
*not resumed*. RECUR is a positive claim about a traversal — a return to a
non-identical coordinate. The pair's attested use asserts the *absence* of a
resumption, which is a different kind of thing about a different kind of object.
And the compositional reading has a cost the frozen reading does not: on the
compositional reading all 19 occurrences are broken segments; on the frozen reading
none is.

**Why it is worth having, and why it is fragile.** If the paraphrase is right, this
is the nearest the corpus comes to expressing a lapse — a relation that stopped —
rather than a graph fact. §10.2 says that nothing in the corpus does anything a
poem does; this is the closest miss, and it is a miss and not an exception.

**Nothing operational turns on it.** No expedition outcome distinguishes a true
*not resumed* from a false one, for the same structural reason that caps BIND and
`-ol` OFF at G1 (§4.2, §4.3). The paraphrase has never been validated and cannot
be. Frozen status **G1**; the paraphrase itself **G4** — one line of argument,
unreplicated, adopted in 1969 because the Archive needed something to print.

**Three-School reading.** Utterance reads a lapsed relation. Instrument reads a
monitoring process that stopped reporting — the same information with nobody in it.
Palimpsest notes that all 19 occurrences sit in one stratum and nowhere else, which
is what a form of a *different* system looks like when it is read through this
paradigm: on that account the pair is not idiomatic at all, merely foreign. The
pair is readable under all three and is admitted on that basis.

**And the Archive's style manual forbids supplying an attender.** The paraphrase
does not say who or what attended, or who or what stopped. That restraint is the
same one that holds `-sen` FRB at G3 (§4.4): naming the party would begin naming
the builders. It stays unnamed, and the gloss stays awkward because of it.

##### What the two pairs establish, and what they do not

They establish that the property is **real and instantiated**, which it was not
before. Two demonstrated pairs are worth more than eleven asserted ones, and one of
them carries a documented reversal with a date and a cost.

They do not establish language-hood, for three reasons, in order of force:

1. **A formalism can have stipulated constants.** A sentinel value is not composed
   from its parts either. The Instrument school reads `kalsira-ru` as a reserved
   constant meaning *terminus of sanction*, stipulated once and never composed —
   which is exactly what an engineer writes when a frequent condition needs a short
   form. Nothing in the data refutes this.
2. **The strongest counter to (1) is lexicalization, and it needs an ordering HEL
   does not have.** A constant is stipulated at design time and does not drift. A
   form that is compositional in one stratum (three completed occurrences in I) and
   frozen in another (24 uncompleted in II–III) has *changed*, and change of that
   kind is a language's signature and not a format's. But the argument requires
   that I precede II, and **§7.2 states that the strata do not sort by find-site
   age and that in this universe find-context is not evidence of age.** Reverse the
   order and the frozen use is original while the three completed tokens are a
   later reanalysis. Read the strata as registers and nothing changed at all —
   there is simply an archival register that writes the edge out and an operational
   one that does not.
3. **Palimpsest, briefly.** If I and II are not the same system, a form behaving
   differently across them is two forms, not one form's history.

**So property 4 moves from asserted-with-nothing to demonstrated-with-two, and its
evidential value still rests on a chronology this document has already said is
unrecoverable.** That is the honest position, and it is the position.

Bound pairs are lexical items, not paradigm values. Their grades are not counted
among the thirty-five of §4 and §5.2, on the same basis as the stratum grades in §7.

#### The scorecard

Of four properties: **one withdrawn** as non-discriminating, **one thin and
dependent** on a second, **one contested** and possibly an artifact of HEL's own
transcription, **one demonstrated** and hostage to an unrecoverable ordering. Add
prosody in Ch-4 — which §5.8 declines to attribute to the corpus rather than to
centuries of human recitation — and shadow segments (§5.5), a system that records
traversals which did not occur. **Both of those additions are weaker than they
look, and in the same way.** The prosody sits entirely in the one channel HEL
produced itself; the shadow segments are identified by pairing two G1 values that
no expedition outcome can test, in a corpus where 41% of sequences end in a broken
half, and §5.5 states the deflation the Archive has never audited for: that they
are misparsed damage and there are none.

**Net: the case that this is a language is live, weaker than it was before this
section was tested, and it has not been abandoned.** Nothing here refutes the
Utterance school; the corpus still does one thing (property 4) that formalisms do
not characteristically do. What has gone is the impression that the case was
settled. §10.2 states what is missing on the other side, and the two sections
should be read as one balance sheet.

### 10.2 Where it honestly is notation, and I will not pretend otherwise

**Not one segment in the corpus does anything a poem does.**

There is no attested way to express an interior state, a wish, a memory, an
address to another party, a comparison, a metaphor, or a judgment of value. The
disciplined position is that absence in 4,100 tokens is not absence in a language
— but *we cannot show it*, and a language you cannot show doing those things
cannot carry an elegy. If a writer sits down to write Namárië in this system,
there is nothing to write it with.

That is a real loss and it is the strongest argument against this proposal.

### 10.3 The resolution, as a design decision rather than a dodge

**First: canon forbids me to settle it, and building something readable both ways
is the harder problem, not the evasion.** Handoff §5.1 lists "a language" and
"machine control syntax" as live options and instructs me not to silently decide.
The Three-School Test (§2.2) is the mechanism that keeps both alive. If this
architecture had resolved the question in either direction, it would have violated
the canon on page one.

**Second: the emotional weight lives in the human layer, and that is legitimate,
not a consolation prize.** Tolkien's power is not carried by Quenya's semantics
alone; it is carried by the frame — an English narrator elegizing a language and a
world already lost, with the conlang as the evidence of the loss. Our frame is
different and, for this universe, stronger: centuries of people reciting a formula
they cannot translate; eighty years of argument over a plaque that cannot be
resolved because a clitic dropped out of a stratum; a redaction convention built
from the language's own word for *I do not know*; a discipline whose stratigraphy
its own physics invalidates; a warrant nobody can pronounce, said aloud by every
crew that goes out. **The pathos here is the pathos of reading under uncertainty,
and it is not a workaround — it is what HEL is.** Stewardship without mastery is
the canon's ethic, and a language you can parse but cannot understand is that
ethic's exact linguistic form.

**Third, and this is a commitment, not a hope:** the architecture must not be
allowed to be emotionally inert by construction. Per brief §4, a gap in the
in-world corpus must correspond to something we designed and withheld. So the
design bible reserves specified, structurally identified space where something
that is *not* topology can later be placed — `[41]`, the ritual-only units,
**Stratum III**, the nine bound pairs not set out at §10.1. Those are not blank;
they are the withheld chambers, and §10.1(b) is what one of them looks like when
it is opened: a pair whose attested use is a lapse rather than a graph fact,
unverifiable in principle, and admitted anyway.

**An earlier draft of this section ended here with the sentence "whether anything
goes in them is a decision for a later phase and possibly for a later
architecture." That sentence is struck.** Left standing it guaranteed the answer
was no — a chamber nobody is obliged to fill is a chamber nobody fills — and it
failed the brief on the brief's own terms, which require a withheld gap to
correspond to **something actually designed** and deliberately held back, never to
something we failed to work out. A reservation with no deliverable attached is the
second thing, wearing the first thing's clothes.

**The commitment that replaces it.** The non-topological chambers are a **Phase 2
deliverable of equal rank with the edge classes.** Not a stretch goal, not a later
architecture's problem, not contingent on how Phase 2 goes: the same phase, the
same review, the same standard of specification, and Phase 2 is not complete
without them. The largest of the four is already specified — **Stratum III is
redefined at §7 as material resolving to no topological function at all, and its
full specification is `docs/arch/A-stratum-III-reserve.md`**: membership test,
discriminator, dissolution conditions, distributional signature, authoring rules,
honesty constraint, worked example. That is 17% of the corpus, which was doing no
work whatsoever under the old definition, converted into the one place in this
design where something that is not a graph fact can be put.

**What this does and does not fix.** It does not make Path-Primary a language that
carries a lament — §10.2 stands, and the paragraph below stands. What it fixes is
the specific defect that the absence of a lament was **structural and unowned**:
there was nowhere in the design to put one, and no commitment to build anywhere.
There is now a defined location, a specification, and a phase. Whether what goes
there is any good is a question about future work rather than a question this
architecture has closed by construction, and that is the difference the brief is
asking for.

**And the honest cost, stated plainly for the decision-maker:** this architecture
will produce awe, dread, procedural beauty, and puzzle-pleasure. It will not
produce a lament. If the product needs a language a fan can fall in love with and
write poetry in, Path-Primary is insufficient on its own and should be paired with
something else — or not chosen.

## 11. What this architecture is bad at

Stated without mitigation first, mitigations second where they exist.

1. **Ordinary human content.** There is no way to say *we ate*, *he was afraid*,
   *the river was high*. Every step toward that territory is invention beyond both
   corpus and canon.
2. **Performable dialogue.** Actors cannot play this. It reads as recitation and
   procedure, because that is what it is. If the production needs alien characters
   speaking to each other, this architecture does not supply it and should not be
   stretched to.
3. **Player learnability.** The three-slot coordinate is genuinely hard. Puzzle
   players will love it; casual players will bounce off it. That is a narrow
   audience, and the audience it serves is the smallest of the three plausible
   ones.
4. **Naming — and this one is expensive.** Because roots are states rather than
   names, the architecture is *by design* incapable of generating proper names for
   nodes, artifacts, or creatures. That is correct for canon (handoff §7.5) and
   bad for product: the "memorable alien name" pipeline that merchandising, UI,
   and marketing all want has to be served from HEL's English and catalog layers
   instead. Expect friction, repeatedly, from people who are not wrong to want it.
5. **Structurally throttled vocabulary.** The grammar is rich and the lexicon is
   poor, permanently, because every new word erodes the canon-critical
   fragmentariness. This is the opposite of what most content pipelines assume.
6. **Monotony risk.** Used mechanically, every line reads node-edge-node-warrant.
   A writer with no style guidance will produce four hundred identical-sounding
   segments. This needs an enforced style guide in Phase 4 and it will still
   happen.
7. **It presses against prohibited shortcut #2.** A grammar that encodes network
   topology *this* cleanly makes the Box look more decipherable than canon wants.
   The untranslated residue, the contested glosses, and the strata are
   counterweights, but they are all **lexical**, and a lexical counterweight does
   not slow down a reader who only wants the route. **The one structural
   counterweight is §5.2b:** the PHASE magnitude is absent from every segment in
   the corpus, so a complete undamaged record is an answer key for the *shape* of
   an edge and never for the *size* of the displacement across it, and a route HEL
   has not run cannot be planned from the corpus. That is the difference between a
   decipherable Box and a legible map of a Box that stays opaque. It is not enough
   on its own: the pressure is constant and must be actively policed at every
   review, and this is still the failure mode this architecture will die of if it
   dies.
8. **The emotional peak sits on the weakest evidence.** Ch-4 is the most affecting
   material and the worst-warranted material in the corpus. That is honest and
   thematically apt, and it also means the architecture's best moments are the
   ones a skeptical reader can most easily dismiss.
9. **Two Authority-B dependencies.** The custodianship framing (§5.6) and anything
   touching the pin. If Entity Ontology v0.1 is not ratified, §5.6 degrades from
   institutional resonance to bare grammar.

### 12.1 Open question — how many channels, in how many registers?

Handoff §9.2 asks: *"Is the substrate written, acoustic, electromagnetic, spatial,
mathematical, or multimodal?"* That is **Authority D**. Below are three options
with what breaks under each, so the user can choose. **Everything in §2.3, §5.8,
§7 and Example 3 is written to option 3 and is contingent on option 3 being
selected.** The three are not exhaustive — intermediate combinations (Ch-1 + Ch-2
+ Ch-4, for instance) are available on the same terms.

**One addition this revision makes to all three options.** §5.2b's second account
of the missing PHASE magnitude — that the quantity lived in Ch-3, a register HEL
can log but cannot read — requires Ch-3, so it goes with options 1 and 2. **The
blank itself does not go.** Under any option the magnitude is absent from every
recovered segment; what changes is how many explanations HEL has for the absence.
Options 1 and 2 leave two (compartment damage, and there never was one), which is
a thinner dispute and a slightly more suspicious silence, and neither of them
restores a quantity to any record.

**Option 1 — Ch-1 only. Incised marks on recovered objects.**

- *Cosmological footprint:* **the smallest available.** The only claim is ledger
  row 2: objects bearing marks are recoverable at node loci. The network itself is
  never asserted to emit anything. Ledger row 1 is withdrawn entirely. The
  substrate is answered as "written," and no further register is claimed.
- *What breaks:* the **Palimpsest school loses its evidential base.** One medium,
  one register, one recovery method makes "two unrelated systems were consolidated
  in the 19th century" an unmotivated suspicion rather than a live reading, and the
  Three-School Test (§2.2) — this architecture's main structural defense against
  inventing the builders — degrades from a three-way standoff to a two-way one.
  Stratum IV (§7) is *defined* as the Ch-3 material and must be dropped or
  redefined, taking ~15% of the corpus and the strata's Palimpsest reading with it.
  §5.8 loses Ch-4 and with it the meter/no-meter dilemma that keeps biological
  production open. Example 3 is withdrawn. The surviving corpus is uniformly the
  best-preserved material, which makes the whole system look **more** tractable —
  pressure toward prohibited shortcut #2, from the option that looks safest.

**Option 2 — Ch-1 + Ch-4. Incised marks, plus HEL's own recited formulae.**

- *Cosmological footprint:* **identical to option 1.** Ch-4 is human material.
  Asserting that HEL recites phonologically conserved formulae says nothing about
  what the network emits. Ledger row 1 stays withdrawn.
- *What breaks:* §5.8 and Example 3 survive, so the ritual layer and the emotional
  register are kept at no cosmological cost — this is the option to choose if
  minimum cosmological commitment is the priority. But Stratum IV still goes, with
  ~15% of the corpus. The Palimpsest school is still not viable: Ch-4 is *known* to
  be human, so its divergence from Ch-1 is already explained and proves nothing
  about consolidation. And the Utterance school's strongest evidence — meter —
  would then sit entirely inside the one channel HEL produced itself, which is
  question-begging and a skeptical reader will say so.

**Option 3 — four channels as specified in §2.3. RECOMMENDED, NOT DECIDED.**

- *Cosmological footprint:* **the largest of the three, and the reason this row is
  in the cosmology-exposing table.** It asserts the network leaves recordable
  traces in three distinct physical registers: marks on objects (Ch-1), geometric
  patterns observable at loci during certain traversal states (Ch-2), and periodic
  structure recordable by instrument during traversal (Ch-3). It answers §9.2 as
  "multimodal." It narrows the space of later cosmological choices, and that cost
  is the user's to weigh — it is not a production detail.
- *Why recommended:* **Ch-2 and Ch-3 are what make the Palimpsest reading viable.**
  Ch-3's incommensurability with Ch-1 — different register, different apparatus, no
  shared physical medium, sequencing constraints the others violate — is what makes
  "these may be two unrelated systems a 19th-century committee wrongly consolidated"
  a serious standing position rather than a courtesy. Remove those two channels and
  Palimpsest is decoration. Keep them and it is the mechanism that stops any single
  reading of the corpus from ever becoming settled, which is what the Three-School
  Test runs on. Weakening one of its three legs is a canon cost, not a design cost.
- *What breaks:* nothing internal — the document is written to this option. The
  cost is entirely the cosmological commitment above.
- Approving option 3 is approving a claim about the universe. If it is not
  approved, §2.3, §5.8, §7 and Example 3 require rework before Phase 2.

Whichever option is chosen, the **proportions** (~62 / 19 / 15 / 4) are a separate
Authority C recommendation and can be reset independently.

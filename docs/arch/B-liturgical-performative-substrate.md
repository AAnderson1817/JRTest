# Architecture B — Liturgical-Performative Substrate

**Proposal ID:** B
**Phase:** 1 (canon alignment). Nothing here is approved. No dictionary is produced.
**Governing document:** `docs/00-CANON-HANDOFF.md`. Where this file and the handoff
disagree, the handoff wins.
**Target selected:** Recovered Black Box substrate (handoff §5.1, Authority C),
delivered as a coordinated package with HEL's transliteration and operational
register (handoff §9.1 recommended default).

Every claim below carries an authority level. Unlabeled claims are **Authority C**
by default — this proposal's own synthesis, submitted for approval. No claim in
this document is Authority A or B except where it cites the handoff.

## 1. The one-sentence architecture

**There is no bare proposition. Every well-formed unit is an act — opened, held by
a stated standing, scoped over an extent, and closed by a discharge condition.
Descriptions of the world exist only sealed inside an attesting act. The corpus
therefore does not tell HEL what the network is; it licenses, forbids, binds,
consigns, and releases passage through it, and HEL reconstructs the map as the
shadow of a permission structure.**

Two consequences follow immediately, and they are the reasons to consider this
proposal at all.

**First, the grammar is structurally incapable of asserting anything about
anyone.** It has no declarative clause type. The nearest thing it has — the
attesting frame — does not say *this is so*; it says *let it stand on record that
this was recorded*. Builder opacity stops being a discipline the authors must
maintain and becomes a property of the syntax. See §3.1.

**Second, it explains handoff §6.8 rather than accommodating it.** HEL's conserved
protective formulae — phonologically preserved, semantically opaque, performed in
fixed order, retained because deviation correlates with failure — are not a
cultural phenomenon that happens to reuse substrate sounds. Under this
architecture they are **degraded act frames**, and their most conspicuous
degradation (openers survive, closers do not) has a specific, frightening, and
strictly undecidable operational meaning. See §5.8 and §9, Example 2.

## 2. The organizing hypothesis, stated as a hypothesis

This document is written from inside HEL's **Act school** reading. That reading is
an interpretation, not a finding, and this architecture is built so that the two
standing rival readings remain fully available on every form. Where the document
says "act," "standing," "discharge," a Field-school analyst reads "opcode,"
"permission field," "lifetime flag," and the forms support both.

### 2.1 The three schools (Authority C)

| School | Claim | Strongest evidence for it | If it is right |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Act** | The material performs; frames are illocutionary | Obligatory standing even when uninformative (`=we`); scope ambiguity of a kind formalisms eliminate; meter in R-series; the `=lo` unstanding marker, which no format would carry | Recitation may be operative. The 1911 Remission Protocol is necessary. |
| **Field** | The material is status syntax; frames are structured records emitted by a mechanism | Slot rigidity; perfect recoverability of frame boundaries; nothing present that a resource-permission format would not need; total absence of interiority, address, or evaluation | Recitation is inert. HEL has spent a century performing a safety ritual against nothing, at real cost. |
| **Assemblage** | The Register wrongly consolidates two or more unrelated systems, and the apparent coherence of the act inventory is an artifact of nineteenth-century cataloguing | The Aberrant stratum (§8) shares ~40 elements with the rest and violates closure constraints the rest obey; the M-series does not parse as frames at all | There is no "the language." Eighty years of paradigm tables describe a filing decision. |

**Design rule — the Dual-Read Rule.** *No construction may be admitted to the
design bible unless it is coherent under both the Act and the Field reading.* A
feature that only makes sense if someone was speaking is rejected; a feature that
only makes sense if a machine was emitting is rejected. This is the mechanism that
makes inventing the builders structurally impossible rather than merely
prohibited, and it is not a defensive posture — designing something that is
simultaneously a credible performative grammar and a credible permission-record
format is harder than designing either, and the features that satisfy both
(obligatory standing, the discharge paradigm, `pii-` non-bindability) are the most
distinctive things here.

### 2.2 The builders are not touched

**The Black Box builders remain Authority D.** No species, no biology, no era, no
motive, no name, no claim that anything was ever spoken by anything.

HEL's own term of art, adopted in the Register's 1908 preface specifically to stop
analysts from writing *speaker*, is **frame-bearing material of undetermined
agency**. The corpus is material HEL found behaving in a patterned way at
thresholds. Whether a pattern has a performer is the open question, not the
premise.

**The largest builder-inference exposure in this architecture, named up front.** A
grammar of licensing, interdiction, custody and remission implies an *order of
authority*: something entitled to permit, something bound by the permission.
That is a social inference, and it is exactly the accretion failure the project
fears (benchmark §6, F1). It is hedged three ways, permanently and in the shipped
material:

1. **Field reading.** Permission bits, lock states, custody flags and lifetimes are
   what any mechanism that manages a constrained resource needs. No order of
   authority is implied by a lock.
2. **HEL-artifact reading (Ferro, from 1974).** The act inventory is an 1880s
   analysis produced by men trained in law and in liturgy, who found six illocutionary
   classes because those were the six categories they owned. The underlying opposition
   set may be much smaller and HEL may have over-differentiated it by projection.
   The re-glossing movement inside the Frame Section wants the classes renamed
   *Frame 1* through *Frame 6* and the English abandoned.
3. **Assemblage reading.** The inventory's tidiness may be a cataloguing artifact.

None of the three is dominant, all three are printed, and the shipped paradigm
tables carry both the English glosses and the neutral frame numbers side by side.

## 3. Why this and not subject–verb–object

Six arguments. Five for; the sixth is against my own proposal, and it is the one
the decision-maker should weigh hardest.

### 3.1 SVO makes every sentence a claim about someone

English cannot form a clause without a subject, and a declarative grammar's most
natural sentence is *X did Y*. Every SVO rendering of this corpus therefore
smuggles in a decision about agency — the precise Authority D question the project
must not resolve — and the pressure is continuous, sentence by sentence, forever.
A previous attempt at this project died of exactly this kind of accumulation.

This architecture removes the pressure at the root. There is no assertoric frame.
The only descriptive content in the corpus sits inside attesting frames, which
means **every proposition HEL holds is one it knows was *recorded*, not one it
knows to be *true*** — and the set of them is small, enumerable, and auditable.
The design bible can literally list every proposition the corpus contains. That is
a governance property no descriptive architecture can offer.

### 3.2 The four negations are not four kinds of "not true"

Handoff §7.3 asks for impossible / forbidden / unavailable / unverified. A
truth-conditional grammar must encode all four as modal operators over
propositions, which is why English does it so badly and needs a paraphrase for
each. A validity-conditional grammar gets them at **four different structural
levels**: non-bindability of the extent, an interdiction in force, a lapsed or
unmet license, and absent standing. See §7. The categories are not competing
members of one paradigm; they do not even sit in the same part of the word. That
is what makes translation into English demonstrably lossy rather than merely
awkward (benchmark §4.10).

### 3.3 Spacetime-as-one-coordinate falls out instead of being stipulated

Acts take **events** as their arguments — a crossing, an arrival, a departure. An
event has no separable where and when. The grammar therefore has **no locative
expression and no temporal expression at all**, not because they were merged into
a composite slot but because there is nothing for them to modify. A bare temporal
expression is not merely misleading here; it is ungrammatical, because a year is
not an event and cannot be bound.

Prohibited shortcut #6 — "imply that a year alone identifies a destination" — is
therefore not avoided by care. It is *underivable*. See §6.2.

### 3.4 The corpus we would actually have is performative

This is a taphonomic argument and it is the one that most persuades me. Real
epigraphic corpora skew violently toward performatives: boundary markers, curse
tablets, dedications, laws, oaths, treaty preambles, foundation deposits. They do
so for a mechanical reason — the texts that get inscribed in durable material at
thresholds are the texts that *govern passage*, because that is what a threshold
is for.

HEL's access to this material is precisely at thresholds: loci where traversal has
been observed to begin, to end, or to fail (§4). HEL has excavated a network's
worth of doorframes and has no houses. Under a descriptive architecture you must
explain why a corpus of route metadata contains almost no route metadata. Under
this one the distribution is what the find-context predicts, and the shape of the
gap is itself informative (benchmark Design rule 1).

### 3.5 Performatives are falsifiable; descriptions of an incomprehensible system are not

A description of an unintelligible mechanism cannot be checked. **A permission
can.** If a frame licenses an outbound crossing and the crossing is traversable, that
is confirmation from the world rather than from a translation key — the canon-safe
form of the tripod tablet (benchmark §4.2), and it discloses nothing about the
builders, because knowing that a string licensed a crossing you have made tells you
nothing about who wrote it.

This is a genuine advantage of B over any descriptive architecture, and it is
where the corpus's verifiable footholds come from. It is also dangerous, and §11.7
says why.

### 3.6 Against myself: SVO — or a topological notation — is better at navigation

Route operations want metadata: *this edge leads there; arrival displaces by
roughly this much; this junction has four outbound edges.* A performative grammar
does not carry that. It carries permissions, and topology has to be inferred from
the permission structure the way a road network is inferred from toll charters —
lossily, slowly, and with systematic blind spots exactly where nothing was ever
licensed or forbidden.

This is the architecture's central operational liability, and §11.1 develops it
without mitigation before offering any. If the product needs the alien text to
*be* the map — if players compute routes from inscriptions — this is the wrong
proposal and Architecture A is the right one.

## 4. Speaker/user model and evidence corpus

### 4.1 The Register

**Designation (Authority C, production-only):** the **Register of Attested
Frames** (RAF), maintained by the Archive's Frame Section; consolidated from
branch registers in 1908; transliteration standard fixed 1883; revised 1961.

Frame-level custody sits with the Frame Section. The **Office of the Precentor** —
a nineteenth-century HEL title retained because the office's job is the recitation
standard — owns layer 3, the spoken approximation, and the two offices have not
agreed since 1961.

**Scale (Authority C, production-only, D-resolved).** These numbers exist so that
"fragmentary" is a specified quantity rather than an alibi.

- ~5,200 catalogued element tokens
- ~1,180 identifiable frames, of which ~700 are complete (opener and closer both present)
- ~340 distinct recurring elements; ~95 occur more than five times
- productive analyzable core: ~70 elements
- longest complete frame: 23 elements; deepest attested nesting: 4
- extent-designating elements with a proposed gloss: 31. Extent-designating elements
  with **no** proposed gloss that survived review: 106.
- **The corpus does not grow on demand.** New material enters only by recovery on
  expedition. Lexicon expansion is a diegetic event with a date, a field report,
  and a named recovering officer.

Deliberately Linear B / Etruscan scale: enough to recover a grammar, never enough
to recover a lexicon.

### 4.2 Series, and why each exists

- **T-series — Threshold marks (~57%).** Marks on recovered hard material found at
  loci where traversal has been observed to begin, to end, or to fail. Best
  preserved; overwhelmingly complete frames; short. This is the bulk of the corpus
  and the reason the corpus is performative.
- **S-series — Sequenced emission (~21%).** Patterned output recorded by instrument
  during traversal states. Frame boundaries are positionally recoverable; element
  identity frequently is not. Whether S-series belongs to the same system as
  T-series is the central Assemblage question. **Nothing in this architecture
  attributes S-series to precursor entities. Entity communicative capacity is
  Authority D and is out of scope (prohibited shortcut #13).**
- **R-series — Recitation (~14%).** HEL's own conserved oral formulae, transmitted
  through human mouths for an undetermined period, first written down in branch
  notebooks in 1874. Evidentially the worst material in the corpus and the only
  material with meter. The emotional core, and the weakest evidence — which is
  honest, and which means the architecture's best moments are the ones a skeptic
  can most easily dismiss (§11.8).
- **M-series — Marginal (~8%).** Material co-occurring on objects that also bear
  frames, but which **does not parse as frames at all**. Either a second system, or
  damage, or the interior HEL does not have. This is the Assemblage school's home
  ground and it is also the architecture's **designed reserve**: the structurally
  identified space where non-performative language could later be placed, per brief
  §4's requirement that a published gap correspond to something withheld rather
  than something unbuilt. Nothing is placed there in Phase 1.

### 4.3 Condition, and the absence of a bilingual

Fragmentary, damaged, mixed provenance, **and without a bilingual.** There is no
Rosetta object and this architecture does not permit one.

Confirmation proceeds by **operational correlation**: an interdiction frame over an
extent HEL has independently observed to be non-traversable; a license frame over a
crossing HEL has made. The correlation is imperfect and HEL keeps the exceptions in
print: licensed crossings that failed (14 recorded), and — the category that
frightens the Archive — **interdicted crossings traversed without apparent
incident (6 recorded, most recently 1957)**. The Archive's style manual forbids
rendering a correlation-warranted reading with the word *because*.

### 4.4 What the gaps are caused by (benchmark Design rule 1)

Every published gap has a stated in-world cause:

| Gap | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| No narrative, no interiority, no evaluation | HEL holds thresholds, not interiors. The find-context selects for texts that govern passage. |
| Tiny extent lexicon (31 glossed of 137) | Extent elements designate crossings. HEL cannot fix a referent for a crossing it has not made, and it has made few. |
| Discharge unrecoverable in ~24% of frames | The Fused stratum marks discharge by vowel length alone, and the 1883 Table's length assignments are contested (§6). |
| No bilingual, ever | None is possible; there is no second party to the record. |
| M-series unparsed | Either a second system or the missing interior. Held open. |

## 5. Core structure

Notation: `-` affix boundary · `=` clitic boundary · `⟨ ⟩` HEL's scholarly frame
brackets, an editorial convention of 1908, not present in any source · `⟦ ⟧`
catalog identifier for an element with no gloss.

### 5.1 The frame

```
⟨ OPENER=STANDING   [ SCOPE ]   CLOSER-DISCHARGE ⟩
```

The opener and the closer are **two halves of one discontinuous morpheme**. They
agree in vowel: the closer echoes the opener's vowel. Everything between them is
under the act's force. Frames nest.

This bracket structure is where the architecture earns its keep:

- **A frame with no closer is not discharged.** In damaged material that is
  ambiguous between physical loss and design, and HEL has a category for each.
- **Nesting is scope.** An interdiction inside a license is a carve-out; a license
  inside an interdiction is an exception; an attestation inside a bind is a recital
  of the condition being held. Depth 4 is attested.
- **Recitation must complete.** An interrupted recitation is a physically
  well-formed opened act. Whether that matters is the architecture's central
  undecidable (§9.2). It is also, without further assumption, the exact reason a
  procedure would be conserved in fixed order and never abbreviated — handoff §6.8
  derived rather than decorated.

### 5.2 Act classes (Authority C)

| Class | HEL gloss | Neutral name | Opener | Closer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **LIC** | license — makes a passage permitted | Frame 1 | `ne-` | `-ne` |
| **INT** | interdict — makes a passage not permitted | Frame 2 | `ma-` | `-ma` |
| **BND** | bind — holds a condition in force | Frame 3 | `ku-` | `-ku` |
| **ATT** | attest — places content on record | Frame 4 | `si-` | `-si` |
| **REM** | remit — discharges a prior act | Frame 5 | `ro-` | `-ro` |
| **CON** | consign — places under care | Frame 6 | `lu-` | `-lu` |
| **⟦F-7⟧** | *none assigned* | Frame 7 | `wee-` | `-wee` |

**⟦F-7⟧ is the no-gloss zone** (benchmark §4.10). A seventh opener/closer pair
occurring in R-series recitation and on nine T-series objects, for which no act
assignment has survived review. Field personnel call it *the seventh*. Its
existence is not secure: see §6.3, where an entire act class turns out to hang on a
transliteration decision made in 1883 by a man writing down what he heard people
chant.

### 5.3 Standing — the obligatory category English lacks

Every opener carries a standing clitic. **There is no bare opener.** Standing
answers not *how do I know* but *on what basis may this act be performed*.

| | Name | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `=ta` | **SEATED** | standing holds at the extent itself; not conferred by any antecedent act |
| `=ke` | **VESTED** | standing conferred by a prior act, identifiable in the record |
| `=nu` | **HELD** | standing by repetition — the act has been performed thus and held |
| `=hi` | **WITNESSED** | standing by presence at the extent |
| `=lo` | **UNSTANDING** | overt: the act is performed *and the performer is not entitled* |
| `=we` | **NULL** | this record does not establish standing |

`=lo` is the strangest morpheme in the design and the most productive. A
grammaticalized way to say *I am doing this and I may not*. Attested 11 times, all
T-series, none in recitation. What an unstanding act does is §9.2 and is not
resolved.

**The standing/evidence conflation, and why it is the architecture's best
argument.** VESTED, HELD and WITNESSED look exactly like inferential, repetitive
and direct evidentials, and HEL read them that way from 1883 to 1961. They are not
evidentials — or rather, HEL cannot show that they are not, and the dispute is
live. Handoff §6.4's demand that evidentiality be central is met by a paradigm that
is *obligatory, structural, and about something subtly other than knowledge*,
which is a harder and more alien thing than an evidential system and which
produces a century of argument rather than a tidy table. See §6.4.

### 5.4 Discharge — what replaces tense and aspect

Every closer carries a discharge suffix. Discharge states when the act stops being
in force. **There is no tense.** An act is not past or future; it is spent,
standing, conditioned, lapsed, or unspecified.

| | Name | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `-tu` | **SPENT** | discharged on performance; single use |
| `-ma` | **STANDING** | in force until remitted by a REM act |
| `-hu` | **CONDITIONED** | in force while the condition stated in scope holds |
| `-ra` | **LAPSED** | in force until the extent is completed |
| `-we` | **NULL** | this record does not state a discharge |

`-ra` is worth noting: "this license is good until you arrive" is one morpheme.
Route completion (handoff §6.3) is a discharge condition, not a description.

**The Open Register.** Nineteen frames in the corpus are `-ma` STANDING with no
attested remission. The Archive maintains a register of them. Their scopes are
partly readable and their extents are, in fourteen cases, elements HEL has never
identified. HEL knows that nineteen acts were placed in force and cannot say over
what. Whether an act performed by undetermined agency at an undetermined time can
still be in force is not a question HEL can answer, and HEL does not operate as
though the answer is no.

Ferro's published position on the Open Register, printed in the Register's front
matter since 1979 at the Archive's insistence: *"Nineteen strings whose closing
strings are missing. That is a statement about our shelves."*

### 5.5 Scope: the extent

Scope contains an **extent** — the event the act binds — and, in ATT and CON
frames, additional arguments.

```
[ORIENT-] ROOT [-REACH] [-RECUR]
```

**ORIENT** — how the crossing is taken. Obligatory. This is where one-way topology
becomes constitutive:

| | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `wa-` | **OUT** | the crossing viewed from the departing side |
| `he-` | **IN** | the crossing viewed from the arriving side |
| `ru-` | **THROUGH** | an intermediate leg of a forced multi-crossing path |
| `mo-` | **AGAIN** | a crossing returning to a coordinate similar to, and not identical with, a prior one |

**ROOT** designates the crossing **by the condition obtaining at it**, not by a
name. Consequence, and it is large: **the substrate contains no proper names for
places, nodes, or objects.** The same crossing bears different roots in different
records. HEL cannot build a gazetteer from the corpus, which is why the operational
layer runs on catalog identifiers and English field names. Handoff §7.5's caution —
do not assume every place has a true name waiting to be translated — is here a
structural property.

**REACH** — how far the act's force extends. This is where loop topology enters the
grammar:

| | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `-na` | **THIS** | this crossing only |
| `-li` | **JUNCTION** | every crossing attaching at this junction |
| `-ho` | **CYCLE** | every crossing on the closed loop containing this one |
| `-ti` | **VESTED-SET** | whatever set a prior act established |

`-ti` grammaticalizes **path dependence**: the scope of this act is determined by
the history of prior acts and cannot be computed from the present record alone. A
`-ti`-scoped frame is unreadable without its antecedent, and in 22 attested cases
the antecedent is not in the corpus. Those frames are readable, complete,
well-formed, and their scope is permanently unknown.

**RECUR** — obligatory, and operationally the highest-stakes morpheme in the
language:

| | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `-mu` | **ONCE** | the act bites at this crossing, this pass |
| `-mii` | **EVERY** | the act bites at every recurrence of a similar-but-not-identical coordinate |
| `-we` | **NULL** | the record does not state |

English cannot mark this. "This route is closed" does not distinguish *closed on
this pass* from *closed on every pass forever*, and an expedition's life depends on
which. Every English rendering of a `-mu`/`-mii` frame is provably lossy, and HEL's
style manual requires the residue to be printed under the translation.

### 5.6 The null element

`we` occupies three slots — standing, discharge, and recurrence — with one meaning:
**this record does not establish it.** It is the most frequent element in the
corpus. HEL identified it distributionally, before it had any gloss, purely from
positional behaviour: an element that appears in three unrelated slots and nowhere
else.

Then HEL adopted `we` as its own **redaction mark**, which was a catastrophe nobody
noticed for forty years. A redacted transcript of a frame is now formally
indistinguishable from an attestation of `we`. The Frame Section's 1961 audit found
that an unknown number of `we` tokens in the Register are HEL's own censorship, and
could not determine which. **The corpus has been contaminated by the institution
studying it**, and the contamination is unrecoverable because the clearance records
that would resolve it are themselves sealed.

## 6. Canon pressures, one by one

### 6.1 Directed motion (handoff §6.1)

Each required distinction has a distinct exponent, and none is optional:

| Canon requirement | Exponent |
| --- | --- |
| motion permitted by the network | LIC frame over the crossing |
| hypothetical reverse motion | **`pii-`** — see below |
| arrival at a node | `he-` IN |
| departure from a node | `wa-` OUT |
| traversal through an intermediate node | `ru-` THROUGH |
| recurrence at a similar but not identical coordinate | `mo-` AGAIN, and independently `-mii` in scope |

There is no generic "go." There is no motion verb at all — motion is not predicated,
it is licensed or interdicted.

**One-wayness is encoded by non-bindability, and this is the most alien thing in the
design.** The substrate has no way to say *you cannot go back*. `pii-` prefixed to an
extent marks it **not a bindable extent** — there is nothing there for an act to take
hold of. A reverse-oriented crossing that does not exist in the network is not
forbidden and not impossible-to-traverse; it is *not the kind of thing that can be
licensed or interdicted*, the way a promise cannot be made about a colour.

Prohibited shortcut #5 — grammar erasing the one-way topology — is unreachable
here: you cannot form a frame over a reverse edge without either `pii-` (conceding
it is unbindable) or committing to its existence and being wrong in a way the
corpus's operational correlation will expose.

### 6.2 Spacetime as one coordinate (handoff §6.2)

Derived in §3.3: acts bind events; events are unitary; the grammar has no locative
and no temporal expression.

The canon's specific distinctions map as follows:

| Canon distinction | How it is expressed |
| --- | --- |
| same place, different time | two extents sharing a ROOT, differing in ORIENT or in the frame's vesting chain — **not** expressible as "same place" |
| same time, different place | **not expressible.** There is no time term to hold constant. HEL treats this as evidence for the architecture and Ferro treats it as evidence against. |
| same route node, different traversal | one ROOT, distinct `-mu` ONCE frames; or one frame `-mii` EVERY |
| predicted destination | LIC frame, `-hu` CONDITIONED — permitted if the condition holds |
| observed destination | ATT frame with `=hi` WITNESSED standing |
| intended destination | **not expressible.** Intention is an interior state; the grammar has none. |
| origin- / traveler- / archive-relative time | **not expressible.** HEL's own three-way relativity lives entirely in its English annotation layer, where it was invented in 1931 and is HEL's, not the corpus's. |

Three "not expressibles" in one table is not a shortfall; it is the engineered
untranslatability the benchmark asks for (§4.10), and it runs in the unusual
direction — the *source* lacks categories the *target* has. That produces the
opposite of the normal translation residue: HEL must constantly add information to
render a frame into English, and every addition is HEL's invention, and the style
manual requires them to be bracketed. A HEL translation is visibly more
informative than its original, which is the clearest possible demonstration that
it is not a translation.

### 6.3 Loop topology (handoff §6.3)

- **sequence, adjacency** — the vesting chain (§6.5)
- **junction** — REACH `-li`
- **convergence** — `-li` on an `he-` IN extent: every crossing arriving here
- **recurrence** — `mo-` AGAIN, `-mii` EVERY
- **inaccessible reverse edges** — `pii-` non-bindability
- **nested cycles** — a `-ho` CYCLE-scoped frame nested inside a `-ho` CYCLE-scoped
  frame. Attested at depth 2. What a cycle inside a cycle is, physically, HEL does
  not claim to know; the grammar presupposes it.
- **path dependence** — REACH `-ti` VESTED-SET
- **route completion** — discharge `-ra` LAPSED

Note that the grammar **presupposes** cycles rather than describing them: `-ho`
cannot be used without committing to the existence of a closed loop containing the
crossing. Authority A canon says loops exist; the grammar treats that as background,
which is what a grammar does with things its users take for granted. This is
evidence HEL cites for the corpus being *about* this network and not another, and
it is the strongest thing HEL has, and it is not proof.

### 6.4 Evidentiality and uncertainty (handoff §6.4)

Handled at two layers, deliberately not merged.

**In the substrate:** the standing paradigm (§5.3), obligatory, structural, and
about entitlement rather than knowledge — with the conflation live. The corpus's
own uncertainty markers are `=we` and `-we` (the record does not establish) and
`⟦ ⟧` catalog citation for elements with no gloss.

**In HEL's annotation layer:** Reading Grades I–V, applied to every published
reading and printed with it.

| Grade | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **I** | frame secure, content secure — all elements in the analyzable core |
| **II** | frame secure, content contested — at least one extent root glossed provisionally |
| **III** | frame secure, content unrecoverable — extent elements unglossed; the frame is read and the item is not |
| **IV** | frame contested — segmentation or stratum assignment disputed |
| **V** | deprecated — a reading formerly relied upon, retired by identified evidence, retained in print |

Grade V is not a wastebasket. Handoff §3.6 requires the archive to contain
deprecated readings, and benchmark §4.8 requires the reading history to be a
first-class deliverable. Every Grade V entry carries the analyst, the date, the
method, and **the specific evidence that killed it**. Retiring at least one reading
per phase is a standing obligation of this architecture.

The handoff's finest category — "ritually preserved without known meaning" — is
Grade III applied to R-series material, which is most of R-series.

### 6.5 Causality under constraint (handoff §6.5)

**The grammar has no causal connective.** It cannot say *because*. What it has is
`=ke` VESTED: this act's standing derives from that act. That is a chain of
*authorization*, not of causation, and the distinction is enforced by HEL's style
manual (a `=ke`-warranted reading may not be rendered with *because*; field
analysts break the rule under deadline and the Frame Section catches it in review).

This produces the architecture's best structural payoff. A vesting chain is a
partial ordering internal to the corpus — an attested relative sequence,
independent of find-context. It is a genuine philological gift, of the kind real
decipherers would trade a limb for.

And then:

- **Three chains are closed.** Act A is vested by act B, which is vested by act A.
  In an ordinary system of authorizations this is a defect. In a network of
  one-directional interlocking loops it may be exactly well-formed. HEL cannot tell,
  and cannot design an experiment that would tell, because both the alleged cause
  and the alleged effect are already in the archive.
- The canon's harder cases map directly: *cause inferred across a loop* is a closed
  vesting; *an action whose consequence precedes the traveler's departure frame* is a
  vesting whose antecedent is later by every other measure (§8.2); *a precaution
  taken because of possible loop effects* is the 1911 Remission Protocol; *a
  prohibited action with unknown consequences* is an INT frame whose interdicted
  crossings have, six times, been traversed without apparent incident.
- *A stable correlation HEL refuses to call causal* is the `=nu` HELD standing —
  "performed thus and held" — which HEL's own house rule forbids rendering causally,
  and which is the standing borne by nearly all R-series recitation.

Nothing here settles the physics, and nothing here needs to.

### 6.6 Stewardship rather than mastery (handoff §6.6)

**The grammar has no possessive construction.** Not a disfavoured one — none. Custody
is not a relation; it is an act in a discharge state.

| English | Structure |
| --- | --- |
| possession | *inexpressible* |
| temporary custody | CON frame, discharge `-ra` LAPSED or `-hu` CONDITIONED |
| authorized access | LIC frame, standing `=ke` VESTED |
| responsibility for care | CON frame, discharge `-ma` STANDING — held until remitted |
| containment without ownership | CON frame nesting an INT frame — consigned, and passage over it interdicted |
| observation without interference | ATT frame with no enclosing LIC — recorded and not licensed |

The ethical charge is structural: **to take custody you must state the condition on
which you will give it up.** A CON frame without a discharge is not a stronger claim,
it is Open Register material.

This is the closest the corpus comes to something a human can feel directly, and
the closest thing the architecture has to an elegiac form is a **REM frame that
cannot be delivered** — a remission of a custody whose ward the record can no longer
identify. Two are attested. That is thin, and §11.3 says so.

**Authority B dependency, flagged.** Handoff §4.3 (pin as custodial credential) and
§4.4 (Entity Ontology v0.1) are Working canon whose final ratification was not
recovered. §10, Example 3 proposes a shape for the pin's authorization formula at
Authority C. If the custodianship model is not ratified, that proposal falls and
the CON class degrades from institutional resonance to bare grammar.

**Prohibited shortcut #13, held.** Nothing here establishes an entity language, and
the standing paradigm encodes nothing about the *nature* of a standing-holder. HEL
cannot determine whether any attested standing refers to a person, a position, a
location, a system state, or a route — which is handoff §5.3's list, unresolved on
purpose. The Ontology office's reading that a ward can bear standing (and therefore
that the grammar has a slot for an entity with agency) is recorded in the Register
as an over-reading, and the Frame Section rejects it.

### 6.7 Secrecy and compartmentalization (handoff §6.7)

Secrecy in a performative universe is not about who may *know*. It is about who may
*do*, and speaking is doing. That is a genuinely different logic and it is
architecture-native:

- **Clearance is standing.** HEL's own clearances are structured as `=ke` VESTED
  chains, traceable to an antecedent authorization, with a discharge condition —
  the institution modelling itself on its object of study, which is what happens to
  institutions that study one thing for a long time.
- **Recitation is restricted.** Because a frame may be operative, HEL restricts *who
  may say what, where*. The Precentor's Standing Order lists eleven frames that may
  not be spoken within a defined distance of an active locus by anyone below a
  stated grade. Whether this restriction does anything is unknown. It is enforced.
- **Redaction uses the corpus's own morpheme** (`we`, §5.6), with the consequences
  described there.
- **Cover terms.** Cable traffic refers to items by frame class: *a Frame 2 item*,
  *a seventh item*. Externally meaningless, internally exact, and deliberately
  incomplete — a Frame 2 item is an interdiction and the cable does not say of what.
- **Names that outlived their explanations.** The Office of the Precentor is named
  for a duty (leading recitation) that the 1961 revision argued is founded on a
  misreading. The office kept the name. HEL keeps names.

### 6.8 Ritual without confirmed mysticism (handoff §6.8)

This is the architecture's home ground, and the mechanism is specific rather than
atmospheric.

**The degradation is directional and rule-governed.** Closers are frame-final,
unstressed in HEL's own recitation convention, and carry the discharge suffix. In
oral transmission, frame-final unstressed material is what goes. The R-series is
therefore **systematically opener-heavy**: 41 openers attested against 12 closers
across the whole recited corpus.

The consequence, stated flatly: **every time a HEL crew recites the departure
formula, they open frames and close few of them.** What that means is §9.2 and is
undecidable.

Handoff §6.8's five requirements are met by construction rather than by
description:

| Requirement | Mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| phonologically conserved | recitation preserves segments; the 1883 Table was built from the recitation |
| semantically opaque | Grade III — frame read, extent elements unglossed |
| performed in a fixed order | frames nest; nesting is scope; reordering changes what is inside what |
| retained because deviation correlates with failure | an abbreviated recitation leaves frames open; HEL's records show a correlation and the Archive refuses to call it causal (§6.5) |
| analyzed differently by scientists, archivists, and field personnel | Act / Field / Assemblage, three offices, one budget |

**And prohibited shortcut #19 is held in both directions.** The architecture does
not establish that recitation does anything (Field school: it is inert, and the
Protocol is a hundred years of wasted breath). It does not establish that recitation
does nothing (Act school: the Open Register is growing, and we are the ones
growing it). The 1911 Remission Protocol requires that every recitation be followed
by the Archive's **reconstructed** remission formula — a scholarly conjecture,
graded IV, said aloud as a safeguard by people who know it is a conjecture.

That is the image this universe is made of: a hypothesis recited as a prayer, by
people who could tell you its grade.

## 7. Four-way negation

The four negations sit at **four different structural levels**, which is the
architecture's answer to handoff §7.3 and the mechanical source of its
untranslatability.

| Canon category | Level | Exponent | Reading | What HEL may **not** infer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **impossible** | property of the extent | `pii-` | the extent is not a bindable thing; there is nothing here for an act to hold | that the network *prevents* it. `pii-` says the crossing is not the kind of thing that takes an act, not that it was tried. |
| **forbidden** | act class | INT frame in force | an interdiction is standing over this extent | **by whom or by what.** Reading INT as "forbidden *by someone*" is HEL's English. The Field school reads it as *gated*. Resolving this would begin naming an authority. It stays open. |
| **unavailable** | discharge state | LIC frame `-ra` LAPSED, or `-hu` CONDITIONED with the condition unmet | permission existed and does not now obtain | whether the lapse is periodic, conditional, or terminal |
| **unverified** | standing | `=we` NULL, or the absence of any ATT frame over the content | the record does not establish it | anything. This is the honest one. |

Three consequences worth stating:

1. **These are not alternants.** They cannot be substituted for one another, because
   they do not occupy the same position. English's single *not* collapses four
   structurally unrelated things, which is why every English rendering of a negative
   frame requires a paraphrase and why HEL's style manual runs to nine pages on the
   subject.
2. **A fifth appears in English and HEL had to name it.** A remitted act — permission
   that existed, was released, and now does not obtain — renders into English as
   "no" and is none of the four. The 1974 style revision added **REMITTED** to the
   annotation vocabulary after a field incident in which a remitted license was
   logged as unavailable and a crew waited eleven hours for it to come back.
3. **Only one of the four is a morpheme.** If a design produces four tidy negation
   affixes, it has built a paradigm. A real system distributes the work across levels
   and leaves the seams showing.

## 8. Strata: where the depth comes from

Four strata, defined by **systematic formal difference** (Authority C). The
differences are rule-governed, so a reader who has seen two examples can predict
the third — benchmark §1.1's requirement that difference be *stateable*, because
difference without a rule reads as sloppiness and difference with a rule reads as
history.

| Stratum | Rule | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Full** | Opener + standing + closer + discharge, every frame. Nesting to depth 3–4. Standing always overt, including `=we`. | ~22% |
| **Contracted** | The closer loses its consonant: `-ma` → `-a`, `-ne` → `-e`. Standing omitted when SEATED. No nesting beyond depth 2. | ~39% |
| **Fused** | No closer at all. Discharge is carried by **length on the opener vowel**: `ne-` LIC.SPENT vs. `nee-` LIC.STANDING. CONDITIONED and LAPSED are **not distinguishable** in this stratum. | ~24% |
| **Aberrant** | Openers present, closers absent *systematically* — on physically intact objects. Standing markers occur in positions the other three strata disallow. | ~15% |

**Full → Contracted → Fused is textbook grammaticalization**: a discontinuous
morpheme collapsing inward until it is an affix and a vowel length. Anyone who has
watched a circumfix die will recognize it. That is the chronological reading.

But the Fused stratum **loses information**: CONDITIONED and LAPSED merge. So HEL
cannot determine, for a quarter of its corpus, whether an interdiction expires on
arrival or persists while a condition holds. Archive policy is to treat all Fused
interdictions as standing. That is a policy, not a finding, and it is printed as
one.

### 8.1 The four readings, all live

1. **Chronological.** Full → Contracted → Fused, with Aberrant either archaic or a
   late collapse.
2. **Register.** Full is archival, Contracted is operational shorthand, Fused is a
   compressed marking format, Aberrant is something else.
3. **Functional.** Aberrant is not degraded at all: it is the genre of **deliberately
   unclosed acts** — perpetual bindings, written open on purpose. Which would mean
   the Open Register is not an accident but a category, and that HEL has been
   cataloguing as damage a class of text whose whole point is that it never ends.
4. **Assemblage.** Aberrant is a different system.

None can be eliminated on 5,200 tokens. The evidence that would discriminate reading
3 from reading 1 is a single Aberrant object bearing a closer; none has been
recovered; the Archive has three times authorized recovery efforts specifically for
one.

### 8.2 The stratigraphic scandal

The vesting chains (§6.5) give HEL an internal relative chronology. It **contradicts
the formal stratigraphy.**

Fused-stratum acts vest Full-stratum acts. The morphologically latest material
authorizes the morphologically earliest. Under any ordinary assumption that is
impossible.

Three standing responses:

1. The vesting references are not chronological — `=ke` cites a *type* of act, not a
   token, and HEL has been reading a typology as a sequence since 1908.
2. The formal stratigraphy is not chronological — the strata are registers, and
   there is no ordering to recover.
3. The corpus records a system in which authorization does not run in our sequence.
   This is derived entirely from Authority A canon: one-directional interlocking
   loops, consequence preceding departure frame. **The Archive forbids publishing it
   as a finding and concedes in print that it cannot be excluded.**

HEL therefore possesses a hundred-year-old philological method — relative
chronology from internal cross-reference — that its own physics may invalidate, and
it keeps using the method because it has nothing better. That is where this
architecture buys diachronic depth without a family tree and without naming anyone:
the corpus has real, systematic, rule-governed internal variation and **no
recoverable ordering**, which is the actual epistemic situation of Etruscan and
Linear A, and which is richer than Tolkien's model in exactly one respect — the
reconstruction is contested *inside the fiction*, permanently, by people with
careers.

### 8.3 The reading history is a deliverable

Named, dated, and falsifiable (benchmark Design rule 5).

- **1874** — branch notebooks first record the recitations. Compilers unidentified.
- **1883 — Halloran's Precentor's Table.** Transliteration fixed, for singing. §9.1.
- **1908** — branch registers consolidated into the RAF. The preface introduces
  *frame-bearing material of undetermined agency* and forbids *speaker*.
- **1911 — Deverell's Remission Protocol**, after the Kinzie Street incident. Every
  recitation to be followed by the Archive's reconstructed remission. Graded IV then
  and IV now.
- **1931** — the three-way time relativity (origin- / traveler- / archive-relative)
  invented for the annotation layer. HEL's, not the corpus's; the Register has
  reminded readers of this in a footnote on every printing since.
- **1961 — Okonjo's Fusion Revision.** §9.1. The single greatest result in the
  field's history, and the cause of its worst decade.
- **1974 — Ferro** publishes the Field reading and the projection argument. REMITTED

- **1979** — Ferro's line on the Open Register enters the front matter, over the

## 9. Human-facing layers

The four layers of brief §7 stay visibly distinct in every document. Layer 3 is
**HEL's**, and this architecture is built to keep anyone from quietly promoting it
into a reconstruction of alien speech (handoff §5.3; benchmark §4.13).

### 9.1 Transliteration inventory (Authority C)

**The phonetic form of this material is HEL's.** Halloran's 1883 Table was compiled
for one purpose: to let the Precentor's office standardize a recitation that was
already being performed and was drifting between branches. It was optimized for
**sustained vocalization and syllable-counting under stress**, not for accuracy,
which nobody claimed then and nobody claims now.

- **Consonants (11):** p t k m n s h l r w y
- **Vowels (5):** a e i o u; length written by doubling (`aa ee ii oo uu`)
- **Syllable:** strictly (C)V or (C)VV. **No codas. No clusters. No geminates.**
- Every element is therefore open-syllabled and mora-countable, which is the whole
  point: a Precentor can count a frame while singing it, and frame-count errors are
  the fatal error class (§9.3).
- **No voiced obstruents**, not for effect but because the 1883 committee was
  standardizing across branches whose recitations disagreed about voicing and chose
  to record the disagreement as unresolved rather than to arbitrate it.
- **Elements with no assigned value** are cited by catalog number: `⟦E-31⟧`,
  `⟦F-7⟧`. Some of the corpus is unpronounceable on purpose, because HEL does not
  know.

**No real-world language supplied phonological material, texture, rhythm, or
"flavor" for this inventory.** The set follows from the recitation-and-counting
rationale and from nothing else. This is a hard constraint (prohibited shortcut
#14), and the in-world rationale exists precisely so that no one is ever tempted to
reach for a real one.

**The length controversy, and why it is load-bearing.** Halloran used doubled vowels
to mark positions where his committee judged that *two elements were present and
could not be separated* — a bound they could not cut. Length in the Table is
therefore, on its face, **a record of HEL's uncertainty, not a recovered contrast.**

Then, in 1961, Okonjo showed that in one large class of material the "extra
element" Halloran could not cut is the **collapsed closer** — that Fused-stratum
length is a real morphological exponent, and that Halloran had recorded something
true without knowing what it was. It is the field's Kober moment: a distributional
observation made blind, vindicated eighty years later by a structural argument.
It is also the corpus's cleanest **verifiable foothold** (benchmark §4.2), because
Okonjo's analysis predicted where length should and should not occur in material
not yet examined, and the prediction held.

And then the decade after: the Fusion analysis was over-applied to everything,
including R-series, and about half the length marks in non-Fused material are now
back in dispute. Success bred error. **The most consequential casualty is `wee-`
versus `we`:** if `wee-` is Fused-stratum length, ⟦F-7⟧ does not exist as an act
class and the departure recitation has no seventh frame. If Halloran was recording
two elements, it does. Unresolved. **The existence of an entire act class rests on
a transliteration decision made by a man writing down what he heard people chant.**

### 9.2 HEL field pronunciation

- **Stress:** first syllable of the opener and first syllable of the closer; level
  through the scope. Chosen by the Precentor's office for countability. Not
  recovered.
- **Long vowels:** Chicago Reading Room recites 2:1 duration; the Halsted tradition
  rearticulates with a pitch step. Nothing rides on it. Crews can tell where you
  trained inside one frame, and say so.
- **Closers are unstressed and frame-final**, which is why they are the first thing
  lost in oral transmission (§6.8) — the architecture's degradation model and its
  pronunciation convention are the same fact.

### 9.3 The count-back

Because a frame that is opened must be closed, HEL's field checksum is a **frame
count**, not a word count. Three turns, always, even in the same room.

> **A.** *Three frames. Ma=hi, ne=ke, si=we. Closed, closed, open.*
> **B.** Three frames — interdict witnessed, license vested, attest null. Two closed,
> one open. Which is open?
> **A.** The attestation. Open by the card, not by damage.
> **B.** Copy. Three frames, one open, by the card.

"Open by the card, not by damage" is field shorthand for *the source object is
intact and the frame is unclosed by design* — the distinction that decides whether
an item goes to the Frame Section or to the Open Register.

### 9.4 English gloss layer

Operational translations — *interdicted outbound*, *license lapsed on arrival*,
*consigned, standing* — are HEL's, are known to be lossy, and carry a Reading Grade.
A field officer may say a transliterated frame with total confidence while the
footnote records that the frame may not be an act at all.

## 10. Three worked examples

Trace notation for the script (§11 is the concept; glyphs are Phase 4): `(` and `)`
are contour opening and sealing strokes, `∘` is a standing mark riding on the
boundary, `⌒` is an unsealed contour gap.

Leipzig abbreviations used: LIC license · INT interdict · BND bind · ATT attest ·
REM remit · CON consign · OPEN frame opener · CLOSE frame closer · SEAT seated ·
VEST vested · HELD held · WIT witnessed · UNST unstanding · NULL null ·
SPENT · STAND standing · COND conditioned · LAPSE lapsed · OUT · IN · THRU ·
AGAIN · THIS · JUNCT · CYCLE · VSET vested-set · ONCE · EVERY · NBND non-bindable.

### Example 1 — Threshold interdiction (T-series, Full stratum) — the foothold

**Provenance.** Incised on hard material, recovered 1948 at a locus where outbound
traversal had failed on four recorded attempts between 1912 and 1946. Object
intact. Chain of custody complete. Frame Section catalogue T-0417.

**Trace**

```
( ∘hi  wa-tunali-na-mii  )ma
```

**Transliteration**

> ma=hi wa-tunali-na-mii ma-ma

**Gloss**

```
ma=hi          wa-tunali-na-mii                 ma-ma
INT.OPEN=WIT   OUT-hold.closed-THIS-EVERY       INT.CLOSE-STAND
```

**Natural translation.** "Outbound passage by this crossing is interdicted — this
pass and every recurrence — witnessed at the locus, and standing until remitted."

**Residue English cannot carry.** That *interdicted* is an act in force and not a
property of the crossing; that the interdiction is entitled by presence and the
record does not say whose presence; that *until remitted* is a discharge condition
and not a prediction; that *hold closed* is a provisional gloss for an element that
may not describe the crossing at all.

**Reading Grade II.** Frame secure — every element but the root is core paradigm.
`tunali` provisionally glossed.

**Why this is the foothold.** `tunali` occurs on four T-series objects. At three of
those loci HEL has independently recorded repeated outbound failure. At the fourth,
outbound traversal **succeeded, once, in 1957**, and that single datum is the most
argued-over line in the Register. Three explanations, none eliminable: the frame at
that locus is Fused and the discharge was misread; the interdiction had been
remitted by an act not recovered; or the 1957 crew did something. The confirmation
is by the world's behaviour, never by a translation key, so nothing about the
builders is disclosed — and the one exception is what keeps it from being a puzzle
box with the answer on the lid.

**Canon note.** New assumptions: that inscribed material is recoverable at loci
(C, cosmology-exposing, #2 in §12); that HEL can independently observe repeated
traversal failure at a locus (C, cosmology-exposing, #1). Nothing here identifies an
interdicting party, and §7 forbids inferring one from INT. Prohibited shortcuts #1,
#2, #5, #6 checked.

### Example 2 — The departure recitation (R-series) — two defensible readings

**Provenance.** First recorded in branch notebooks, 1874, compiler unidentified.
Transmitted orally before that for an undetermined period. Recited at departure by
expedition crews; the Precentor's office holds the standard. **Thirty-four crews on
record recited it; three did not return (1889, 1902, 1954).** The Archive's own note,
printed under the text since 1955, states that the correlation is not significant.
The recitation continues.

**Transliteration (Contracted stratum, as recited)**

> ma=nu he-samireni-ho-mii a-ma · wee=we ru-ranahu-ti-we · lu=ta ⟦E-31⟧-ti-mu

**Reading A — Halloran / the Precentor's office (traditional).** `a-ma` is the
contracted INT closer `-a` plus discharge `-ma` STANDING. The first frame is closed.

```
ma=nu        he-samireni-ho-mii              a-ma
INT.OPEN=HELD IN-bear.through-CYCLE-EVERY    INT.CLOSE-STAND
```

> "Arrival into this cycle is interdicted at every recurrence — held by repetition,
> standing until remitted. [Seventh frame, null standing:] through, ⟦unglossed⟧,
> vested-set, unstated. Consign, seated: ⟦E-31⟧, vested-set, once."

**Reading B — Deverell / the Frame Section (from 1911).** The `-a` is the contracted
closer with its **discharge elided in recitation**, and `ma` is a *second INT
opener* beginning a frame whose remainder was lost in transmission. The recitation
contains an interdiction that is opened and never closed.

```
ma=nu        he-samireni-ho-mii            a          ma
INT.OPEN=HELD IN-bear.through-CYCLE-EVERY  INT.CLOSE  INT.OPEN[frame lost]
```

> "Arrival into this cycle is interdicted at every recurrence — held. And a second
> interdiction is opened. …"

**Both readings are fully specified, both are coherent with the whole grammar, and
they differ in operational consequence as sharply as any pair of readings in the
Register.** Under A, the departure recitation is a complete, closed, discharged act
and reciting it is safe. Under B, every departure since at least 1874 has opened an
interdiction that was never remitted, over a scope nobody has read, and HEL is the
agency doing it.

**Discriminating evidence.** A T-series attestation of the same string, where frame
boundaries are physically visible as sealed and unsealed contours. **None is known.**
The recitation exists only in R-series. The Archive has three times authorized
recovery efforts specifically targeting it, most recently in 1998, and the standing
authorization has not been withdrawn.

**Reading Grade IV** (frame contested) for the first frame; **III** for the
remainder — read, and not understood: `samireni` is glossed provisionally,
`ranahu` is not glossed, `⟦E-31⟧` has no gloss and no candidate, and `wee=we` is the
seventh frame whose existence depends on §9.1's length controversy.

**Consequence in practice.** Deverell took Reading B seriously in 1911 and instituted
the Remission Protocol: every recitation is followed by the Archive's reconstructed
REM formula. The reconstruction is a scholarly conjecture, graded IV, and it has been
said aloud at every HEL departure for a hundred and fifteen years by people who
could tell you its grade.

**Canon note.** New assumptions: that HEL possesses conserved oral formulae of
undetermined origin (C, production-only, and directly supported by handoff §3.6 and
§6.8); that reciting them may or may not do something (C — and the *may or may not*
is the assumption, not either branch). Prohibited shortcut #19 is held in both
directions: the architecture does not establish that the recitation is efficacious
and does not establish that it is empty, and the Field school's position — that the
Protocol is a century of wasted breath at real institutional cost — is in print.
Prohibited shortcut #18 held: this item carries three grades in one entry.

### Example 3 — Open Register, Entry 6 (T-series, Fused stratum) — custody

**Provenance.** Impressed on hard material, recovered 1936. **The object is intact
and the frame is complete.** Frame Section catalogue T-0092.

**Trace**

```
( ∘ta   ⟦E-31⟧-ti-mu   he-samireni-ho-we      [no sealing stroke — Fused: none expected]
```

**Transliteration**

> luu=ta ⟦E-31⟧-ti-mu he-samireni-ho-we

**Gloss**

```
luu=ta                ⟦E-31⟧-ti-mu          he-samireni-ho-we
CON.OPEN.STAND=SEAT   E-31-VSET-ONCE        IN-bear.through-CYCLE-NULL
```

**Natural translation.** "⟦E-31⟧ — of whatever set the prior act established, once —
is consigned to care, on standing seated at the extent itself, in force until
remitted; the extent being arrival into this cycle, recurrence unstated."

**Reading Grade I for the frame. No grade for the content.** Every paradigm element
is core: the Fused opener with length is unambiguous, `=ta` SEATED is the most
frequent standing, `-ti-mu` and `he-…-ho-we` are all core. **HEL reads this item
perfectly and does not know what it is about.** `⟦E-31⟧` has no gloss, no candidate
gloss, and eleven attestations, all in CON frames.

**Why it is Entry 6.** The frame is CON with STANDING discharge and no attested
remission. It is not damaged: the Fused stratum has no closers, so nothing is
missing. It is complete, it is in force by its own terms, and HEL cannot identify
what it consigns or where. Marchetti's contested observation is that `samireni` —
the extent here — is the same element as in the departure recitation (Example 2),
which if true means the thing HEL recites at departure and the thing standing in
Entry 6 are scoped to the same cycle. The Frame Section has neither confirmed nor
withdrawn it.

**What HEL built on it.** The Office of Custody adopted the CON frame's *structure*
— consignee, extent, standing, discharge — as the model for HEL's own custodial
formula, which is in **English** and is HEL's, and which requires a custodian to
state the condition on which custody will be given up before it can be taken. This
is offered as a candidate shape for the pin's authorization formula, which handoff
§4.3 leaves open.

**Canon note.** New assumptions: that a CON act class exists in the corpus (C,
cosmology-exposing — a grammar of custody implies something entitled to consign, and
this is the architecture's largest opacity exposure; hedged three ways in §2.2 and
listed in §12); that HEL models its custodial formula on the frame (C,
production-only). **Authority B dependency:** handoff §4.3–4.4 (pin as credential,
custodianship over ownership) is Working canon with ratification unrecovered; if it
falls, the last paragraph falls with it. **Prohibited shortcuts #11, #12, #13
checked:** the pin is a credential and nothing else; the grammar has no possessive
and cannot express entities as property; `⟦E-31⟧` is not identified as an entity,
is not glossed, and the Ontology office's reading that it might be is recorded in
the Register as an over-reading that the Frame Section rejects.

## 11. Script concept

A record is a **field of nested enclosures**: each frame is a closed contour, opened
by a stroke that breaks the boundary at a determined point — the shape of the break
encodes act class — and closed by a sealing stroke whose shape encodes discharge;
the standing mark rides **on the boundary itself** rather than inside, because
standing is a property of the frame and not of its content, and scope material sits
within, with nesting rendered as literal containment so that a carve-out is drawn
inside the license it cuts. The single most useful property is that an unclosed act
is a contour with a visible gap, legible across a room, which is why HEL's threshold
objects can be triaged before they are read and why the Frame Section's intake form
asks *sealed or open* before it asks anything else. Remission cannot be written by
reopening a seal — a sealed contour cannot be broken without destroying the surface
— so a REM frame is drawn as a **new contour enclosing the old one**, with the
consequence that remitted acts are physically surrounded by their remissions and an
object's history of intervention is legible as onion-layers, oldest innermost, which
is the closest thing in the corpus to a dated sequence and is exactly the evidence
§8.2's scandal is fought over. The Fused stratum draws no seal at all and carries
discharge as a thickening of the opening stroke, which is why Fused objects look
unfinished and were catalogued as damaged for fifty-three years. The **field hand**
collapses all of it to bracket pairs on a ruled line with standing marks above the
opening bracket and discharge marks below the closing one — writable at speed in a
notebook, typable as a linear string, information-preserving, geometry-free, and
regarded by the Archive as a barbarism.

## 13. What this architecture is bad at

Stated without mitigation first; mitigations second, where any exist.

1. **Route navigation, which is the setting's operational core.** The grammar
   carries permissions, not topology. HEL reconstructs the network as the shadow of
   a permission structure, which is lossy, slow, and systematically blind wherever
   nothing was ever licensed or forbidden. *Mitigations, honestly limited:* REACH
   `-li` and `-ho` leak real structure (a cycle-scoped act tells you a cycle exists
   and what is on it); ATT frames do carry observed arrivals, and their scarcity
   makes them precious, which is dramatically right and operationally thin; HEL's
   English operational layer carries the navigation load, which is canon-correct but
   means **the alien text is not the map.** If the product wants players computing
   routes from inscriptions, choose Architecture A. B's puzzle is *may I*; A's is
   *how do I get there*. Those are different games.
2. **It stakes more on an unverifiable semantic hypothesis than a formal
   architecture does.** A's categories are positional and survive reinterpretation. B's
   core categories — act, standing, discharge — are *semantic* claims, and if the
   Field school is right they are HEL's projection onto a status word. That is a
   genuine dispute and therefore an asset, and it is also a structural fragility: a
   reviewer who does not accept the performative reading has rejected the
   architecture, not a detail of it.
3. **Register risk: this can read as faux-legal or faux-ecclesiastical.** *License,
   interdict, remit, consign, standing, discharge, precentor* is the vocabulary of
   the chancery and the vestry, and prohibited shortcut #17 forbids reaching for
   institutional age through borrowed register. The defence — that the English is
   explicitly HEL's 1880s translation convention, that the neutral frame numbers
   ship alongside it, and that a re-glossing movement exists in-world — is real but
   it is a defence, not an absolution. **A careless writer will produce something
   that sounds like a liturgy in translation, and that is a permanent risk requiring
   permanent policing.**
4. **No interiority, no narrative, no evaluation.** There is still no way to say *he
   was afraid*, *the river was high*, *we ate*. The one native elegiac form — an
   undeliverable remission (§6.6) — is two attestations wide and cannot carry a
   Namárië. The M-series is the designed reserve for non-performative material, and
   it is empty in Phase 1, and calling an empty reserve an answer would be exactly
   the alibi brief §4 forbids.
5. **Descriptive vocabulary is structurally starved.** Content words occur only
   sealed inside ATT frames, which are rare by taphonomic design. Worldbuilding
   through lexicon — the ordinary way a conlang accumulates texture — is
   unavailable here, and content pipelines will keep asking for it.
6. **No proper names, by design.** Roots designate crossings by condition. Correct
   for handoff §7.5 and bad for product: the "memorable alien name" that
   merchandising, UI and marketing all want must come from HEL's English and catalog
   layers. Expect friction, repeatedly, from people who are not wrong to want it.
7. **The falsifiability advantage (§3.5) cuts toward prohibited shortcut #2.** A
   corpus whose readings can be confirmed by watching the network behave is a corpus
   that gets decoded, and a decoded corpus makes the Box look solvable. The
   counterweights are real — no bilingual, 106 unglossed extent elements, six
   interdicted crossings traversed without incident, the Fused stratum's information
   loss — but the pressure is constant and this is the failure mode B will actually
   die of if it dies.
8. **The emotional peak sits on the worst evidence.** R-series is the affecting
   material and the least warranted material in the corpus: centuries of humans
   repeating something. That is honest and thematically apt, and it means a skeptical
   reader can dismiss the architecture's best moments as folklore. There is no fix.
   There is only printing the grade.
9. **Nesting is hard.** Depth-3 frames are genuinely difficult to parse aloud and
   worse to learn casually. Puzzle players will enjoy the count-back; most players
   will bounce.
10. **Two Authority-B dependencies.** §6.6 and §10 Example 3 lean on handoff
    §4.3–4.4. If the custodianship model is not ratified, the CON class degrades from
    the architecture's ethical spine to a sixth frame.

## 14. Prohibited-shortcut check

Re-read before submission.

**1** builders — untouched; §2.2 and the Dual-Read Rule make touching them
structurally impossible; the one exposure (a grammar of authorization implying an
order of authority) is named in §2.2, hedged three ways including a non-biological
and a HEL-artifact explanation, and listed in §12 as assumption 4.
**2** full decipherment — blocked by the absence of a bilingual, 106 unglossed
extent elements, Grade III as the corpus's most common grade, the Fused stratum's
information loss, and the fact that HEL can read Entry 6 perfectly and does not know
what it is about; the pressure is acknowledged in §13.7.
**3** "Black Box" as self-designation — never used; the Box is not the source of
any corpus item in this proposal and §12 declines to say whether its casing bears
marks.
**4** unrestricted or reversible travel — `pii-` non-bindability, ORIENT
obligatory, `mo-` AGAIN for non-identical recurrence; nothing here creates a portal.
**5** grammar erasing topology — ORIENT is obligatory on every extent; a frame
cannot be formed over an unoriented crossing.
**6** a year identifies a destination — *underivable*: there is no temporal
expression, and a year is not an event and cannot be bound (§3.3, §6.2).
**7** HEL as galactic power — absent.
**8** HEL omniscient — the Open Register, the `we` contamination of HEL's own
archive, a hundred-year Protocol built on a Grade IV conjecture, a method its own

**10** Standard Egg — not referenced.

**12** entities as property — the grammar has **no possessive construction**;

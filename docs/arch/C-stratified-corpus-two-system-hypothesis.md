# Architecture C — Stratified Corpus / Two-System Hypothesis

**Proposal ID:** C
**Target (handoff §9.1):** coordinated package — recovered Black Box substrate **plus**
HEL transliteration, annotation, and operational register.
**Phase:** 1 (canon alignment). Nothing here is a dictionary; the lexical material
below exists only to make the structure judgeable.
**Authority discipline:** every non-A claim is labeled. Canon handoff §12 ledger governs.

---

## 1. The one-sentence architecture

**HEL's catalog contains two structurally different systems that it has filed as one
for sixty years; they show regular formal correspondences; whether those
correspondences mean descent, elaboration, contact, or nothing at all is the
central unresolved dispute of HEL's linguistics section, and it stays unresolved.**

The handoff (§5.1) lists "several systems incorrectly grouped together" as one of
seven live scholarly readings of the corpus. Architectures A and B treat that line
as a caveat. This architecture treats it as the load-bearing wall.

**Why:** the thing that makes Tolkien's languages feel like they have a floor under
them is not vocabulary size. It is that they are related to each other in a way you
can *work out*, and that working it out is itself an activity. This architecture
reproduces that engine — two systems, systematic correspondences, a contested
reconstruction behind both — while removing the one part of Tolkien's model this
canon forbids: a named ancestral people. Here there is no proto-language and no
proto-speaker. There is a correspondence table and three factions fighting over
what it means.

**The one-line typological pitch:** *the coordinate is inflected; the predicate is not.*
Both systems put their morphology on the spacetime anchor and leave the verb bare.
That is the inverse of English, and it is the structural consequence of a world
where **where/when you are** is the hard problem and **what happened** is the easy one.

## 2. Speaker/user model

### 2.1 The non-resolution, stated as a design commitment

**This architecture makes no claim about who or what produced any corpus item, and
its grammar is built so that it cannot be forced to.**

That is not a disclaimer. It is a structural property, and it is the strongest
single argument for this architecture:

- The clause core is a **directed edge between two coordinates**. Agents are not
  arguments of the predicate. There is **no attested agent slot** in either system.
- Therefore no well-formed sentence in either system requires a producer, a
  speaker, an author, or a "who." A translator who wants an agent has to supply one
  from outside the text, and HEL's own style manual forbids it in published readings.
- Producing builder-lore in this architecture requires *breaking the grammar*, not
  merely resisting temptation. **The canon violation this project most fears is
  ungrammatical here.**

**Authority D remains D.** The Black Box builders are unknown and undescribed
(handoff §12). Nothing below names, dates, locates, embodies, or motivates them.

What the architecture models instead is **provenance**: what HEL physically holds,
where it got it, what condition it is in, and what it has done with it. Provenance
is a claim about HEL. It is not a claim about the universe.

### 2.2 The two systems

HEL's catalog prefix is **CS** — originally *Casing Series*, assigned in the 1890s
when every known item came off the encasement and mounting hardware. Most CS-2
material has no casing association whatsoever. The label is unfixable: sixty years
of route documentation, containment orders, and expedition authorizations cite it.
This is handoff §6.7 ("archival names that outlive their original explanations")
occurring in the catalog's own header.

| | **CS-1** | **CS-2** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Field slang | "the tight" | "the long" |
| Archivists' slang | "notch" | "branch" (after the script hands) |
| Character | terse, formulaic, operational | extended, non-repeating, discursive/inscriptional |
| Typical length | 4 cells (median), 9 max | 107 cells (median inscription), 312 max |
| Type/token profile | 3,112 tokens / **238 types** | ~4,400 cells / ~900 sequence types, **381 hapax** |
| Cell inventory used | 31 of 47 | 46 of 47 |
| Phonotactics (as transliterated) | closed syllables, clusters | strictly open syllables, no clusters |
| Evidential system | **7 values** | **5 values** |
| Coordinate morphology | minimal; anchors usually zero | elaborate; four cell-classes on one anchor |
| Causal linkage | 2 values | 5 values |
| Envelope | fused onto the relator as codas | free-standing obligatory constituent |

Read that table again and notice the shape of the problem. **CS-1 is evidential-rich
and coordinate-poor. CS-2 is coordinate-rich and evidential-poor.** Neither is
simply "more" than the other. HEL calls this **the Inversion Problem**, and no
reconstruction explains it cleanly. Every HEL linguist's first published paper is
about the Inversion Problem. None of them has solved it.

### 2.3 The evidence corpus — where, how much, what condition

**Authority C throughout this subsection.** Items marked ⚠ make a claim about the
wider universe and need explicit approval; the rest are institutional or production
detail.

**Five provenance channels:**

1. **Casing-adjacent (in situ).** Incised marks on the encasement and mounting
   hardware. ~3% of CS-1; 3 CS-2 surfaces. **HEL cannot establish whether these are
   original to the object or were added by earlier human custodians** — the
   metallurgy of the mount is not the metallurgy of the Box, and the Box is
   permanently encased (canon A), so the question is not answerable from the
   outside. This ambiguity is deliberate and permanent.
2. ⚠ **Instrument transcription at stream nodes.** 71% of all CS-1 material. At 23
   catalogued nodes, HEL's apparatus records recurring patterned phenomena that the
   1930s Transcription Board resolved into cells. *This asserts that stream nodes
   produce something recordable* — handoff §9.2 lists this as open. **It is the
   single largest cosmological exposure in this proposal.** Note what it does not
   assert: not that the Box emits anything, not that the phenomena are messages,
   not that the Board's resolution into cells is correct. Ferreira's people
   maintain that step 3 is a hallucination of the apparatus.
3. ⚠ **Recovered portable objects.** 41 substantial CS-2 inscriptions and 19% of
   CS-1, on hardware and portable objects retrieved by expedition from
   stream-accessible locations. *This asserts that inscribed artifacts exist beyond
   the Box.*
4. **Archive copy.** 12 CS-2 inscriptions and 7% of CS-1 survive **only** as HEL
   rubbings, field sketches, and photographs of objects since lost, destroyed, or
   stranded behind a route that has gone dormant. Combined with (5), **roughly 14%
   of the corpus cannot be re-examined by anyone now living.** This is the ordinary
   condition of Etruscan and Linear A philology and it is what makes disputes
   permanent rather than merely current.
5. **Oral transmission.** 19 "carried lines" in HEL practice, phonologically
   conserved, transmitted internally. 6 map onto CS-2 sequences at ≥80% cell match.
   4 map partially. **9 match nothing in the written corpus.** Whether those nine
   are degraded CS-2, a third system, or nineteenth-century human invention is open
   and is meant to stay open.

**Condition grades** (HEL's own scale, printed on every catalog card): `IS` in situ ·
`RO` recovered object · `IT` instrument transcription · `AC` archive copy, original
extant · `AL` archive copy, original lost · `OT` oral transmission.

**The hapax number is the important one.** 381 of ~900 CS-2 sequence types occur
exactly once. **42% hapax rate is a mathematical guarantee that CS-2 can never be
fully read**, no matter how good HEL's linguists get, because there is no second
context to constrain the reading. The corpus is not "resistant to translation"
because we declined to write more. It is resistant because of a countable property
of the evidence. That is the honest answer to "fragmentary but Tolkien-deep": the
*design bible* generates every hapax completely (brief §4); the *corpus* withholds
their second attestation, and the withholding has a number attached to it.

## 3. Why this and not subject–verb–object

Not asserted. Argued, on four grounds.

**3.1 SVO forces an agent, and an agent is a canon violation.**
An SVO clause has a subject slot that wants filling. Every sentence you write in an
SVO substrate is a small pressure to answer "who." Answer it eighty times and you
have accidentally described the builders. The edge-frame has no subject slot at all:
the atomic proposition is *between coordinate X and coordinate Z there is an edge of
type Y, in status S, warranted by E.* The custodial and containment vocabulary (§5.6)
is built from the *same* frame — relations between a party and a ward are directed
edges, not transitive verbs — so even the institutional register never acquires a
grammatical agent. **The architecture's protection against the project's worst
failure mode is structural, not disciplinary.**

**3.2 SVO carries tense, and tense is exactly what canon breaks.**
Canon A: a date does not determine a place; return to a familiar year may emerge
elsewhere; the network is one-directional and interlocking. English tense encodes
speaker-relative time on the predicate, which presumes that the speaker's timeline
is the frame of reference. Under canon that presumption is *the mistake that gets
expeditions killed*. Both CS systems have **no tense whatsoever, and no attested
numeral or date expression anywhere in the corpus** — a fact HEL finds remarkable
and which means **no inscription can be internally dated and no inscription
identifies a year**. Temporality lives on the anchor as congruence and pass-ordinal
(§4.2), i.e. as a statement about position in a network, never as a statement about
a speaker's now. This directly enforces prohibited shortcut #6.

**3.3 SVO makes evidentiality optional, and canon makes it central.**
In English, "probably," "reportedly," and "on instruments" are adverbs you can drop.
Handoff §6.4 says evidentiality "should be central rather than decorative." In CS-2
the **evidential-bearing envelope is an obligatory constituent: a frame without one
is ungrammatical, not merely terse.** In CS-1 it is a fused coda that cannot be
deleted without destroying the word. You cannot make an unmarked assertion in
either system. That is not decoration; it is a constraint that shapes every sentence
in the corpus and it falls out of the canon rather than being bolted on.

**3.4 SVO is an argument-structure grammar, and HEL's problem is a topology problem.**
Handoff §6.3 asks for sequence, adjacency, junction, convergence, recurrence,
inaccessible reverse edges, nested cycles, path dependence, route completion. Those
are relations on a graph. An argument-structure grammar can *describe* a graph the
way English describes chess — with a large lexicon of special-purpose nouns. An
edge-frame grammar *is* a graph notation with an evidential wrapper, so path
dependence (§4.2, `-pel`/`-kor`), through-passage (§4.3, phase `-nu`), and
circuit-completion (phase `-ro`) are **inflectional categories**, not vocabulary
items. Handoff §6.1 warns that "a generic verb equivalent to English 'go' is unlikely
to be enough." In this architecture there is no verb "go" to be insufficient.

**The specific gain from doing this twice:** a single system can be internally
elegant but has no outside. Correspondences give the reader a *method* — you learn
that CS-2 `w` shows up as CS-1 `m` before back vowels, you apply it yourself to a new
form, and it works. That is the sensation Tolkien's readers get from Sindarin and
Quenya, and it is not available to any one-system design at any budget.

## 4. CS-2, specified

CS-2 is presented first because it is the system HEL can actually read. This
ordering is a fact about HEL's evidence, **not** a claim that CS-2 is prior.

### 4.1 The frame

A CS-2 clause is a **frame** of four constituents in fixed order:

```
⟨ANCHOR₁⟩   ⟨RELATOR⟩   ⟨ANCHOR₂⟩   ⟨ENVELOPE⟩
 source      edge-type    target      how known / what status
 coordinate   + phase      coordinate  + causal linkage
```

- Constituent order is rigid. No attested reordering in 41 inscriptions.
- ANCHOR₁ is omissible when recoverable (~31% of frames). ANCHOR₂ is not.
- **The ENVELOPE is obligatory.** Zero counterexamples in CS-2.
- Frames are separated by ⟨|⟩ and are not embedded. CS-2 has **no subordination**;
  inter-frame relations are marked on the *following* frame's envelope (§4.5).

### 4.2 The anchor — where the morphology lives

```
ANCHOR = LOCUS-root + CONGRUENCE (+ PASS) (+ PATH-ROLE) (+ PROJECTION)
```

**LOCUS roots.** ~140 attested. A locus is not a place and not a time; it is a
position in the network. HEL does not know whether locus roots are proper names,
class terms, addresses, or descriptions, and its style manual requires citing them
untranslated in small capitals. Examples used below: *kosila*, *welinu*, *hepani*,
*taruna*, *ngoli*, *wosala*.

**CONGRUENCE** — obligatory. Four values. This is where handoff §6.2's
same-place/different-time problem is solved:

| Form | Gloss | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `-tani` | SAME | this locus, this pass. Full identity. |
| `-naki` | RECUR | this locus, a different pass. *(same place, different time)* |
| `-mari` | CONGRUENT | a distinct locus that presents the same face to the network — interchangeable for routing. *(different place, same routing position)* |
| `-sowa` | RESEMBLANT | presents as this locus; identity **not** established. |

`-sowa` is HEL's "false-return marker" and is the direct grammaticalization of
handoff §6.1's "recurrence at a similar but not identical spacetime coordinate."
Note that `-tani`, `-naki`, `-mari` share a final `-i` and `-sowa` does not; `-sowa`
is also the only congruence cell attested as a free form. Ferreira builds on this.

**PASS ordinal** — optional. Which traversal of the locus is meant:

| Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `hui` | this pass |
| `kila` | a pass ordered before this one |
| `kilo` | a pass ordered after this one |
| `kiwa` | **a pass whose order relative to this one is not established** |

`kiwa` is the single most alien morpheme in the design and it is entirely
canon-derived. In an interlocking directed loop system, "before" and "after" are not
guaranteed to be defined between two passes of the same locus. CS-2 has a dedicated
inflection for that undefinedness. English has no way to say it without a paragraph.

**PATH-ROLE** — optional. Path dependence, grammaticalized (handoff §6.3):
`-pel` obligatory intermediate (you may pass through, you may not terminate) ·
`-kor` optional intermediate · `-suta` terminus · `-wana` source.
`-pel` is canon A's "A→C→B may be required" as an *inflection on the coordinate*.

**PROJECTION** — optional, ANCHOR₂ only. Handoff §6.2's predicted/observed/intended
triad, plus one HEL needed and English lacks:
`-wisu` obtained (traversal actually delivered here) · `-rahi` projected (the route
model predicts here) · `-panu` intended (this is what was wanted) · `-teso` nominal
(this is what the record calls it). A route report that marks its target `-rahi` and
not `-wisu` is saying *we have not been there; the model says it is there* — and
HEL's containment doctrine turns on that distinction.

### 4.3 The relator — deliberately thin

```
RELATOR = (COUNTER-) + EDGE-stem + PHASE
```

~30 edge stems attested. The navigational core: `kanu` traversable edge · `ngoli`
junction edge (three or more incident) · `wosala` convergent edge (two paths
arriving at one locus) · `taruna` bounding edge (the network admits no crossing).
The custodial set is in §5.6.

**PHASE** — obligatory, six values:
`-ta` departure/detachment · `-ki` arrival/settling · `-nu` **through-passage
(traversed, not a terminus)** · `-ro` circuit completion (return to a locus already
on this path) · `-si` engaged without arrival (the abort case) · `-lu` sustained,
held open, no terminus.

### 4.4 The Counter-Edge Constraint — canon rule #5 enforced by grammar

Prohibited shortcut #5 says grammar must not erase the one-way topology. Most
designs handle this by *not writing* reverse-motion sentences. This one makes them
ill-formed.

Reverse traversal has no verb. It has a **counter-edge prefix `wesa-`**, which marks
the frame as concerning an edge in the direction the network does not admit. And:

> **The Counter-Edge Constraint.** A frame containing `wesa-` may take only the
> warrants `hesu` (IMPOSSIBLE) or `hemi` (UNWARRANTED). It may **never** take `hena`
> (HOLDS).

**CS-2 cannot assert that a reverse edge holds.** Not "does not"; *cannot*. Zero
violations in 41 inscriptions. The language can discuss reverse motion as fully as
you like — hypothetically, as a warning, as a denial — and is structurally incapable
of affirming it.

There are **two apparent violations in CS-1**, both from the same node, both famous.
Sennett calls them mis-segmentations. Ferreira calls them evidence the constraint is
a CS-2 property that was never in CS-1. Ostrowe calls them evidence that CS-1 and
CS-2 are not the same kind of thing at all. They are reproduced in every
introductory reading list.

### 4.5 The envelope — evidentiality, warrant, causality

```
ENVELOPE = EVIDENCE + WARRANT (+ LINKAGE)
```

**EVIDENCE (5 in CS-2):**

| Form | HEL label | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sa-` | direct | a party was present at the locus |
| `wi-` | trace | recorded by apparatus; no party present |
| `ru-` | inferred | derived from observed routing consequences |
| `po-` | carried | received from an earlier record or from repetition; no current warrant |
| `mu-` | unattributed | source not stated. **HEL disputes whether this means "anonymous" or "not applicable."** Ferreira denies it is an evidential at all (§6.2). |

**WARRANT — the four-way negation (handoff §7.3, deliverable §2.9):**

Negation in CS-2 is **not an operator**. It is a value of the obligatory warrant
slot. There is no way to negate a constituent; the warrant scopes the entire frame.

| Form | HEL label | Claim type | Content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hena` | HOLDS | — | the edge holds |
| `hesu` | **IMPOSSIBLE** | topological | the network contains no such edge |
| `taruka` | **INTERDICTED** | normative | the edge exists and must not be traversed. **The text never says by whom.** |
| `heli` | **DORMANT / UNAVAILABLE** | state | the edge exists and is admitted but is not currently traversable |
| `hemi` | **UNWARRANTED / UNVERIFIED** | epistemic | no warrant exists either way |

Two structural facts worth keeping:

- **Four of the five share the formative `he-`. `taruka` is suppletive.** The odd
  one out is the *normative* category — the only one that is about permission rather
  than about the network. Both major reconstructions cite this, in opposite
  directions (§6).
- **Because negation cannot scope below the frame, denying one element requires
  restating the whole frame.** This predicts a corpus full of near-identical frames
  in sequence differing in a single cell — and that is exactly what CS-2 looks like.
  A grammatical rule explains a corpus statistic. That is the kind of coherence that
  makes a constructed system feel found rather than made.

**LINKAGE (5 in CS-2) — causality under constraint (handoff §6.5):**
CS-2 has no conjunctions. The relation between a frame and its predecessor is marked
in the *second* frame's envelope:

| Form | Content |
| --- | --- |
| `nasi` | ordinary consequence within one directional segment |
| `naku` | consequence attributed **across a loop** — cause and effect on different passes of a shared locus |
| `nari` | consequence located **before the acting party's own departure frame** |
| `nawe` | **a correlation held without a causal claim** — an explicit refusal |
| `nalo` | precaution taken against a possible relation, not a known one |

`nawe` has entered HEL's English. Personnel say "we can only *nawe* it" to mean the
correlation is solid and the causation is not claimed. It appears in written field
reports, in the containment classification scheme, and in at least one HEL joke. This
is handoff §6.7 working correctly: institutional vocabulary as the medium of secrecy
and precision, rather than a "secret language."

Note also `naku` and the congruence cell `-naki`. They are one cell apart. **An 1890s
archive reading confused them systematically, translating a whole class of route
labels as loop-caused when they were merely recurrent.** The corrected readings were
issued in 1951; the *old* names for four routes are still what field crews say.
Deprecated readings are retained in the catalog by policy (handoff §10, Phase 4.7).

## 5. Canon pressures, one by one

### 5.1 Directed motion (handoff §6.1)
Permitted traversal = bare relator. Hypothetical reverse = `wesa-` under the
Counter-Edge Constraint, warrant-restricted. Arrival = phase `-ki`. Departure =
phase `-ta`. Intermediate traversal = phase `-nu` **plus** anchor path-role `-pel`
(obligatory) or `-kor` (optional) — the distinction between *you may pass here* and
*you may only pass here*. Recurrence at a similar-but-not-identical coordinate =
congruence `-sowa`, with `-naki` reserved for verified recurrence. Six categories,
six morphemes, no generic "go."

### 5.2 Spacetime as one coordinate (handoff §6.2)
There is no "where" morphology and no "when" morphology, because there is no
where/when distinction. There is a **locus** with congruence, pass-ordinal, path-role
and projection. Same-place/different-time = `-naki`. Same-routing-position/different-
locus = `-mari`. Same-node/different-traversal = `-tani` + pass-ordinal contrast.
Predicted = `-rahi`; observed = `-wisu`; intended = `-panu`; what-the-record-calls-it
= `-teso`. Origin-relative, traveler-relative and archive-relative chronology are the
`kila`/`kilo`/`kiwa`/`hui` series, and `kiwa` says the ordering is *undefined* rather
than unknown. Prohibited shortcut #6 is enforced by the absence of numerals.

### 5.3 Loop topology (handoff §6.3)
Sequence and adjacency: frame chaining, no subordination. Junction: edge stem `ngoli`.
Convergence: edge stem `wosala`. Recurrence: `-naki` / `-sowa`. Inaccessible reverse
edges: `wesa-` + Counter-Edge Constraint. Nested cycles: phase `-ro` scoped by
pass-ordinal — a `-ro` frame with `kila` closes an earlier circuit, one with `hui`
closes the current one. Path dependence: `-pel`/`-kor`. Route completion: `-ro` vs
the abort phase `-si`. All of these are inflectional. None is a noun.

### 5.4 Evidentiality (handoff §6.4)
Obligatory envelope; no unmarked assertion possible in either system. The
substrate carries source-of-knowledge (`sa- wi- ru- po- mu-`); **HEL's annotation
layer carries confidence** (§7.3), because confidence is HEL's problem and not the
corpus's. Handoff §6.4's "ritually preserved without known meaning" is `po-`
(carried) in the substrate and condition grade `OT` in the catalog — and, honestly,
often confidence C5 as well. Keeping source and confidence in different layers is
what stops the design from claiming the corpus knows how sure HEL is.

### 5.5 Causality under constraint (handoff §6.5)
The five-value linkage series (§4.5) covers all six of the handoff's listed cases:
ordinary sequence (`nasi`), cause inferred across a loop (`naku`), consequence
preceding the traveler's departure frame (`nari`), precaution against possible loop
effects (`nalo`), correlation refused as causal (`nawe`), and prohibited action with
unknown consequence (warrant `taruka` with envelope evidence `mu-`, i.e. interdicted,
source of interdiction unstated). **The physics is not settled by any of this.** The
grammar shows only that its users needed these distinctions — which is precisely
what the handoff asks for.

### 5.6 Stewardship, not mastery (handoff §6.6)
**CS-2 has no possessive construction of any kind.** Not a disfavoured one — none.
A relation between a party and a ward is an edge in the same frame system as a route,
with the same phases and the same obligatory envelope:

| Edge stem | HEL gloss |
| --- | --- |
| `temi` | sustaining — the party supplies condition or care to the ward |
| `hala` | screening — the party stands between the ward and the network (containment) |
| `suri` | observing; takes phase `-lu` (sustained) to mean observation without interference |
| `pota` | warrant-of-approach — authorized access |

The ward is never the target of a control-type edge, because **no control-type edge
is attested**. There is no relator meaning "hold as property."

**And here is the discipline this architecture is for.** The field reading of that
absence — that the users were custodians, not owners — is popular, moving, and
inadmissible. It is an argument from silence over a 41-inscription corpus with a 42%
hapax rate. HEL's archive prints the absence as a **distributional fact with a
sample-size note attached** and refuses the inference. The design must never cash it
in. Absence of a possessive is evidence about the corpus. It is not evidence about
anyone's ethics, and treating it as such would be prohibited shortcut #1 arriving
through the back door wearing a nice coat.

Consequence for the pin and custodianship (canon B): the Custodial Line (§8,
Example 3) is expressible; "my entity" is **not expressible in CS-2 at all**, which
gives the working ontology's custodian-not-owner principle a grammatical basis
rather than a policy basis.

### 5.7 Secrecy and compartmentalization (handoff §6.7)
Four mechanisms, all institutional rather than cryptographic:

- **Catalog IDs are the stable layer** (`CS-1/N-0044`). Names change when hypotheses
  fall; IDs do not. Four routes currently carry a 1951-corrected catalog name and a
  pre-1951 field name that crews still use.
- **Sleeved names**: a mundane public-facing term paired to a catalog ID, chosen for
  being boring. Handoff §7.5's "code words whose literal meanings are intentionally
  mundane."
- **Challenge–response uses CS-1 strings precisely because nobody can read them.**
  A string's *meaning* is not the credential; correct recall of a low-frequency
  string from the archive is. Unfakeable by anyone without archive access, and it
  requires no secret to be kept, because there is no secret — only access.
- **Clearance is graded by provenance channel, not by content.** Channel 2
  (instrument transcription at nodes) is restricted at a higher grade than Channel 3
  (recovered objects), because it reveals node locations.

### 5.8 Ritual without confirmed mysticism (handoff §6.8)
The 19 carried lines are phonologically conserved, performed in fixed order, and
partially unmatched to the written corpus. The operative mechanism is the **parity
doubling**: a carried line is spoken twice, and the second utterance must differ from
the first in one specified cell. *If the two utterances are identical, the line is
void and must be restarted.* It is a checksum (handoff §7.2) and it is also, visibly,
a ritual, and HEL cannot tell you which description is prior.

Three complications, all unresolved by design:
1. The 1908 Chicago manual describes the parity rule as **"restored."** HEL cannot
   determine whether that means *recovered from evidence* or *reconstructed by the
   committee*. The man who wrote it left no notes.
2. The parity substitution in the Custodial Line changes `-mari` to `-naki`, which
   under every current reading **means something substantively different**. HEL may
   be asserting something it does not intend, twice a week, in every intake room.
   Ostrowe has published on this. Nothing has been changed.
3. Deviation from the lines correlates with expedition failure in HEL's own records.
   The correlation is real, the sample is small, the confound is obvious (crews who
   skip the lines are crews under time pressure), and HEL's statistics office marks
   it `nawe`. **It keeps performing them.** That is handoff §6.8 exactly: neither
   proven magic nor proven superstition, and the institution acts anyway.

## 6. CS-1, and the correspondences

### 6.1 CS-1 structure

CS-1 writes the whole frame as **one word, unsegmented**. Anchors are usually zero,
recoverable from the physical placement of the mark or from the node at which the
instrument trace was taken — *the inscription's own location is its ANCHOR₁*. This is
why CS-1 works as a control notation and also why it is nearly unreadable.

```
CS-1 word  =  EVID + EDGE-stem + PHASE + (PATH-ROLE) + (CONGRUENCE) + WARRANT
                 prefix   reduced    single C    cluster      cell          coda
```

- **7 evidentials**: `sa- si- wi- we- wo- ru- po-`. No reflex of CS-2 `mu-`.
- **2 linkage values**: `-ns` linked, `-nw` correlational. The five-way CS-2 series
  has no CS-1 counterpart.
- **Phases are single consonants**: `-t -k -n -r -s -l`.
- **Warrants are codas**: `-en -es -el -em`, plus bulky irregular `-truk`.
- Codas and clusters are permitted; CS-2 has neither.
- An **epenthetic `a`** repairs illegal clusters. Remember that vowel. It is the most
  written-about single sound in HEL linguistics (§6.3).

### 6.2 The correspondence sets

Stated **non-directionally**, as `CS-2 ~ CS-1`. A comparativist states correspondences
before deciding which way they run; so does this design, and so must anyone building
on it.

| | Set | Examples | Attestation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **R1** | final vowel ~ ∅ | `kanu ~ kan`, `temi ~ tem`, `hala ~ hal`, `suri ~ sur`, `pota ~ pot` | 58/58 relator stems. Exceptionless. |
| **R2** | medial `h` ~ ∅ with vowel coalescence | `pehila ~ peel`, `tehewa ~ tewa` | 11 pairs |
| **R3** | `w` ~ `m` / __ o,u ; `w` ~ `w` elsewhere | `wosala ~ mosal`, `wehila ~ wehil` | 9 + 14 pairs, **no counterexamples** |
| **R4** | `ng` ~ `k` initially; `ng` ~ `n` medially | `ngoli ~ kol`, `tangu ~ tan` | 7 + 6 pairs. Creates a CS-1 merger of `taNGu` and `tanu`. |
| **R5** | envelope word ~ suffixal coda | `hena ~ -en`, `hesu ~ -es`, `heli ~ -el`, `hemi ~ -em`, `taruka ~ -truk` | closed set |
| **R6** | phase CV ~ C | `-ta ~ -t`, `-ki ~ -k`, `-nu ~ -n`, `-ro ~ -r`, `-si ~ -s`, `-lu ~ -l` | closed set |
| **R7** | `sa- ~ sa-/si-` · `wi- ~ wi-/we-/wo-` · `ru- ~ ru-` · `po- ~ po-` · `mu- ~ ∅` | — | **the crux; see §6.4** |

**R3 is why the field believes the systems are related at all.** A conditioned split
with no counterexamples is not something you get from coincidence, from borrowing, or
from a shared subject matter. It is the kind of fact that founded Indo-European
studies, and in-world it founded HEL's.

**The residue.** 91 CS-1 string types have no CS-2 correspondent. 214 CS-2 sequences
have no CS-1 correspondent. Both camps claim the residue and neither can explain all
of it.

### 6.3 The epenthesis question

CS-1 `wikantaplel` (Example 1) contains an `a` between the phase consonant `-t` and
the path-role cluster `-pl`. Is that `a`:

- **the surviving vowel of CS-2 phase `-ta`** — in which case CS-1 preserves CS-2's
  shape and descent is nearly demonstrated; or
- **epenthesis**, inserted by CS-1's own phonotactics to repair `ntpl` — in which
  case it is evidence of nothing whatsoever?

Both readings are formally available. There is no CS-1 form that discriminates them,
because the environments overlap completely. This single vowel has more published
pages behind it than any other feature of the corpus, and the fact that a sixty-year
discipline can hinge on one unstressed `a` is exactly the texture this architecture
is buying.

### 6.4 The three reconstructions

All three are **Authority D as to truth**. The *existence of the dispute* and the
*institutional consequences* are Authority C (proposed here for approval). **None of
the three may ever be ratified in canon.** A decision-maker approving this
architecture is approving a permanently open question, and that is the point.

---

#### **The Sennett Reduction** — CS-2 → CS-1 (majority; institutionally load-bearing)

*Marya Sennett, Chicago Archive, principal papers 1961–1974.*

**Claim.** CS-1 is a compressed operational descendant of CS-2, produced by regular
attrition: final-vowel loss (R1), h-loss (R2), envelope-to-coda fusion (R5),
phase reduction (R6).

**Evidence cited.**
1. **R1 is exceptionless across 58 stems.** Regular sound change, textbook.
2. **The suppletive warrant survives as a suppletive warrant.** `taruka ~ -truk` is
   bulky and irregular in *both* systems. Irregularity in high-frequency paradigm
   members is the signature of inheritance; nobody borrows an irregularity.
3. **The recoverability argument.** CS-1's fused words are segmentable — at all —
   only by reference to CS-2 shapes. A system that can only be parsed through another
   system is very likely derived from it.
4. **The notch-form argument** (§9): CS-1 notch glyphs are recoverable as reduced
   CS-2 branch glyphs by a consistent geometric rule.

**Weakness she conceded in print.** R7. CS-2 `wi-` corresponds to CS-1 `wi-/we-/wo-`,
and under Reduction that is an **unconditioned three-way split**. Historical
linguistics does not accept unconditioned splits. Sennett spent eleven years looking
for the conditioning environment and published, in 1974, that she had not found it.

**Operational consequence — this is what matters.** Under Reduction, a CS-1 route
command may be *inflated* through the correspondence sets and glossed with CS-2
semantics. **HEL's Route Authorization Formulary is built on exactly this move.**
Every authorization phrase HEL currently uses to license an expedition is a
Sennett-inflated CS-1 string. If the Reduction falls, HEL has been authorizing
expeditions for fifty years on readings it is not entitled to.

---

#### **The Ferreira Elaboration** — CS-1 → CS-2 (rising minority; better on the data)

*Ines Ferreira, Second Archive, principal papers 1989–present.*

**Claim.** CS-1 is the primary system — a compact control notation. CS-2 is a later
elaboration of it: a discursive layer built *on top of* a control notation by
someone or something making it say more than it originally said. Therefore **CS-2's
rich semantics must not be projected backward onto CS-1.**

**Evidence cited.**
1. **R7 runs her way.** Three-to-one merger (CS-1 → CS-2) requires no conditioning.
   Splits need environments; mergers do not. This is her strongest card and Sennett
   conceded it.
2. **The Inversion Problem favours her.** CS-1 is finer-grained on evidence; CS-2 is
   finer-grained on coordinates and causality. A *reduction* should not produce a
   descendant that is richer than its ancestor in any category. An *elaboration* is
   expected to develop exactly the categories CS-2 develops — coordinates and causal
   linkage are what a commentator adds — while flattening operational distinctions a
   commentator has no use for.
3. **The suppletive warrant is the normative one.** `taruka` INTERDICTED is the only
   warrant about permission rather than about the network, and it is the only one
   outside the `he-` formative. She reads it as an intrusion into a system that
   originally spoke only about topology.
4. **The residue splits predictably.** The 214 CS-2-only sequences are
   disproportionately the discursive and causal vocabulary; the 91 CS-1-only strings
   are disproportionately operational. That is the distribution elaboration predicts.

**Weakness she concedes.** CS-2 contains ~30 forms with unpredictable suppletive
alternations that pattern *with* CS-1 forms. Secondary elaborations do not usually
manufacture inherited-looking irregularity. Her answer — that the elaborators took
over frozen chunks whole — is admitted to be weaker than the objection.

**What she refuses to say, and this is important.** Ferreira will not say *who*
elaborated. Her students have split into a "second party" wing and a
"same source, later phase" wing; she has published that the question is
**undecidable on present evidence** and has declined to join either. The Elaboration
hypothesis requires no second party of any described kind and is equally satisfied by
a later phase of the same process, an automated one, or something the categories
"party" and "phase" do not fit. **Authority D is untouched by this hypothesis and
must remain so in every downstream use of it.**

**Operational consequence.** Under Elaboration, CS-1 may be read only
*distributionally* — by correlating strings with observed route behaviour, claiming
no meaning. Ferreira's people maintain the **Behavioural Concordance**: 238 CS-1
string types listed against outcomes, with the semantic column deliberately blank.
It is far less useful than the Formulary and far more likely to be right. Field
crews find it maddening. Two of them keep it in their kit anyway.

---

#### **The Ostrowe Objection** — no relation demonstrable (small, respected, obstructive)

*Jan Ostrowe, Second Archive, 1996–.*

**Claim.** The correspondences are an **artifact of the transliteration standard.**
STL-3 was designed in 1938 by people who already believed the two series were
related. Its segmentation of CS-1 was performed by analogy to CS-2 shapes. Of course
the correspondences come out clean: they were built into the instrument that produced
the data.

**Evidence cited.** The 1938 Board minutes, which say so, in the Board's own words.
The two Counter-Edge violations in CS-1 (§4.4), which he holds are only "violations"
if you assume a shared grammar. The complete absence of CS-2 `mu-` from CS-1, which
he reads as a category that simply does not exist in the other system.

**His remedy** is STL-4: re-transliterate the entire corpus under a neutral standard
that segments each series on its own internal evidence. **It has been approved in
principle four times and deferred four times**, because re-cataloguing sixty years of
route notation would ground every expedition for the duration. The last deferral
memo is three sentences long and does not give a reason.

Ostrowe is right about the methodology and everyone knows it. He will not win,
because winning costs more than the institution can pay. **That is the most HEL
sentence in this document**, and it is the best available demonstration that this
architecture produces institutional drama out of pure philology.

## 7. The human-facing layers

The four layers of brief §7 are kept visibly distinct: **underlying system** (CS-1 /
CS-2 structure, partly reconstructable) · **STL-3 transliteration** · **FP-2 field
pronunciation** · **English gloss**. Nothing in layers 2–4 is evidence about layer 1.

### 7.1 STL-3 — the transliteration standard (Authority C)

The corpus's recurring units are transliterated as **cells**, not letters. A cell is
`(C)V`.

- **Onsets (12 + zero):** `p t k m n ng s h l r w y`
- **Vowels (5):** `a e i o u`
- 65 possible cells; **47 attested** corpus-wide; 31 in CS-1; 46 in CS-2. One cell,
  `ngu`, is **CS-1-only** — a small, famous, much-argued fact.
- **CS-2 is strictly open-syllable.** CS-1 permits codas `m n l r s k t p` and the
  onset clusters `sp st sk pr tr kr pl kl`. STL-3 writes CS-1 solid, unsegmented,
  because segmenting it is a theoretical act (§6.3) and the standard declines to
  perform one in the transliteration line.

**Four non-cell marks:**

| Mark | Meaning | Spoken |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `\|` | frame boundary | a pause |
| `·` | a cell is present but unreadable | skipped |
| `=` | a cell is **certainly absent** — a deliberate blank in the original | skipped |
| `q` | an unclassified recurring unit; **not a cell**; function unknown | omitted, or hummed |

The `·` / `=` distinction is STL-3's most contested editorial decision: it encodes a
theory (that the producers left deliberate blanks) inside what is supposed to be a
neutral transcription. Ostrowe's Objection begins here. `q` is suspected by a minority
to be a numeral, which would overturn the "no numerals" observation of §3.2. Nobody
has made the case stick.

**No apostrophes. No harsh clusters. No honorifics.** The inventory is drawn from
cross-linguistically ordinary segments precisely so that it belongs to nobody, and
the strangeness is carried entirely by §§4–6. **No real-world language supplied
material, texture, or "flavour," and no Indigenous language material of any kind was
consulted or used** (prohibited shortcut #14, treated as absolute). If a form here
reads as alien because of how it is spelled, it has failed and should be changed.

### 7.2 FP-2 — HEL field pronunciation (Authority C)

An approximation optimized for repeatability over authenticity, and **known by HEL to
be probably wrong**.

- Vowels `a e i o u` = [a ɛ i ɔ u]. No reduction, ever — the whole point is that an
  unstressed cell survives a bad channel.
- `ng` = [ŋ]; `r` = alveolar tap; `y` = [j]; `h` = [h].
- **Stress: the penultimate cell of each constituent**, with the frame's final
  constituent taking the strongest stress. Chosen by the Transcription Board in the
  1930s for radio intelligibility. **It has no evidential basis and the Board said so.**
- **Two branch traditions.** The *Chicago hand* elides `h` between vowels
  (`sa-hena` → [sa.ˈɛ.na]) and stresses as above. The *Second Archive hand* keeps `h`
  and stresses the final cell of each constituent. Crews identify each other's
  training inside one sentence and have opinions about it.
- **A quiet joke for the linguists.** The Chicago hand's intervocalic h-elision
  independently reproduces correspondence set **R2**. Sennett cited this in 1968 as
  intuitive support for the Reduction. It is not evidence of anything — it is a
  twentieth-century speech habit — and citing it was the worst paragraph she ever
  published. It is still quoted at her.

### 7.3 The annotation layer — confidence, kept out of the substrate

Source-of-knowledge is in the corpus (`sa- wi- ru- po- mu-`). **Confidence is HEL's,
and lives only in HEL's apparatus:**

| | Grade |
| --- | --- |
| **C1** | corroborated — supported by non-linguistic evidence (route behaviour, instrument record) |
| **C2** | secure — regular, paradigmatic, multiply attested, no competing parse in circulation |
| **C3** | provisional — the standard reading; a competing parse exists |
| **C4** | contested — two or more readings actively defended |
| **C5** | opaque — transliterated only; no reading defended |

Plus one flag: **`[F]` Formulary-bearing** — HEL operations currently depend on this
reading.

**`C4 [F]` is the combination that keeps people awake.** It means the institution is
acting on a reading its own specialists are still fighting about. There are 31 such
entries. Handoff §8's prohibited shortcut #18 — "present every translation with equal
certainty" — is answered by a scale that intersects with operational dependency, so
that uncertainty has consequences instead of adjectives.

## 8. Three worked examples

### Example 1 — CS-1 node marking `CS-1/N-0044` · condition `IT` · the commonest string type in the corpus

**Script (schematic).** Compressed spine, cells as notches; direction of the spine is
the direction of the edge; notch side distinguishes envelope from relator material.

```
    ·│││ ─────────────▶───────────  ││·
     wi          kan·t·a·pl          el
```

**Transliteration (STL-3 writes CS-1 solid):**

> **wikantaplel**

**Gloss — Sennett parse** *(this line is theory-laden and the catalog card says so)*

```
wi-  kan-      t-      (a)-  pl-        el
INSTR-traverse-DEPART-(EPEN)-OBLIG.INT-DORMANT
```

**Natural translation (Formulary R-088):**
"Instrument record. An outbound traversable edge from this node; transit only — you
may pass through, you may not terminate here; currently not open."

**Ferreira's competing parse** — same string, different segmentation, because the
epenthesis question (§6.3) cuts here:
`wi-kan-ta-plel` with `-ta` as a surviving CS-2-shaped phase and `plel` unanalyzed;
or, in her preferred treatment, no semantic parse at all. Her Behavioural Concordance
entry reads, in full: **`N-0044 · class E · 214 recorded approaches · 0 traversals.`**

**Confidence:** `C4` overall. **`C1` for the `-el` warrant** — this is the one place
both camps agree, because agreement here is distributional rather than semantic:
strings ending `-el` have never been observed to carry traffic in 214 recorded
approaches over sixty years. Ferreira will not call it "dormant." She will call it
class E. Operationally the crews behave identically, which is why nobody has forced
the issue.

**Canon note.** New assumptions: instrument transcription at nodes (**C, ⚠ exposes
cosmology** — see §2.3 channel 2); the CS-1 template (C); the Behavioural
Concordance as an institution (C, production-only). Path-dependence morphology
realizes canon A ("A→C→B may be required") as an inflection. Nothing here implies
anything about builders; note that the string does not say who marked the node, and
the grammar has nowhere to put it.

### Example 2 — CS-2 inscription `CS-2/OBJ-11`, frame 3 · condition `RO` · **two defensible readings**

**Script (schematic).** Broad spine, cells as drawn branch glyphs; envelope material
written **off-spine** as annotation beside the path; `wesa-` marks the spine with a
counter-tick against the direction of travel.

```
      ┌─ kosila·tani            ┌─ welinu·sowa·rahi
      │                         │
  ────┴──◀╫──  wesa·kanu·ki  ───┴─────────▶────────
                                         ⌐ mu·hesu·nari   (off-spine)
```

**Transliteration:**

> kosilatani | wesakanuki | welinusowarahi | muhesunari

**Gloss:**

```
kosila-tani    wesa-kanu-ki           welinu-sowa-rahi
KOSILA-SAME    CTR-traverse-ARRIVE    WELINU-RESEMBLANT-PROJECTED

mu-hesu-nari
UNATTR-IMPOSSIBLE-PRIOR.EFFECT
```

**Reading A — Sennett / standard archive reading, in the Formulary since 1974**

*Natural:* "From KOSILA itself: a counter-edge arriving at a projected
WELINU-resemblant locus does not exist. Unattributed. And the non-existence is
already in force before departure."

*Operational gloss (Formulary R-114):* **"Reverse transit from KOSILA is not a route.
What your projection calls WELINU is not WELINU. The failure is fixed before you
commit."**

**Reading B — Ferreira**

She denies two things. First, that `mu-` is an evidential at all: she treats it as a
frame-external scope particle taking the whole inscription in its domain. Second,
that `nari` here means "prior effect": she takes it as its linkage value, which
*requires an antecedent frame*. Since frame 3 is the first surviving frame on a
broken object, the inscription is a **fragment**, and HEL has spent fifty years
reading a subordinate clause as a complete statement.

*Natural:* "[antecedent frames lost] — and consequently, over the whole of the
foregoing: there exists no counter-edge arrival at a projected WELINU-resemblant
locus."

*Operational gloss:* **"[…], therefore there is no way back to anything like
WELINU."**

**Why both are defensible.** `mu-` is attested in envelope position (Sennett) and in
inscription-initial position where no envelope is possible (Ferreira, 4 instances).
`nari` is attested as a linkage cell requiring an antecedent (Ferreira) and in 6
frames that are demonstrably inscription-initial, where no antecedent can exist
(Sennett). **Each camp's best evidence is the other camp's counterexample set.** The
object is broken exactly where the question would be settled.

**Why it matters operationally.** The practical warning survives both readings —
which is why it has never been urgent to resolve. But Sennett's reading contains a
*causal* claim ("already in force before departure") that Ferreira says is simply not
in the text, and **HEL's containment class PF ("pre-committed failure"), which
governs procedure at four nodes, was built on it.** If Ferreira is right, PF is a
doctrine derived from a mis-parsed subordinate clause, and four nodes have been
handled under a rule that describes nothing.

**Confidence:** `C4 [F]`. Transliteration `C2`. `wesa-`+`hesu` combination `C1`
(Counter-Edge Constraint, exceptionless in CS-2). `nari` `C4`. `mu-` `C4`.
`-sowa` `C3`. `-rahi` `C3`.

**Canon note.** Realizes canon A directly: one-way topology (`wesa-` cannot take
`hena`), and "return to a familiar coordinate may not be that coordinate" (`-sowa`
plus `-rahi`: what the model projects is not what identity has been established for).
Prohibited shortcut #4 (unrestricted portals / reversible travel) is blocked
grammatically rather than by fiat. **The inscription does not say who forbids, who
warns, or who wrote it, and there is no slot in which it could.** New assumptions:
recovered inscribed portable objects (**C, ⚠ exposes cosmology**); the PF containment
class (C, production-only); the `mu-`/`nari` dispute (C as a dispute; **D as to
truth, permanently**).

### Example 3 — The Custodial Line (the "Third Line") · condition `OT` · spoken, with parity doubling

HEL practice, recorded in Chicago field manuals from at least 1908. CS-2 material with
an **HEL-inserted anchor**: the catalog identifier engraved on the custodian's
credential is spoken into the ANCHOR₁ slot. Purists object to splicing a modern human
identifier into a recovered frame. They have objected since 1908.

**Utterance 1**

> ⟨custodian-ID⟩-mari | temi-lu | ⟨ward-ID⟩-tani | sa-hena

```
ID-mari         temi-lu             WARD-tani     sa-hena
ID-CONGRUENT    sustain-SUSTAINED   WARD-SAME     DIRECT-HOLDS
```

**Utterance 2 (parity)** — one specified cell differs; identical repetition **voids

> ⟨custodian-ID⟩-**naki** | temi-lu | ⟨ward-ID⟩-tani | sa-hena

**Natural translation:** "Standing congruent to the position: a sustaining edge, held

**HEL operational gloss:** **"Custody, not possession. Open, not completed.
Witnessed."**

**Field pronunciation, Chicago hand** (stress = penultimate cell of each constituent;

> [… ˈma.ɾi ‖ ˈtɛ.mi.lu ‖ … ˈta.ni ‖ sa.ˈɛ.na]

**Second Archive hand** (final-cell stress; `h` retained):

> [… ma.ˈɾi ‖ tɛ.mi.ˈlu ‖ … ta.ˈni ‖ sa.hɛ.ˈna]

**The problem nobody has fixed.** The parity substitution changes `-mari`

change of claim, not a checksum bit: utterance 2 asserts that the custodian is *the
same position on a different pass*. Ostrowe's 1998 note: *"We are saying something we
do not mean, twice a week, in every intake room."* No change has been made. The 1908
manual describes the parity rule as **"restored,"** and HEL cannot determine whether
that means recovered or invented.

**Confidence:** transliteration `C2`. `temi-lu` reading `C3`. The ID-splice `C5` — it
is not a reading of anything, it is HEL's own practice. Parity rule provenance `C5`.

**Canon note.** Supports the pin/custodianship working canon (B) without making the
pin an object of power: the pin supplies a *catalog identifier that fits a
grammatical slot*, nothing more. Prohibited shortcut #11 is untouched — no egg, no
container, no key. Prohibited shortcut #12 is answered structurally: **"my ward" is
not expressible in CS-2**, because CS-2 has no possessive construction (§5.6). Rule

ritual, and the manual's word for it is ambiguous in the manual. New assumptions: the

made that the corpus's producers had ethics, customs, or a view about ownership.**

## 9. Stratigraphic depth without naming anyone

The two-system split is the top layer. Beneath it, three further sources of
argued-about variation:

**9.1 CS-2 internal strata (II-a / II-b / II-c).** Distinguished by *measurable*
features, not by vibe:

| | II-a (14 inscr.) | II-b (19) | II-c (8) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cells attested | 41 | 46 | 39 |
| Envelope obligatory | yes | yes | **12% of frames lack one** |
| Warrant INTERDICTED | `taruka` (suppletive) | `taruka` | **`heruka`** — regularized into the `he-` paradigm |
| Median frame length | 6 cells | 9 | 5 |
| Pass-ordinal `kiwa` | absent | 22 tokens | 3 tokens |

**The `taruka` → `heruka` regularization in II-c is the single best diachronic datum
in the corpus.** Analogical levelling of a suppletive high-frequency paradigm member
is one of the most characteristic things real languages do over time, and it cannot
be produced by borrowing, by register variation, or by scribal error. It is *change*.
But: II-c also shows a *smaller* cell inventory and a *breakdown* of the obligatory
envelope, which reads like degradation rather than development. So is II-c late and
decaying, or early and not-yet-formed?

Whether II-a/b/c is **chronology, node-geography, functional register, or transmission
quality** is unresolved and the evidence genuinely underdetermines it: II-c comes
overwhelmingly from two nodes, which is exactly what you would expect from a dialect,
from a late period, from a workshop, or from a bad recording apparatus.

**9.2 CS-1 hands.** Two: **1-N** (notch) and **1-R** (rail). They differ in cell
inventory (1-R lacks `ngu`) and in whether the warrant coda is written at all (1-R
omits it in 40% of strings). Chronology, or two recording apparatuses, or two
transcription conventions inside HEL's own 1930s Board? The Board's records for
1934–1937 were destroyed in a fire the archive describes as "routine."

**9.3 The nine unmatched carried lines.** Oral material matching nothing written.
Degraded CS-2, a third system, or nineteenth-century human confection. Three of the
nine contain cell sequences that violate CS-2 phonotactics, which is either the
strongest evidence for a third system or the strongest evidence that someone made
them up.

**What none of this does.** No stratum is attributed to a people, a period, a place
of origin, or a purpose. The strata are properties of **HEL's evidence**: this many
inscriptions, from these nodes, in this condition, showing these countable
differences. Every explanatory story about them is attributed to a named human
scholar and marked disputed. That is how you get the *feeling* of deep time —
layers, change, levelling, decay, loss — with **zero** cosmological commitment. This
is the architecture's central trick and it is the reason it exists.

## 10. Script concept

A single principle serves both systems and their divergence is itself evidence.
Text is written on a **spine**: a continuous directed stroke that is read along its
direction of travel, so that reading order and traversal order are the same operation
and reversing the page does not produce a readable text. Cells attach to the spine as
**branches**, and the side of the spine on which a branch sits distinguishes anchor
material from relator material, while **envelope material is written off-spine
entirely**, as annotation beside the path — evidentiality and warrant are literally
marginal to the route, which is handoff §7.4's "annotations outside the primary path"
taken at its word. **Junction marks** where one spine meets another encode frame
linkage; a spine that closes on itself is a completed circuit (phase `-ro`), one that
terminates open is a terminus, and a counter-tick against the direction of travel is
`wesa-`. **CS-2 draws full branch glyphs on a broad spine; CS-1 cuts cells as notches
directly into a compressed line** — fast, small, machine-like — and the notch-forms
are recoverable as reduced branch-forms by a consistent geometric rule, which is
Sennett's fourth argument (§6.4) and which Ferreira answers by pointing out that the
rule runs equally well backwards. The **field hand** is neither: a straight
left-to-right rail with tick marks and marginal dots, invented by HEL because a true
spine cannot be drawn quickly in a notebook, and it loses the direction information —
which is why field notes carry an arrow at the top of the page and why crews who
forget it have caused at least one incident. Production cost: **three asset families**
(branch, notch, rail), which is a real number and appears in §11.

## 11. What this architecture is bad at

Written to be used against itself. If the decision-maker discards C, these are the
reasons.

**11.1 It costs roughly 2.2–2.5× a single-system architecture, and the multiplier is
in the wrong place.** Two morphologies is only ~1.8×. The rest is the correspondence
layer, which must be maintained in *lockstep*: change a CS-2 morpheme and you must
re-derive its CS-1 reflex, re-check the seven correspondence sets, re-check whether
the change strengthens or weakens each of the three reconstructions, and update the
dispute literature. **There is no cheap edit in this architecture after Phase 2.**

**11.2 The real failure mode is two half-languages.** Under budget pressure the
temptation will be to build CS-1 thinly, because it is short and formulaic and looks
easy. A thin CS-1 makes the correspondences hollow, and hollow correspondences make
the entire scholarly apparatus a costume. If the project cannot commit to building
CS-1 to genuine depth, **this architecture is worse than a one-system design**, not
better. That is the single strongest argument against C and it should be weighed
before any of its advantages.

**11.3 Audience confusion is real and only partly mitigable.** Two forms for related
concepts, a catalog prefix that does not mean what it says, and a UI that must
constantly signal which system a string belongs to. Mitigations exist (colour-coded
catalog IDs, the notch/branch script distinction is instantly visible, field slang
"tight/long" is short and learnable) but the cognitive load is higher than A or B and
the first hour of exposure is the worst hour.

**11.4 Half the design is unspeakable.** CS-1 as specified — consonant-final,
clustered, fused into single words — is deliberately awkward in the mouth, because a
control notation should be. Any product needing substantial performed alien dialogue
will use CS-2 and the carried lines only, and will get roughly half the design's value
back. If voice is a major deliverable, that halves the return on the extra cost in
11.1.

**11.5 It resists closure, including closure the product may need.** Puzzle design
that requires a unique correct answer fights this architecture at every step, because
the architecture's entire proposition is that correct answers are contested. There
are workarounds — puzzles keyed to `C1`-corroborated material only, where both camps
agree — but that restricts puzzle content to a small, distributional subset. **If
players must be able to solve it (handoff §9.5), C is the weakest of the three.**

**11.6 It requires a curator, permanently.** Two writers answering the same lore
question from different camps will produce contradictory fiction. A one-system design
degrades gracefully under inconsistent authorship; this one produces incoherence that
looks like the *authors* are confused rather than the *scholars*. The mitigation is a
canon ledger that records which camp each published statement came from — real
ongoing overhead, and the failure is invisible until it is embarrassing.

**11.7 The payoff is back-loaded.** The comparative pleasure requires enough exposure
to both systems for the reader to feel the correspondence. A small first release shows
only the cost: two confusing systems and some argumentative footnotes. **C is the
right choice for a long-lived property and the wrong choice for a short one.**

**11.8 The cosmological exposure is concentrated in the corpus model, not the
grammar.** The grammar is exceptionally safe (§2.1). But channels 2 and 3 of §2.3
assert that stream nodes produce recordable phenomena and that inscribed artifacts
exist beyond the Box — both open questions in handoff §9.2. Those two assumptions
carry the whole architecture and cannot be trimmed: without channel 2 there is no
CS-1 corpus, and without channel 3 there is no CS-2 corpus. **If either is refused,
C does not survive in this form.** A/B are less exposed here.

**11.9 Honest scope check.** Phase 2 for this architecture: ~140 CS-2 locus roots,
~30 edge stems, the full inflectional apparatus of both systems, 7 correspondence
sets with attestation counts, 3 CS-2 sub-strata with differentiating features, 2 CS-1
hands, 3 script asset families, ~55 corpus items fully worked, and a dispute
literature with three positions and their evidence. That is a large Phase 2. It is
achievable, and it is not small.

## 13. Prohibited-shortcut check

1. Builders named/described — **no.** No slot in the grammar admits an agent (§2.1).
2. Box fully decipherable — **no.** 42% hapax rate; 31 `C4 [F]` entries; `q` unread.
3. "Black Box" as alien self-designation — **no.** Human operational label throughout; the catalog prefix CS is likewise human and admittedly wrong (§2.2).
4. Unrestricted portals / reversible travel — **no.** Counter-Edge Constraint (§4.4).
5. Grammar erasing one-way topology — **no.** The grammar *is* the topology; §4.4 makes reversibility unassertable.
6. A year identifying a destination — **no.** No numerals, no dates, no tense (§3.2); destination is locus + congruence + projection.
7. HEL as galactic government / military — **no.** HEL appears here as an archive with a funding problem and a deferred re-cataloguing project.
8. HEL omniscient or uniformly correct — **no.** §6.4 is three factions; §4.5 documents a systematic 60-year mistranslation; §7.2 records Sennett's worst paragraph.
9. PNP lore / Culture Codex — **no.** Nothing imported.
10. Standard Egg canonized — **no.** Not mentioned in any corpus item, and none proposed.
11. Pin as egg/soul/key — **no.** The pin supplies a catalog identifier that fits a grammatical slot (Ex. 3).
12. Entities as property — **no.** CS-2 has no possessive construction at all (§5.6), and the argument-from-absence about the producers' ethics is explicitly refused.
13. Entity language established — **no.** Nothing here is attributed to entities.
14. Real Indigenous language material — **no.** None consulted, none used, no "flavour," no texture. The inventory is deliberately unmarked (§7.1). The Chicago anchor appears only as an archive location and no corpus item is attributed to the mound site or to any Indigenous source.
15. Mound = proof of alien authorship — **no.** Never linked.
16. English substitution cipher — **no.** The systems are not isomorphic to English; CS-2 has no tense, no numerals, no possessives, no subordination, and no agent.
17. Faux-Latin for institutional age — **no.** Age is carried by correspondence sets, analogical levelling, a fire in 1934–37, and a name that outlived its meaning.
18. Equal certainty everywhere — **no.** C1–C5 plus `[F]`; per-element ratings in every example.
19. Ritual as definitely magical or definitely meaningless — **no.** §5.8: a checksum with a real mechanism, a correlation marked `nawe`, a manual that says "restored" and cannot be interrogated, and an institution that keeps performing it anyway.

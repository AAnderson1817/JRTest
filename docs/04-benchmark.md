# 04 — The Benchmark

**What this document is:** the standard of judgment for the HEL language project. It
defines what "rivals Tolkien" has to mean given a canon that forbids the conditions
under which Tolkien's languages achieve their effect. It ends in a rubric that governs
every later review round.

**Authority convention in this file.** Claims about the real world — Tolkien's corpus,
other conlangs, the history of decipherment — are ordinary factual claims and carry no
authority label; they are checkable against published sources. Every claim about the HEL
project or its universe carries a level. This document introduces **no** Authority A or B
material. Its design recommendations are **Authority C** throughout and are marked. Where
it touches an open question it says so and leaves it open.

**Notation warning.** This file contains no proposed substrate forms. Where an example is
needed, it uses bracketed placeholders (`⟦R-114⟧`, `[unit-17]`) that are deliberately not
pronounceable and are **not lexical proposals**. Nothing here may be quoted as a form.

---

## 1. What actually makes Quenya and Sindarin succeed

The standard praise — "beautiful sounds," "he was a philologist" — explains nothing
transferable. Six mechanisms do the actual work.

### 1.1 The root system is a generator, not a wordlist

Common Eldarin roots (√KAL "shine," √MBAR "dwell," √NDOL "head," √KYELEP "silver") are not
words. They are consonantal-vocalic seeds that pass through a shared derivational apparatus
— A-fortification, nasal infixion, suffixal extension, compounding — before any daughter
language's sound laws touch them. The consequence is that Quenya and Sindarin words are
*siblings*, not translations of each other, and they diverge by an amount that a reader can
feel but not immediately compute:

| Root | Quenya | Sindarin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| √MBAR "dwell" | *már, mar-* (Eldamar) | *bar* (Brithombar) |
| √NDOL "head" | *nóla* | *dol* (Mindolluin, Dol Guldur) |
| √KAL "shine" | *calina* "bright" | *calen* "green" |

Three things to extract. First, the initial-cluster reflexes (*mb-* → Q. *m-*, S. *b-*;
*nd-* → Q. *n-*, S. *d-*) are *regular*, so a reader who has seen two pairs can predict the
third. The pleasure is the pleasure of a solved puzzle, and it recurs on every page of
names. Second, √KAL → S. *calen* "green" is a **semantic** divergence, not a phonological
one: "bright" became "green." That single word implies a people whose brightness is
foliage. No sentence of lore was required to say it. Third — and this is the mechanism most
projects miss — the daughters are not merely *different*; they are different *by rule*, and
the rule is stateable. Difference without a stateable rule reads as sloppiness. Difference
with a rule reads as history.

### 1.2 The sound-change chain is a timeline you can walk

Sindarin is Welsh-shaped in its morphophonemics: i-affection and a-affection restructure
the vowels, and the initial mutations (lenition, nasal, stop, liquid) are grammatically
load-bearing — a mutation *is* the marking of a syntactic relation. Quenya has syncope,
cluster simplification, and the *þ* > *s* merger. The important point is not that these
exist but that they are **ordered**, so that a form's shape encodes *when* in the chain it
entered the language. A late loan looks late. An archaism looks archaic. The corpus
therefore contains its own stratigraphy: you can date a word by looking at it. This is what
produces the sensation of depth. Not age asserted — age *legible in surface form*.

### 1.3 Sound change is used as plot

Two exhibits, and they are the two most important things Tolkien did for this project.

**The Shibboleth.** The Noldorin merger of *þ* into *s* was resisted by Fëanor, whose mother
was Míriel *Þerindë*. He treated the change as a slight to her memory and to himself,
bound up with his father's second marriage. A regular phonological merger becomes a
factional badge, a family grievance, and a marker of who you stand with. This is real
sociolinguistics — shibboleths work exactly this way — and it is the only place in the
legendarium where a sound law has a body count.

**The Doriath ban.** Thingol, learning of the Kinslaying at Alqualondë, forbade Quenya in
his realm. Quenya became a lore-tongue among the Exiles; Noldorin princes took Sindarin
names (Findaráto → Finrod, Nelyafinwë Maitimo → Maedhros). A **language policy** — an act of
state, with a moral cause and an enforcement mechanism — reshapes an entire dialect
landscape. Nothing else in fantasy linguistics comes close, and note what it costs: one
paragraph of narrative.

Also note the inverse move: Quenya *telpë* "silver" should regularly be *tyelpë*; Tolkien
explains the irregular form as Telerin influence, because the Teleri were the silversmiths.
A phonological exception is justified by a **contact history and a craft economy**. An
irregularity that carries an explanation is worth more than ten regularities.

### 1.4 Etymology is a form of memory, and memory is the theme

Túrin's names track his self-understanding: *Neithan* "the wronged," *Gorthol* "dread helm,"
*Agarwaen* "bloodstained," *Mormegil* "black sword," finally *Turambar* "master of doom" —
and the epitaph inverts it: *Túrin Turambar turún' ambartanen*, "master of doom by doom
mastered." The wordplay *is* the tragedy; you cannot state the tragedy more compactly in
English.

The general principle is bigger than the example. Tolkien's legendarium is *about* memory,
loss, and the persistence of the old inside the new. A word that carries its history in its
shape is the same kind of object as an Elf who carries the First Age into the Third. **The
philology and the theme are one object.** That identity is why the etymologies feel
meaningful rather than clever. It is the single most important thing to steal, and §4.14
states its HEL form.

A related technique: one referent, many names, each encoding a namer's viewpoint —
Orodruin / Amon Amarth / Mount Doom; Taur-nu-Fuin / Dorthonion; Gandalf / Mithrandir /
Olórin / Tharkûn / Incánus. The name inventory of a thing is a map of who has had to deal
with it. HEL's brief already demands this in §7.5 (catalog / field / recovered / historical
layers); recognize that it is a Tolkien technique and hold it to Tolkien's standard.

### 1.5 The reader is made to do linguistic work

*Ennyn Durin Aran Moria: pedo mellon a minno.* The audience decodes a door. The famous
mistranslation is a **grammar** joke (imperative vs. quotation of a password) and the reader
gets to feel the click. The lesson is participatory: an audience that has personally derived
a reading owns it in a way that no amount of exposition can produce. Depth felt is depth
worked for.

### 1.6 Emotional weight is loaned from the story and repaid in compression

*Ai! laurië lantar lassi súrinen* works on readers who cannot parse a word of it, because by
the time they hear it the book has taught them what fading costs. Afterwards, the phrase
carries that meaning by itself, more compactly than English could. This is a two-stage
transaction — the story lends the words weight, then the words hold it — and it only closes
if the same forms **recur** across the corpus. Tolkien's corpus is small but extraordinarily
dense in cross-reference: a root appears in a name in one book, a song in another, a
footnote in a letter. **Density of internal cross-reference within a bounded corpus, not
corpus size, is what generates the impression of a whole language.** A hundred-word lexicon
used six times each will feel deeper than a thousand-word lexicon used once each.

## 2. Tolkien's real weaknesses

Stated precisely, because each one is a place this project can beat him.

**Incompleteness, of the worst kind.** There is no finished grammar of Quenya by Tolkien.
The verb system is reconstructed by scholars from scattered late manuscripts. Nobody can
compose a sentence with confidence. Crucially, this is *extradiegetic* incompleteness — the
author ran out of life — and it is experienced as frustration, not mystery. §4.1 turns on
this distinction.

**Unstable revision, unconsolidated.** The "Qenya" of 1915 is not the Quenya of 1968.
Etymologies were rewritten, sometimes reversing themselves; the 1950s recasting of
"Noldorin" as Sindarin retroactively changed a language's identity and speaker population.
Every citation has to be dated, and forms from different decades do not compose. The
corpus is not one system, and the incoherence is authorial, not designed.

**Thin syntax, near-absent pragmatics.** Tolkien worked on phonology, historical morphology,
and lexicon. Word order, subordination, relative clauses, information structure, discourse
particles, politeness, repair, conversational management — sparse to absent. The languages
support inscriptions and lyric; they do not support a conversation.

**Human phonology, European semantics.** The inventories are Finnish, Welsh, Latin, Greek —
a deliberate and successful aesthetic choice, but it means the alienness is zero and was
never sought. Semantically the lexicon is near-isomorphic to English: few obligatory
categories English lacks, no evidentiality, no obviation, no unusual alignment, no
untranslatable grammatical requirements. Elvish thinks like a European philologist, because
one made it. **This is the single largest opening for the HEL project**, whose canon supplies
four or five categories English genuinely lacks (§4.10).

**Almost no sociolinguistics.** The Shibboleth and the Doriath ban are brilliant *because
they are the exceptions*. Otherwise: no registers, no class dialects, no child language, no
slang, no taboo, no disfluency, no scribal error, no orthographic variation, no spelling
reform, no one speaking badly. Everything is in high style. Elves do not mumble.

**No administrative corpus.** Tolkien's material is overwhelmingly poetic and onomastic.
Real corpora of dead languages are overwhelmingly *bureaucratic* — inventories, receipts,
ration lists. This is not a small point of texture; §4.5 shows it is a primary source of
both decipherability and pathos, and HEL is an archive-keeping institution, which means the
opening is already built into the canon.

**No modeled misunderstanding.** Tolkien's in-world loremasters are essentially correct.
There is no substantial in-world tradition of failed decipherment, no deprecated readings,
no scholar who was wrong for eighty years. He never needed one. HEL cannot function without
one (§4.7), and that is an advantage, not a handicap.

## 3. The serious competition, and what transfers

**Okrand — Klingon (1985).** Built to be maximally un-English on purpose: OVS basic order,
a phoneme inventory selected for asymmetry (retroflex *D*, uvular *Q*, the affricate *tlh*,
glottal stop as a full consonant), no copula, and a noun-class system realized in the
plural suffixes — *-pu'* for beings capable of language, *-Du'* for body parts, *-mey* for
everything else — which is a cultural claim smuggled into inflection. Okrand also wrote the
grammar as an *in-universe artifact*, hedged in the voice of a fallible human field
linguist ("some speakers say…"), which let later canon changes be absorbed as corrections
rather than retcons. **Transferable:** deliberate structural asymmetry as the source of
alienness; a grammatical category that encodes an ontology; and the in-character fallible
describer, which is precisely HEL's four-layer model. Klingon also proved that a community
will use a language hard enough to find every gap — build for use, and treat gaps as
answerable in character.

**Peterson — Dothraki, High Valyrian (2009–).** The best production discipline in the field.
He derived his languages *backwards from a tiny inviolable canon* — *khaleesi*, *arakh*,
*valar morghulis* — treating the author's handful of words as data that the rules must
generate. He then built internal diachrony: High Valyrian's four-gender system and full case
paradigm eroding into the Bastard Valyrians through realistic sound change and paradigm
collapse, so the daughter varieties are *derived*, not invented. He shipped on schedule with
recordings and actor coaching. **Transferable:** (a) prior fragments are data you must
derive, never overwrite — directly applicable if any HEL string already exists in an asset;
(b) generate variants by attrition of a parent rather than by inventing siblings, which is
exactly the mechanism the brief's "corpus strata" idea needs; (c) a dated, versioned
canonical wordlist, without which a multi-year project decoheres.

**Frommer — Na'vi (2005–).** Ejective stops, a tripartite-flavored case system (agentive
*-l*, patientive *-ti*), and — the important part — **infixation as the primary verbal
morphology**: tense/aspect, mood, and speaker attitude are inserted *inside* the verb root at
fixed positions. Na'vi feels alien while remaining pronounceable by actors on a set,
because the strangeness lives in the morphological architecture rather than in the sound
inventory. **Transferable:** non-concatenative morphology is the cheapest genuine alienness
available; it survives transliteration into Latin letters, which matters enormously for a
project whose surface layer is a human transliteration standard.

**Elgin — Láadan (1982).** A hypothesis-testing language: could a language built to encode
women's perceptions change what is easy to say? Two mechanisms are directly usable.
Sentence-initial **speech-act particles** (*bíi* declarative, *báa* interrogative) that scope
the whole utterance, and sentence-final **obligatory evidentials** — *wa* known by perception,
*wi* self-evident, *we* perceived in a dream, *wáa* believed because the source is trusted,
*wo* imagined or hypothetical. Also deliberate lexicalization of distinctions the culture
must make (*radíidin*, a "non-holiday": a festival that is labor disguised as celebration).
**Transferable:** obligatory, utterance-scope evidentiality — which is not decoration in
Láadan but the spine — and the practice of lexicalizing a distinction *because the speaker
community cannot function without it*. **Cautionary:** Láadan was built to prove a thesis and
reads, at times, like a lecture. A language that argues loses the ability to simply be.

**Quijada — Ithkuil (1978–).** The most ambitious semantic engineering ever attempted: every
formative obligatorily specifies configuration, affiliation, perspective, extension, essence,
and context, among ~90 cases and a large affix inventory — including a **validation**
category that is evidentiality in all but name. Ithkuil's real contribution is the
demonstration that **grammar can be an ontology**: the obligatory categories *are* a theory of
what kinds of things exist and how a mind may stand toward them. **Transferable:** exactly
that — design HEL's obligatory categories as a claim about what the system's users had to
distinguish. **Cautionary, and severe:** Ithkuil is unspeakable, unlearnable, has no corpus
with emotional weight and no community. Precision without warmth, and no one to talk to.
Take the category design; take none of the density.

**Brown / LLG — Loglan, Lojban (1955 / 1987).** Syntactically unambiguous and machine
parsable, with two features worth stealing outright. First, **place structure**: every root
predicate is defined by its numbered argument frame (x1 does x2 to x3 under conditions x4),
not by an English gloss. Defining a lexicon this way makes vagueness impossible to hide and
is the correct discipline for HEL's route, traversal, and custodianship predicates —
"traverse" is meaningless until you say how many places it takes and what they are. Second,
Lojban handles **spatial and temporal reference with the same machinery**: the same slot
system takes distance, direction, and interval markers for time and for space, so a
"tense" can be a composite spatiotemporal specification. That is a working, tested,
pre-existing model for the canon's composite event-location requirement (handoff §6.2), and
this project should study it before designing that grammar. **Cautionary:** Lojban's goal —
eliminating ambiguity — is the inverse of this project's, whose central asset is *productive*
ambiguity. The lesson is not to eliminate ambiguity but to **know exactly which ambiguities
you have licensed and where they live**, which Lojban's formal grammar is the only
demonstration of anyone doing rigorously.

## 4. The hard problem

**The apparent contradiction.** Tolkien's languages feel deep because the system is (in
intent) complete and its history is known. HEL canon requires a corpus that is fragmentary
and resistant to translation, produced by builders who are Authority D — unknown,
undescribed, and to remain so. It looks as though the project has been forbidden the two
conditions that produce the effect it is chasing.

It has not. The conditions are not the effect. The rest of this section identifies the
mechanisms that actually generate depth, shows that real fragmentary corpora generate more
of it than complete ones do, and states the design rules that follow. This is the
intellectual core of the project; everything downstream is bookkeeping.

### 4.1 Diegetic vs. extradiegetic absence

The first and most important distinction. Two corpora can have an identically shaped hole
— say, no attested paradigm for a verb class — and one hole is fascinating while the other
is annoying. The difference is **who is responsible for the hole.**

Tolkien's missing Quenya verb paradigm is an *extradiegetic* absence: the author died. It
produces frustration and fan reconstruction that everyone knows is fan reconstruction. The
missing Minoan lexicon is a *diegetic* absence: the past withheld it. It produces a
century of scholarship.

The distinguishing test is not sincerity but **whether the shape of the gap is itself
informative.** Linear B contains almost no literary text, and we know why: the tablets are
one administrative year's records, unbaked clay accidentally fired in the destruction of
the palaces that stored them. That explanation converts the absence from a defect into
evidence — it tells you about the recording practice, the archive, and the fire. An
absence with a stated cause is content.

> **Design rule 1 (Authority C).** Every gap in the published corpus must have an in-world
> cause that is *itself* stated in the material. A gap whose cause cannot be stated is not a
> mystery; it is unfinished work. See failure mode F2 (§6).

### 4.2 The verifiable foothold — the single most important transfer

Every real breakthrough came from a place where a guess could be **checked**. Egyptian had
the Rosetta Stone; Old Persian and Akkadian had Behistun; Ugaritic had a thirty-sign
inventory that implied an alphabet, a living Semitic comparative background, and short
formulaic inscriptions (bronze adzes reading, in effect, "chief of priests") — deciphered in
roughly two years by Bauer, Dhorme, and Virolleaud, with an abecedary tablet later
recovered that confirmed the sign order outright. Hrozný cracked Hittite on one sentence —
*nu NINDA-an ezzatteni watar-ma ekutteni* — where a Sumerogram supplied "bread" and the verbs
resembled Indo-European "eat" and "drink," yielding "now you will eat bread, and you will
drink water." A ration formula. Not a poem.

The cleanest case is Linear B. Alice Kober's inflectional "triplets" established that the
language had case morphology before anyone knew a single value. Ventris built the grid — a
table of which signs alternate in which positions — and then guessed that certain frequent
words were Cretan place names: *ko-no-so* Knossos, *a-mi-ni-so* Amnisos, *tu-ri-so* Tylissos.
Values fell out. Then Blegen sent him a newly excavated Pylos tablet (PY Ta 641) on which
the syllabic spelling *ti-ri-po-de* stood beside a **drawing of a tripod**, and other entries
matched their own ideograms by handle count. The picture confirmed the reading. Skeptics
converted.

That tablet is the model. It is the moment where an interpretation stops being an opinion
because the corpus contains its own answer key for one item.

Contrast the negative case. The Voynich manuscript has language-like statistics —
Zipf-like frequency behavior, constrained word structure, Currier's "A" and "B" systems —
and vellum radiocarbon-dated to the early fifteenth century, which forecloses the easy
hoax theories without confirming any content. It has attracted an unbroken procession of
solutions since Newbold's microscopic-shorthand theory of 1921 (demolished by Manly in
1931) through the recent proposals that specialists rejected within weeks. It generates
enormous *amateur* energy and near-zero scholarly yield, for one reason: **nothing can be
confirmed or refuted, so no result accumulates.** Its cultural energy is uncoupled from its
intellectual yield, and serious scholarship has largely ceded it.

> **Design rule 2 (Authority C).** The corpus must contain verifiable footholds: items whose
> reading can be confirmed or refuted by evidence internal to the published material. Target
> the Linear B / Etruscan condition. Do **not** target the Voynich condition. Ambiguity
> without a mechanism for adjudication is not productive ambiguity; it is noise, and
> audiences and reviewers can tell within about two hours.

The HEL form of the tripod tablet is available and canon-safe: a fragment whose referent HEL
has *independently observed* — a route that behaved as the fragment described, an arrival
displacement that matched a notation. The reading is confirmed by the world, not by a
translation key, so nothing about the builders is revealed. (Authority C; the specific
mechanism is for Phase 3.)

### 4.3 Structure is visible before meaning

What convinces a scholar that there is a language there at all is statistical and
positional, not semantic: sign-frequency distributions, positional constraints, repeated
strings, morphological alternation — the same stem with varying endings in correlated
environments. Kober's triplets were pure distribution. The grid was built with no values in
it. Linear A, though undeciphered, can be **read aloud** approximately, because it shares
signs with Linear B; we can pronounce the recurring libation formula on the stone tables
(*a-sa-sa-ra-me* and its companions) without knowing what any of it means.

This is the specific experience the HEL corpus must produce, and it has a hard engineering
consequence. A corpus assembled by sprinkling glyphs will not support it — distributional
analysis will find nothing, or will find the author's habits. The corpus must have real
structure whether or not anyone reads it.

> **Design rule 3 (Authority C).** Generate the published corpus by *rendering* it from the
> complete internal grammar, then apply a documented damage/attrition model. Never author
> damaged text directly. Consistency must be a mathematical property of the pipeline, not a
> matter of authorial care.

### 4.4 The Etruscan condition: readable, not understood

Etruscan is the closest real-world analogue to what HEL canon describes, and the project
should treat it as the primary reference case.

Its alphabet is Euboean Greek-derived, so the phonetic values are secure: **anyone can read
Etruscan aloud.** Understanding is another matter. It is an isolate (with Lemnian and Raetic
as probable relatives), attested in something on the order of ten thousand-plus
inscriptions that are overwhelmingly short and funerary — *mi larθia*, "I [am] of Larth";
the recurring tomb formulae — plus a small number of longer texts. Morphology is
substantially understood; the lexicon runs to a couple of hundred confident items. The
Pyrgi Tablets (gold, c. 500 BCE, Etruscan with a Phoenician parallel text) gave a partial
bilingual anchor. The Tabula Cortonensis turned out to be a land transaction. And the
longest text of all, the *Liber Linteus Zagrabiensis* — roughly twelve hundred words of a
ritual calendar — survives because the linen book was cut into strips and used to wrap an
Egyptian mummy, and was not recognized for what it was until the nineteenth century.

Four things to take:

1. **Readable-but-not-understood is a stable, generative epistemic state.** It sustained two
   centuries of scholarship. It is exactly HEL's four-layer model: field pronunciation
   exists (layer 3) and meaning does not (layer 4).
2. **The Liber Linteus is the single best model for a HEL flagship text**: long, formulaic,
   ritual, morphologically transparent, semantically opaque, useless for narrative
   exposition and inexhaustible for analysis.
3. **The transmission story is half the fascination.** A ritual book survives as mummy
   wrapping. Nobody forgets that. Which leads to §4.6.
4. **Morphology can be known while lexicon is unknown**, and that asymmetry is more
   interesting than either total ignorance or total knowledge, because it lets you *parse*
   a sentence you cannot read — you can see how many arguments a predicate takes, which
   elements are inflected together, where the boundaries fall — and every parse constrains
   the possible meanings without fixing one.

### 4.5 Formulae, and the melancholy of the administrative register

Fragmentary corpora are dominated by repetition. Linear B tablets are overwhelmingly
"place-name, commodity ideogram, numeral." Etruscan is overwhelmingly "this is the tomb of
X, son of Y, who lived Z years." Formulaic density does two jobs at once.

Operationally, it makes partial decipherment possible: you can read the **frame** long before
you can read the **content**, and the frame tells you what kind of document you are holding.
This is the traction that Design rule 2 requires, delivered cheaply.

Emotionally, it produces a very specific and very powerful sadness. We know the Mycenaean
words for sheep, chariot wheels, and unguent boilers. We do not have one line of their
poetry. The receipts survived; the songs did not. **The absence of the poetry is felt
precisely because the receipts are there** — the mundane surviving material measures the
size of what is gone. Nothing in Tolkien produces this effect, because Tolkien has no
receipts.

HEL is an archive-keeping institution operating under compartmentalization. Its recovered
material would plausibly be dominated by route labels, warnings, authorizations, and
inventory marks. **This is an asset, not a limitation.** (Authority C.)

### 4.6 Taphonomy: why *these* items and not others

A corpus is a biased sample, and in every real case the bias is legible and is part of the
object's meaning. Linear B survives because unfired clay was baked by the fires that
destroyed the palaces — we have one year of records because that was the year the archive
burned. Ugaritic survives because a burned archive at Ras Shamra was excavated in 1929. The
Liber Linteus survives because of Egyptian funerary reuse. Rongorongo survives on roughly
two dozen objects because that is what escaped nineteenth-century destruction on Rapa Nui,
after slave raiding and epidemic collapse severed the chain of people who could read them.

In each case the survival mechanism explains the register, the length, the condition, and
the gaps *simultaneously*. A fragmentary corpus without a taphonomy reads as arbitrary — the
author simply chose to write short texts.

> **Design rule 4 (Authority C).** The project must specify a taphonomy: the physical and
> informational reasons these particular items exist in HEL's hands and others do not, with
> consequences visible in item length, register, condition, and distribution across strata.
> This is what separates a corpus from a wordlist.

**Prohibition #14 note.** Rongorongo is cited here, as the task requires, strictly as a
precedent for *epistemic structure* — undeciphered script, severed transmission chain, tiny
object count, a cycle of claimed readings. No Rapa Nui material, and no material from any
real Indigenous language or writing tradition, may enter the design in any form: not
vocabulary, not glyph shapes, not phonological texture, not "inspired-by" rhythm. The same
absolute prohibition covers the Chicago mound connection (handoff §3.2). The precedents in
this section supply *methodology only*.

### 4.7 The proper-name wedge

Names are the crowbar in nearly every decipherment. Ventris got in through Cretan
toponyms. Etruscan is known primarily through personal names, which is why its onomastics
and kinship terminology are better understood than its verbs. Names work because they are
**referentially anchored but semantically shallow**: you can fix a phonetic value or a
morphological pattern from a name without understanding a single content word.

The HEL analogue is direct and canon-safe. HEL knows the *referents* of some items — nodes
it has traveled to, arrivals it has recorded, artifacts it holds — without knowing their
*meanings*. That produces the Linear B situation exactly: material that is readable and
identifiable but not understood, and that leaks nothing about the builders, because knowing
that a string designates a node you have visited tells you nothing about who wrote it or
why. (Authority C.)

### 4.8 Contested reading as ongoing drama — the substitute for diachrony

This is the mechanism that replaces Tolkien's Common Eldarin, and it deserves to be
understood as a *substitution*, not a consolation prize.

Fragmentary corpora generate scholarly communities, and scholarly communities generate
people. Arthur Evans's institutional insistence that the Minoan language was not Greek
shaped the field for a generation and was overturned only after his death, by an architect
working in his evenings. Hrozný announced Hittite as Indo-European in 1915 against
widespread skepticism and was vindicated. Kober died before she could be proved right.
Knorozov's phonetic approach to Maya was resisted for Cold War reasons as much as
scholarly ones, and Proskouriakoff's demonstration that the inscriptions recorded dynastic
history reorganized an entire discipline. Rongorongo has a documented cycle of claimed
decipherments, of which one — the lunar calendar on the Mamari tablet — is broadly accepted
and the rest are not. The Voynich has a century of failures, each of which added to the
object's aura.

**The history of attempts becomes part of the object.** You cannot study Linear B without
studying Ventris; the decipherment is inseparable from the corpus.

Tolkien's languages carry diachrony *in the object*: the word remembers its history.
HEL's languages cannot do that without a known past, so HEL relocates diachrony **from the
object to the observer**: the corpus does not have a known history, but the *reading of the
corpus* does — dated, attributed, contested, superseded, and populated by people who can be
wrong. That history is fully ours to write, requires no builders, exposes no cosmology, and
has one advantage over Tolkien's model that should be stated plainly: **his loremasters are
right, and ours can be wrong.** Error is a renewable resource. A superseded reading is a
character, a method, a bias, and a specific piece of evidence that killed it — four
narrative objects for the price of one footnote.

> **Design rule 5 (Authority C).** The reading history is a first-class deliverable, not
> flavor. Every disputed item ships with named analysts, dates, methods, institutional
> affiliation within HEL, the evidence for each reading, and — where a reading has been
> superseded — the specific evidence that killed it. At least one live dispute must remain
> unresolved in the shipped material, and the project must not know the answer either.

### 4.9 Stratification: "is it even one system?"

Linear A and Linear B share much of a sign repertoire; one is read and the other is not,
and their exact relationship is itself a research question. Cypro-Minoan is subdivided into
CM1/CM2/CM3 on typological grounds, and whether those are one script or several is
genuinely contested. The handoff's own §5.1 lists "several systems incorrectly grouped
together" as a live in-world possibility.

The mechanism this creates is the most valuable long-run asset available to the project:
**when the corpus may not be one system, every new fragment can reorganize the entire
picture.** A single item can promote a hypothesis, demote another, or split the corpus. This
generates indefinite renewal *without adding a single fact about the builders*, because
"these two strata may be unrelated" is a claim about HEL's classification, not about
anyone's history.

The brief's "corpus strata" instinct (§4 of `00-BRIEF.md`) is correct and this is why. The
strata must differ **systematically** — by rule, per §1.1 — so that the competing readings
(chronological change / functional register / dialect / unrelated systems) are all
*genuinely* available on the evidence. If one reading is obviously right, the mechanism is
dead.

### 4.10 Resistance to translation, engineered rather than asserted

"Resistant to translation" must not be implemented as "we didn't define it." There is a
real linguistic mechanism, and it is available.

Translation loses information when the source has **obligatory grammatical categories the
target lacks**. If a language obligatorily marks evidential source on every predication, an
English translation must either drop that information or add a clause that the original did
not have as a clause. Every rendering is then provably lossy, and the loss can be
*displayed*: source form, gloss, English, and a stated residue.

HEL's canon supplies these categories already, which is remarkable and should be
recognized as the project's largest structural advantage over Tolkien:

- composite event-location as a single obligatory coordinate, where English requires
  separate "where" and "when" (handoff §6.2)
- directed traversal, distinguishing permitted motion, hypothetical reverse motion,
  arrival, departure, intermediate traversal, and recurrence at a similar-but-not-identical
  coordinate (§6.1)
- evidential source and confidence as obligatory rather than optional (§6.4)
- causal status under loop constraint, including correlation explicitly refused as causal
  (§6.5)
- custodial relation, which cuts the English possessive into possession / temporary custody
  / authorized access / responsibility for care / containment without ownership (§6.6)
- negation split four ways: impossible, forbidden, unavailable, unverified (handoff §7.3)

Each of these is a category English cannot express without adding words, and several
cannot be expressed at all without changing what is asserted. Build them as **obligatory**,
and the corpus becomes genuinely untranslatable in a demonstrable, teachable way — not
untranslatable because it is vague.

A second device, complementary and cheap: the **no-gloss zone**. A set of recurring units
for which HEL has solid distributional evidence — position, frequency, co-occurrence
constraints — and *no semantic hypothesis at all*, carried in the documentation under
catalog identifiers rather than glosses (`⟦R-114⟧`, not "*probably* 'seal'"). Real corpora
look exactly like this. A corpus in which every unit has a gloss, even a hedged one, is a
corpus that has been solved by its author. (Authority C.)

### 4.11 Ambiguity that is productive vs. ambiguity that is empty

The distinction the rubric must enforce:

**Productive ambiguity** = two or more *fully specified* readings, each internally coherent
with the entire grammar, each with *different operational consequences*, where the evidence
that would discriminate them is identified and its absence explained. Example shape: a
warning that reads either as "this edge cannot be traversed" or "this edge must not be
traversed" — impossible versus forbidden — where the difference decides whether an
expedition is a physics problem or a disciplinary one, and where the discriminating
morpheme is damaged on every surviving attestation. That ambiguity does work: it generates
in-world policy, faction, risk, and story, and it can be resolved later by new material.

**Empty ambiguity** = "meaning uncertain." No candidate readings, no consequence, nothing
to argue about, nothing that could ever settle it. This is the Voynich failure mode
(§4.2), and it is what a project produces when it mistakes withholding for depth.

The test is mechanical and belongs in review: *for each ambiguous item, name the readings,
name the consequence of each, name the evidence that would decide, and say why it is
missing.* Items that cannot survive that test are defects.

### 4.12 The reader's labor, quantified

Across every case — Tolkien's *mellon*, Ventris's toponyms, an amateur staring at the
Voynich — fascination is proportional to the work the audience personally does, and to the
probability that the work will pay off. Two conditions must hold together:

1. The material must be **tractable in part**: published transliteration, enough repetition
   to support pattern-finding, and at least one confirmable reading derivable from public
   material alone, without insider documents.
2. The payoff must be **real**: a correct derivation must be confirmable, and a wrong one
   refutable.

Condition 1 without condition 2 produces the Voynich. Condition 2 without condition 1
produces a puzzle box with the answer printed on the lid. Both together produce a field.

### 4.13 Depth without an author: the Builder-Inference Test

The remaining worry: can a language be deep if we may never say who made it? Yes, and the
reason is structural. **A grammar is evidence of a mind without being a portrait of one.**
Intricacy is a property of the system; biography is a claim about a person. The two are
separable, and the entire discipline of this project consists in keeping them separate.

Every temptation to explain *why* the system is shaped as it is must be answered in-world
by **competing HEL hypotheses**, never by narration. HEL may speculate; the document may not
adjudicate.

The operational test, to be run on every feature and every review round:

> **Builder-Inference Test.** Take the published deliverables and only those. Attempt to
> write a coherent profile of the builders — sensory modality, body plan, number of
> manipulators, environment, social organization, timescale, motive — supported by the
> material better than by chance. If a coherent profile can be written, the design leaks and
> the leaking features must be identified and hedged with at least two competing in-world
> explanations, of which at least one must be non-biological (mechanical, procedural,
> artifactual, or "this is HEL's analytic artifact, not a property of the source").

Leaks are rarely single violations. They accumulate: a phoneme inventory implying a vocal
tract, a numeral base implying digits, a spatial deixis implying a body axis, a color term
implying an eye, a kinship term implying reproduction. Each is defensible alone. Ten of
them are a species profile. See failure mode F1 (§6).

Note the specific trap this project is most exposed to: treating the **field pronunciation**
(layer 3) as evidence about the source. Whether the substrate was ever spoken biologically
is Authority D (handoff §5.3). HEL's pronunciation convention is a *human* artifact adopted
for repeatability, and any design that quietly promotes it into a reconstruction of alien
speech has resolved an open question by accident. Keep layer 3 explicitly HEL's, and give
it at least one competing tradition and at least one unit whose pronunciation is disputed,
so that its human authorship is visible on the page.

### 4.14 The resolution, stated compactly

Tolkien's depth = **completeness of the system** + **knownness of its history** + **weight
loaned by the legendarium**. HEL replaces each term:

| Tolkien | HEL substitute | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complete system, visible | Complete system, **detectable** — every fragment obeys hidden rules, so the more you look the more coherent it gets | §4.3, Design rule 3 |
| Known history of the language | **Known history of the reading** — dated, attributed, contested, superseded; diachrony relocated from object to observer | §4.8, Design rule 5 |
| Weight loaned by narrative | Weight loaned by **provenance** — circumstances of recovery, not semantics | §4.15 |

The first substitution is the one that carries the whole load, so state it precisely: the
audience must be able to **detect** that a complete system exists even where they cannot see
it. Detection comes from consistency under test — from the fact that every fragment they
examine turns out to obey rules they can partly infer, and that no fragment ever violates
them. The audience's confidence that *there is something down there* is the entire game,
and it is destroyed permanently by a single form that breaks the hidden grammar. One
inconsistency does not cost one point of quality; it costs the premise.

And the deepest transfer from §1.4: in Tolkien, the philology and the theme are the same
object — a word that carries its history is the same kind of thing as an Elf who carries
the First Age. **HEL's theme is stewardship under radical uncertainty. Therefore HEL's
linguistics must be *about* uncertainty**: obligatory evidentiality, contested reading,
provisional gloss, the four-way negation, the no-gloss zone, the deprecated interpretation
retained in the archive. If the structure of the language and the structure of the theme
are the same object, depth is not something the project has to add. It is what the project
is made of. That is the target.

### 4.15 Provenance is the emotional payload

The final mechanism, and the one most likely to be neglected because it looks like
metadata.

The elegiac charge of Linear B does not come from the tablets' content. Their content is
sheep. It comes from knowing that the scribe recorded a routine delivery and that the
palace burned before the year was out, and that the fire is the only reason we can read
the sheep at all. Meaning did not create the feeling; **circumstance did.**

This is available to HEL in full, requires no builders, and is compatible with total
semantic opacity. A phrase recited at departure by expeditions, three of which did not
return. A warning misread once, with a recorded consequence. A custodianship formula whose
second clause is physically missing from every attestation. In each case the emotional
weight attaches to the *circumstances of recovery and use*, not to the translation, and it
survives — is often stronger — when the meaning is unknown.

> **Design rule 6 (Authority C).** Every corpus item carries a provenance line: find
> context, medium, condition, recovery date, chain of custody, and use history. That line is
> where the feeling lives. Emotional effect must never be produced by writing a grandiose
> English gloss; a gloss that has to sound impressive is a gloss covering for a fragment
> that does not matter.

## 5. The rubric

**Scope.** Scored 1–5 on each criterion, weighted, summed to 100. This rubric governs every
review round from Phase 1 through Phase 4.

**Interpolation rule (applies to every criterion).** Levels 1, 3, and 5 are defined
behaviorally below. Score **2** when the deliverable meets level 1 but exhibits at least one
level-3 behavior. Score **4** when it meets level 3 and shows no more than one of the
deficiencies that level 5 excludes. Do not invent intermediate reasoning beyond this rule.

**Gate 0 — Prohibited Shortcuts.** Before scoring, check the deliverable against the
nineteen prohibited shortcuts (`00-BRIEF.md` §3). Any violation is a **blocking defect**:
record it, stop, return the deliverable. Do not score a deliverable that fails Gate 0.

**Gate 1 — Canon gates.** A score of **1** on Criterion 1 or Criterion 2 fails the
deliverable outright regardless of weighted total.

**Gate 2 — Substance cap.** A score of **≤2** on Criterion 4 or Criterion 5 caps the
weighted total at 74 (i.e., cannot be accepted), regardless of arithmetic.

**Thresholds.** ≥85 accept for the phase · 70–84 revise and resubmit · <70 reject.

**Procedure.** Two evaluators score independently without conferring. Any criterion where
scores differ by ≥2 must be reconciled by both parties citing specific line numbers in the
deliverable; if reconciliation fails, the lower score stands and the disagreement is
recorded in `/critique/`. Every score of 1 or 2 must cite at least one specific instance.

**Phase applicability.** P1 = Phase 1 (canon alignment), P2 = specification, P3 = corpus,
P4 = production. A criterion marked "—" for a phase is not scored in that phase and its
weight is redistributed proportionally across the scored criteria.

### C1 · Canon compliance and authority labeling — weight 12

- **5** — Every claim about HEL fact carries an explicit A/B/C/D label at first assertion.
  Every C- and D-level item names at least one alternative that was considered and not
  chosen. Zero instances of plausibility-as-warrant reasoning ("it would make sense
  that…", "naturally the builders would…"). The deliverable includes an explicit list of
  new assumptions with levels, cross-referenced to the canon ledger (handoff §12).
- **3** — Labels present on major claims but absent on ≥3 incidental ones. C-level material
  is labeled but alternatives are not offered. Assumption list missing, or present but
  omitting items visible in the body text.
- **1** — Labels absent, decorative, or applied retroactively to material that was written
  as fact. **Or** any Authority D item is silently resolved. **Or** the deliverable reads as
  though canon settled a matter the ledger leaves open. *(Gate 1: fails the deliverable.)*

### C2 · Builder opacity — weight 10

- **5** — The deliverable passes the Builder-Inference Test (§4.13) with zero leaks: a
  hostile reader given only the published material cannot write a coherent builder profile
  better supported than chance. Every feature that could imply a builder trait carries ≥2
  competing in-world explanations, at least one non-biological. Layer 3 (field
  pronunciation) is explicitly marked as HEL's human convention and is never used as
  evidence about the source.
- **3** — No builder is named or described, but 1–2 features leak weak inferences (a sensory
  modality, a body axis, a numeral base implying digits, a home environment, a lifespan
  scale) without hedging.
- **1** — The builders are named, described, or presupposed. **Or** leaks are numerous enough
  that a coherent profile can be assembled. **Or** the substrate is treated as decisively a
  spoken biological language (resolving handoff §5.3 by assumption). *(Gate 1: fails the
  deliverable.)*

### C3 · Productive ambiguity — weight 9

- **5** — For every item marked ambiguous: ≥2 *fully specified* readings, each internally
  consistent with the whole grammar, each with a *stated and different operational
  consequence*; the evidence that would discriminate them is named and its absence
  explained. At least one historical reading has been falsified by in-corpus evidence and
  is retained as deprecated. At least one dispute is left live, with the project itself not
  holding a private answer.
- **3** — Alternatives are offered but at least one is a token — not worked out to the same
  depth, or carrying no consequence difference from the preferred reading. No falsified
  reading documented.
- **1** — Ambiguity asserted rather than exhibited ("meaning uncertain," "possibly ritual")
  with no candidate readings specified. **Or** every item carries exactly one gloss.

### C4 · Hidden-system integrity — weight 9

- **5** — A complete internal system exists as a file. Every published fragment is *derived*
  from it, and a reviewer can demand the derivation of any published form and receive a rule
  trace. A documented damage/attrition model explains what was removed and why (§4.1,
  Design rule 3). An audit is on record showing zero published fragments in violation of the
  hidden rules. Every published gap maps to a sealed internal entry specifying what is
  withheld.
- **3** — A system exists and most fragments derive from it, but some were authored by hand
  and retrofitted. No damage model. Gaps are asserted rather than backed by sealed entries.
- **1** — Fragments authored directly; the "system" is a style guide; gaps correspond to
  undone work. *(Gate 2: caps total at 74.)*

### C5 · Category mismatch / engineered untranslatability — weight 8

- **5** — ≥3 grammatical categories are **obligatory** in the substrate and unavailable in
  English. Every English gloss states its **residue**: what the translation added or
  dropped. At least one canonical text is demonstrably untranslatable without a choice that
  changes operational meaning, and that demonstration is shown, not claimed. A no-gloss
  zone exists, carried under catalog IDs.
- **3** — Obligatory categories exist and are described, but glosses do not state residue and
  untranslatability is claimed rather than demonstrated. No no-gloss zone, or one that is
  trivially small.
- **1** — Every substrate utterance maps 1:1 onto an English sentence; alienness resides in
  orthography, glyph shape, or vocabulary rather than in structure. *(Gate 2: caps total at
  74.)*

### C6 · Morphosyntactic rigor and productivity — weight 8

- **5** — Rules are productive under test: a reviewer supplies an English gloss and receives
  a derived form with a rule trace. A round-trip test passes — one analyst composes, a
  second glosses independently, the results reconcile. ≥3 multi-clause texts exist with
  defined subordination. Negation distinguishes impossible / forbidden / unavailable /
  unverified as morphologically distinct forms. Predicates are defined by argument frame
  (place structure), not by English gloss.
- **3** — Morphology stated and mostly productive; syntax limited to single clauses;
  subordination undefined or hand-waved; predicates defined by gloss.
- **1** — Word lists with no derivational rules. Examples cannot be extended by a reviewer.
  Forms in the corpus contradict the stated rules.

### C7 · Corpus realism: taphonomy, provenance, register — weight 7

- **5** — A taphonomy is specified and its consequences are visible in the material (item
  length, register, condition, distribution across strata). Every item carries a provenance
  line (§4.15, Design rule 6). Register distribution is skewed toward administrative and
  formulaic material as real corpora are. ≥1 repeated formula is recognizable as a frame
  without translation. Stated corpus size is consistent with what is shown.
- **3** — Provenance present on some items; register mix plausible but unmotivated; no
  taphonomy; formulae present but not exploited.
- **1** — Items float free of find context. Every item is a dramatic inscription; the corpus
  is all high style; no repetition; no stated corpus size or a size inconsistent with the
  material.

### C8 · Reading-history depth — weight 7

- **5** — ≥3 named in-world analysts with dated readings, institutional affiliation within
  HEL, stated method, and identifiable bias. At least one reading superseded, with the
  *specific* evidence that killed it recorded. At least one live disagreement whose
  resolution is not stated anywhere. Deprecated readings retained rather than deleted.
- **3** — Competing readings exist but are unattributed or undated; no analyst has a method
  or a bias; nothing has been superseded.
- **1** — A single authoritative reading. **Or** "scholars disagree" asserted with no
  scholars, no dates, and no evidence.

### C9 · Phonological and transliteration integrity — weight 6

- **5** — Inventory and phonotactics stated with a distributional rationale; every published
  form conforms. Transliteration is an explicit mapping with **documented lossiness** — it is
  stated what the transliteration cannot represent. Field pronunciation is explicitly HEL's
  convention, with ≥1 variant tradition attributed to a different archive branch and ≥1 unit
  whose pronunciation is contested. No apostrophe-as-decoration, no gratuitous clusters, no
  fantasy honorific list.
- **3** — Inventory stated and consistent, but transliteration is treated as transparent
  (no lossiness stated); a single pronunciation tradition with no contested units.
- **1** — Forms violate the stated inventory (≥3 instances), or the inventory is
  unstated. **Or** apostrophe-sprinkling / unpronounceable noise. **Or** the transliteration
  is a cipher of English (prohibited shortcut #16). **Or** faux-Latin used to signal age
  (#17).

### C10 · Layer discipline — weight 6

- **5** — Every citation is labeled with its layer (1 underlying / 2 scholarly
  transliteration / 3 field pronunciation / 4 English gloss). No passage mixes layers
  without marking. Field dialogue never implies decipherment. ≥1 documented case where the
  field form and the scholarly analysis conflict, and the conflict is left unresolved.
- **3** — Layers defined and mostly maintained; ≥2 passages collapse them; no
  field-vs-scholarship conflict is exhibited.
- **1** — Layers declared in front matter and ignored in the body. **Or** characters speak
  fluent substrate. **Or** a gloss is presented as the meaning rather than as HEL's reading.

### C11 · Solvability footholds and audience traction — weight 6

- **5** — An attentive outsider using only published material can derive ≥1 nontrivial
  correct reading, and that path is documented internally as having been tested on someone.
  ≥1 fragment is confirmable against an independently known referent (the HEL analogue of
  the tripod tablet, §4.2). Repetition density and positional constraint are sufficient to
  support distributional inference rather than assertion.
- **3** — Footholds exist but require insider documents to use; repetition present but too
  sparse to support inference; no tested derivation path.
- **1** — Nothing is derivable; no reading can be confirmed or refuted from published
  material; the corpus is decorative. *(This is the Voynich outcome — see F3.)*

### C12 · Emotional charge via provenance — weight 5

- **5** — ≥3 items whose weight derives from circumstances of recovery or use rather than
  from semantics. ≥1 item whose meaning is unknown and whose loss is nevertheless felt. No
  item depends on a grandiose English gloss for its effect; removing the gloss does not
  remove the feeling.
- **3** — Provenance present, but the feeling is carried mainly by the wording of the
  translation.
- **1** — Emotional effect attempted through portentous glosses; provenance absent or
  generic.

### C13 · Production fitness — weight 4

- **5** — Glyphs legible at UI sizes with a stated construction grid; a simplified field
  hand exists alongside the high-concept form; a performer-facing pronunciation guide
  exists; font/asset path specified; naming rules are **generative** (rules that produce
  names) rather than a fixed list; a canon ledger records every approved term with date and
  status.
- **3** — High-concept forms only; no field hand, or no legibility testing at target sizes;
  naming supplied as a list.
- **1** — Unproducible as specified; no guidance for assets, performers, or UI.

### C14 · Aesthetic distinctiveness — weight 3

- **5** — A reader shown three forms can identify the language. Its character is describable
  in non-circular terms (not "alien," not "ancient"). It is not confusable with Elvish,
  Klingon, Valyrian, Na'vi, or generic sci-fi glyphwork, and the distinguishing property is
  structural, not orthographic.
- **3** — Internally consistent but generic; would be mistaken for another well-known
  fictional language by a knowledgeable reader.
- **1** — Derivative, incoherent, or distinctive only through orthographic costume.

## 6. The three most likely ways this project fails

Ranked by probability, each with observable early warnings and a runnable countermeasure.

### F1 — Builder reconstruction by accretion

**The failure.** Not one dramatic violation. A hundred small, individually defensible design
choices that each imply something about the builders and that sum to a portrait: a phoneme
inventory implying a vocal tract, a numeral base implying digits, spatial deixis implying a
body axis, a color term implying an eye, a kinship morpheme implying reproduction, a
politeness distinction implying social hierarchy. Every one passes review in isolation.
Together they are a species profile, which is exactly the error that scrapped the previous
attempt. This is the most likely failure because it is invisible at the level at which
review normally happens.

**Early warnings.** A design rationale that begins "the builders would have needed…". Any
feature justified by function rather than by evidence. Layer 3 pronunciation being
discussed as though it were reconstruction. A reviewer being able to answer a question
about the builders without noticing that they answered it.

**Countermeasures.**
1. **Inference ledger.** For every design decision, record what a clever reader could deduce
   about the builders from it. One line per decision. Reviewable, and the accumulation is
   visible in a way that per-decision review can never be.
2. **Adversarial builder profile, every round.** One reviewer's sole job is to write the
   builders' species profile from the deliverables and argue for it. If they produce
   something coherent, the leak is located by tracing which entries in the inference ledger
   they used, and those features are hedged to the standard of C2 level 5.
3. **Two-explanation requirement.** Any feature that could support a builder inference ships
   with ≥2 competing in-world explanations, at least one non-biological — including the
   always-available option "this is an artifact of HEL's analysis, not a property of the
   source."
4. **Rationale ban.** The design bible may not contain a functional justification for a
   substrate feature. Justifications live in the mouths of named HEL analysts, where they
   are opinions, and where a second analyst can disagree.

**Detected by:** C2 (gate), C1, C10.

### F2 — Fragmentariness as an alibi for thinness

**The failure.** The brief already names this — "a gap must correspond to something we
designed and withheld, never to something we failed to work out" — which means the project
has identified the risk but not yet defended against it. The failure is silent because the
defense and the defect look identical from outside: an absent paradigm is an absent
paradigm. Its most common surface form is **atmosphere over structure**: the project ships
glyphs, a font, moody inscriptions, and a mood board, every example is one clause long,
nobody ever composes a new sentence, and the grammar quietly stops being load-bearing.

**Early warnings.** No example longer than a clause. No one on the team can produce a new
form on request without a day's notice. Two fragments that cannot both be true under any
single grammar. "We're leaving that open" used about something nobody has tried to close.
The lexicon growing faster than the rule set.

**Countermeasures.**
1. **Sealed-withholding register.** Every published gap requires an entry in a sealed
   internal file stating what exists and why it is withheld, with the in-world cause of the
   gap (Design rule 1). A gap without an entry is a defect, not a mystery. The register is
   auditable by count: published gaps must equal sealed entries.
2. **Render-then-damage pipeline.** Generate corpus text from the complete grammar, then
   apply a documented attrition model (Design rule 3). Consistency becomes a property of the
   process. This also makes C4's audit mechanical rather than a matter of reading care.
3. **On-demand derivation.** Any reviewer may hand the team an English gloss and require a
   derived form with a rule trace, within the review session. Failure to produce one is a
   finding.
4. **Round-trip test.** One analyst composes; a second, independently, glosses; the results
   must reconcile. Run on ≥5 items per round.
5. **No-orphan-morpheme audit.** Every morpheme in the published corpus must appear in the
   internal system with a full specification, and every internal morpheme intended for
   publication must appear ≥2 times in the corpus (per §1.6: cross-reference density, not
   lexicon size, is what reads as depth).

**Detected by:** C4 (gate), C6, C5, C7.

### F3 — Unproductive ambiguity: the Voynich outcome

**The failure.** The project's own resolution strategy generates this risk directly. Having
been told to be fragmentary and resistant to translation, the project produces material that
is genuinely resistant — and yields nothing. There are no confirmable readings, so no
interpretation ever accumulates; no interpretation accumulates, so there is no reading
history; there is no reading history, so the substitute for Tolkien's diachrony (§4.8) never
materializes. The result attracts attention and loses respect, and the loss is
irreversible, because a reader who concludes there is nothing underneath does not check
again. The tell is that the project cannot distinguish its own depth from noise, and neither
can anyone else.

**Early warnings.** "Meaning uncertain" appearing more than once per document with no
candidate readings. Competing readings that carry the same operational consequence. No
item in the corpus that a reader could get *wrong* — that is, nothing falsifiable. Every
disagreement in the reading history resolving in the same direction. In-world scholars who
never accumulate results.

**Countermeasures.**
1. **Foothold quota.** Every corpus release includes at least one item that is confirmable
   or refutable from published material alone — the HEL analogue of the tripod tablet
   (§4.2). Confirmation is by *independently known referent* (a route HEL observed), never
   by a translation key, so nothing about the builders is disclosed.
2. **Ambiguity triage.** Every ambiguous item must pass §4.11: name the readings, name the
   differing consequence of each, name the evidence that would decide between them, and
   state why it is missing. Items that fail are either specified or cut.
3. **Outsider decipherment trial.** Before each release, give the published material to
   someone with no access to the internal system and a time budget. They must derive at
   least one nontrivial correct reading. If they cannot, the release lacks traction — this
   is a stop-ship condition, not a note.
4. **Falsification budget.** Each round must retire at least one in-world reading using
   in-corpus evidence. A reading history in which nothing is ever refuted is decoration; a
   field that never converges on anything is not a field.
5. **Aim at Etruscan, not Voynich.** Adopt as a standing target the readable-but-not-
   understood condition (§4.4): secure surface, transparent morphology, opaque lexicon,
   partial and contested semantics. Say so in the design bible so that the target is
   reviewable.

**Detected by:** C11, C3, C8.

## 7. What this benchmark asks for, in one paragraph

Beat Tolkien where he is weak — syntax, pragmatics, sociolinguistics, obligatory categories
English cannot carry, and modeled misunderstanding — and match him where he is strong by
substitution rather than imitation: a system complete enough that its completeness is
*detectable* from any fragment, a diachrony relocated from the object to the people reading
it, and emotional weight carried by provenance rather than by translation. Do it without
ever saying who wrote the thing. The success condition is that a reader who spends a year
with the material finds it getting *more* coherent, not less, and still cannot tell you what
the builders were.

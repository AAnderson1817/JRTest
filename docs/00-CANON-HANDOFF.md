# Horizon Expedition League

## Canon review and fictional-language brief for Claude

**Status:** Canon audit and design handoff  
**Prepared:** 11 August 2026  
**Purpose:** Give Claude enough authoritative context to design a fictional language for the Horizon Expedition League universe without inventing over unresolved canon.

---

## 1. Executive conclusion

The Horizon Expedition League canon currently defines a compelling cosmological and institutional core, but it does **not** yet define an alien civilization, a native speaker community, or a canonical alien language.

The strongest established material is:

- The Horizon Expedition League is an ancient, secretive organization based in Chicago.
- Its history is tied to a Native American burial mound or geographic hotspot near the mouth of the Chicago River.
- HEL studies and cautiously uses an incomprehensible, prehuman extraterrestrial artifact called the **Black Box**.
- The Black Box enables travel through fixed, one-directional, interlocking timespace conduits commonly described as **streams**.
- A route is not necessarily reversible or geographically intuitive. Reaching B from A may require A → C → B, and returning to the same year may mean emerging in another city.
- HEL exists to explore, document, contain, and protect these phenomena—not to master them.
- HEL culture combines science, archaeology, intelligence tradecraft, ritual, myth, secrecy, and stewardship.
- HEL is never omniscient. Its members manage a system they do not truly understand.
- PNP contributes underlying game and product systems only. Its lore was fully replaced by HEL lore; the Culture Codex was set aside.
- The **Standard Egg** is an internal development placeholder for a shared precursor state, not an established diegetic name or final creature design.
- The physical pin is best understood as a League credential or authorization for custodianship. It does not contain, create, or predetermine an entity.

For language design, this means Claude should not casually create “the HEL language” as though the League were a separate ethnic civilization or as though the builders of the Black Box were known. The safest and most productive language target is a **fragmentarily recovered nonhuman substrate associated with Black Box interfaces, route markings, or related artifacts**, presented through HEL’s imperfect human transliteration and analysis. This is a recommendation, not yet canon.

## 2. Canon authority model

Claude should treat every statement in this brief according to one of four authority levels.

| Level | Meaning | Claude may do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A — Established canon** | Recovered as a settled premise or governing direction | Build directly on it; do not contradict it |
| **B — Working canon** | A developed direction that supports current systems but has not been shown to receive final ratification | Use provisionally and flag dependencies |
| **C — Recommendation** | A new synthesis in this handoff intended to make language design coherent | Develop it, but label it for approval |
| **D — Open** | Deliberately undecided, unsupported, or mysterious | Offer options; do not silently decide |

The central discipline is simple: **plausibility is not canon**. HEL benefits from unanswered questions. A conlang should deepen the mystery without solving the Black Box, naming its creators as fact, or supplying a complete ancient history.

## 3. Established universe canon

### 3.1 The Horizon Expedition League

**Authority: A — Established canon**

The Horizon Expedition League is an ancient, secretive organization centered in Chicago. It is not merely a modern scientific agency formed after a recent discovery. Its institutional roots extend much farther back, although no founding year, continuous organizational lineage, founder, charter, or historical public identity has been fixed.

HEL is embedded within ordinary institutions and infrastructure. This implies concealment, compartmentalization, inherited access, and the ability to operate across generations without publicly declaring its real purpose. The specific fronts, agencies, buildings, ranks, governing council, recruitment system, and funding mechanisms remain undefined.

HEL’s role is best summarized as **stewardship under radical uncertainty**. It studies, interprets, cautiously uses, protects, and contains phenomena that exceed its understanding. It is neither the creator of the transit system nor the sovereign master of it.

An earlier summary described HEL members as “curators of cosmic phenomena.” That phrase accurately captures the established tone, but should be treated as descriptive English rather than a mandatory in-world title.

### 3.2 Chicago origin and geographic anchor

**Authority: A — Established canon**

HEL’s origin is tied to a Native American burial mound or geographic hotspot near the mouth of the Chicago River. The canon supports an ancient significance attached to the location; it does not establish why the location matters, who first recognized it, whether the mound and the anomaly are causally related, or whether the Black Box arrived there by accident or design.

Important negative constraints:

- It is **not established** that any real Indigenous nation created the Black Box.
- It is **not established** that an Indigenous language descends from, encodes, or deciphers the Black Box language.
- It is **not established** that the mound exists because of the artifact.
- It is **not established** that HEL legitimately inherited the site or its knowledge.

Claude should therefore avoid treating real Algonquian, Anishinaabe, Potawatomi, Miami-Illinois, or other Indigenous vocabulary as convenient “alien” source material. The Chicago connection can influence HEL’s institutional history without collapsing Indigenous history into extraterrestrial mythology.

### 3.3 The Black Box

**Authority: A — Established canon**

The Black Box is:

- prehuman;
- extraterrestrial;
- archaic or ancient;
- permanently encased or otherwise unrevealed;
- visually unspecified;
- not understood by HEL at a fundamental level; and
- capable of enabling timespace travel.

“Black Box” is a human operational label. It may describe an opaque system whose inputs and outputs can be observed without access to its internal workings; it need not describe its literal color, shape, or original purpose.

The artifact’s builders, age, intended use, construction, power source, consciousness, language, and relationship to other phenomena are all open. It is also open whether the Box itself performs transit, controls access to an existing network, predicts routes, or merely permits humans to interact with something larger.

The Black Box must retain explanatory resistance. A fictional language associated with it should create evidence and interpretive leverage, not become a universal answer key.

### 3.4 The streams and travel topology

**Authority: A — Established canon**

The Black Box permits access to timespace conduits analogous to stellar streams. “Stream” is the established human metaphor. The following properties are canon:

- Streams follow fixed paths or constrained routes.
- They are one-directional.
- They form interlocking loops.
- Routes can cross both space and time.
- Departure and arrival are not symmetrical.
- A traveler may need to route through an intermediate point to reach a destination that appears closer.
- Returning to a familiar date does not guarantee returning to the same place.
- Example logic: A → C → B may be required because A → B is unavailable.
- Example displacement: an attempted return to “2024” may emerge in Los Angeles rather than Chicago.

The system therefore behaves more like navigation through a directed network than free time travel. The vocabulary of “past,” “future,” “back,” “home,” “before,” “after,” “near,” and “return” becomes unreliable unless it specifies both spatial and temporal frames.

Still open:

- whether routes are perfectly immutable or only appear fixed;
- how destinations are identified;
- whether every node is a place, an era, a condition, or some combination;
- how precise arrivals can be;
- whether streams have windows, tides, capacities, hazards, or costs;
- whether matter, information, or causality can move outside the travel direction;
- whether loops are closed in a strict causal sense;
- what happens if an expedition alters a visited era;
- whether multiple travelers can take different paths to the same event;
- whether HEL’s maps describe reality or merely accumulated observations.

### 3.5 Mission and ethics

**Authority: A — Established canon**

HEL’s mission comprises:

- exploration;
- study and documentation;
- protection;
- containment;
- prevention of weaponization;
- prevention of destabilization; and
- prevention or mitigation of unintended consequences within the interlocking loops.

This mission gives HEL a fundamentally conservative relationship to discovery. It is willing to travel and learn, but it is not a conquest organization, an extractive colonial power by default, or a technology company trying to commercialize the network.

The canon does not guarantee that HEL always lives up to these principles. Internal abuses, dissent, historical wrongdoing, compromised cells, or competing interpretations remain possible, but no such faction or scandal has yet been established.

### 3.6 Institutional culture and tone

**Authority: A — Established canon**

HEL culture blends:

- empirical science;
- archaeology;
- intelligence tradecraft;
- inherited myth;
- superstition;
- protective ritual;
- secrecy;
- documentation; and
- stewardship.

These elements should not be flattened into either “hard science agency” or “occult order.” HEL is compelling because the two modes coexist. A procedure may be statistically validated, recorded in a field manual, and still resemble a ritual because nobody knows which part is causally necessary. A traditional phrase may be repeated because expeditions that omit it fail, even if the phrase’s translation has been lost.

The desired epistemic tone is cautious and intellectual. HEL knows more than outsiders, but far less than it wishes. Its archives should contain competing analyses, deprecated readings, confidence levels, route annotations, field reports, and unresolved contradictions.

### 3.7 Cosmological posture

**Authority: A — Established canon**

HEL anchors the exploration mechanics of the universe but does not define or comprehend the whole cosmos. The existence of the Black Box demonstrates extraterrestrial intelligence or manufacture; it does not establish the number, nature, survival, or intentions of alien civilizations.

No canon currently establishes:

- a galactic government;
- a known builder species;
- a universal precursor civilization;
- a pantheon;
- a settled multiverse model;
- deterministic history;
- a war between cosmic factions;
- a universal translation technology; or
- an omniscient archive.

Claude should resist introducing any of these merely to support a language family or origin story.

## 4. Systems retcon and entity canon

### 4.1 Relationship to PNP

**Authority: A — Established governing direction**

PNP supplies game and product systems. HEL supplies the entire fictional universe. This was defined as a **full retcon, not a crossover**.

Consequences:

- PNP lore does not coexist with HEL lore.
- PNP proper nouns, cosmology, cultures, and metaphysical explanations do not carry over automatically.
- A useful mechanic may be translated into HEL terms, but its former narrative explanation is discarded.
- The Culture Codex was explicitly set aside rather than adapted into HEL.

Claude should not use old PNP linguistic, cultural, elemental, or naming assumptions unless the user separately reapproves them for HEL.

### 4.2 The Standard Egg

**Authority: A for its development role; D for its diegetic status**

“Standard Egg” is an internal systems-development placeholder. It represents a universal, undifferentiated precursor state used to prototype an entity system. It is **not** established as the final in-world name, form, taxonomy, or lore.

The current 3D Standard Egg asset proves that a benchmark asset exists. Its title and visual presentation do not by themselves canonize the object’s name, appearance, materials, markings, or origin.

The intended system-level qualities of the precursor are:

- every participant begins from a shared state;
- final identity is not predetermined;
- differentiation follows environment, exposure, statistics, care, and player behavior; and
- development should create distinct outcomes rather than reveal a preassigned creature.

Any alien labels engraved on, spoken by, or used for the Standard Egg would be premature unless the user first chooses what the precursor actually is in HEL canon.

### 4.3 Physical pin and custodianship

**Authority: B — Working canon with strong systems alignment**

The physical NFC pin was translated into HEL as a credential or authorization. Scanning it authorizes a participant’s custodianship of a precursor already under League protection.

The pin:

- does not contain the entity;
- does not create the entity;
- does not determine its final identity; and
- functions as an access or custodial credential within HEL systems.

The intended loop includes:

- habitat;
- care and study;
- expeditions;
- acquisition of materials and data;
- fabrication; and
- later adaptations.

The exact credential name, issuer, series, authorization formula, legal status, and inscription system remain open.

### 4.4 Entity Ontology v0.1

**Authority: B — Proposed working canon; final approval not recovered**

The July 2026 development process authorized creation of an “HEL Entity Ontology — Working Canon v0.1.” The resulting direction proposed that:

- the precursor is a living intelligence whose physical form remains unresolved;
- identity precedes or is not reducible to body;
- emergence, adaptation, and convergence shape development;
- habitat, care, relationship, exposure, and player behavior drive differentiation;
- the participant is a custodian rather than an owner;
- adoption describes a relationship, not possession;
- the pin authorizes access but does not contain or generate life;
- entities retain agency; and
- five adaptation or research domains could organize development.

Because no later final ratification was recovered, Claude must not treat the specific ontology, five domains, or terminology as immutable canon. The broad custodianship model is highly consistent with HEL’s stewardship ethic and is the best current working direction.

Open entity questions include:

- precursor name;
- origin;
- physical and metaphysical nature;
- whether all precursors are one species;
- whether they are native to the stream network;
- whether they are related to the Black Box builders;
- collective name;
- sentience and communicative capacity;
- life cycle;
- relationship to matter, information, and time;
- final adaptation domains; and
- whether their language is learned, innate, emergent, embodied, or absent.

## 5. What language actually needs to be designed

### 5.1 The unresolved speaker problem

There is no canonical answer to “Who speaks the fictional language?” This must be solved before Claude can responsibly produce a full conlang.

Three viable targets exist:

| Target | What it is | Canon fit | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **HEL operational register** | Specialized English-derived jargon, codes, titles, and route notation used by League personnel | Very high | This is an argot, not a truly independent language |
| **Recovered Black Box substrate** | Fragmentary nonhuman language or symbolic system observed in interfaces, artifacts, or route phenomena | Highest for a true conlang | Can reveal too much or falsely imply the builders are understood |
| **Entity language** | Communication used by precursors or their developed forms | Potentially strong later | Entity nature and communicative capacity are not settled |

**Recommendation (Authority C):** Claude should design the **Recovered Black Box Substrate** first, while also defining the HEL transliteration, annotation, and operational terms used to discuss it. This produces a genuine fictional language without requiring a known living alien civilization.

The substrate should be framed as a human reconstruction from incomplete evidence. HEL scholars may disagree about whether it is:

- a language;
- machine control syntax;
- route metadata;
- a safety protocol;
- a mathematical notation;
- ritualized speech recorded by unknown users; or
- several systems incorrectly grouped together.

This ambiguity belongs in the design.

### 5.2 Recommended language layers

**Authority: C — Recommendation**

Claude should distinguish four layers:

1. **Underlying system** — the actual nonhuman communicative structure, only partly reconstructable.
2. **HEL scholarly transliteration** — a standardized way humans represent recurring units.
3. **HEL field pronunciation** — a speakable approximation used in operations, which may be phonetically wrong.
4. **English gloss** — operational translations such as “outbound node,” “verified return path,” or “do not traverse.”

This prevents readable dialogue from implying perfect decipherment. A field agent can say a transliterated route term while a linguist notes that it may encode topology rather than meaning “route.”

### 5.3 Knowledge limits the language must preserve

The language must not establish as fact:

- the name of the Black Box builders;
- what the builders looked like;
- where or when they lived;
- why the stream network exists;
- whether the Box was intended for travel;
- whether the substrate was spoken biologically;
- whether a text is ancient testimony rather than machine output;
- whether recurring first-person-like forms indicate a person, system, location, or route perspective; or
- whether HEL translations are correct.

A good design should permit multiple analyses. Claude may provide a preferred scholarly model, but every foundational claim should carry a confidence level and at least one plausible competing interpretation.

## 6. Canon-derived linguistic requirements

These are not predetermined grammar rules. They are the semantic and structural pressures the established universe places on any suitable language.

### 6.1 Directed rather than symmetric motion

Because streams are one-directional, the language should encode a sharp distinction between:

- motion permitted by the network;
- hypothetical reverse motion;
- arrival at a node;
- departure from a node;
- traversal through an intermediate node; and
- recurrence at a similar but not identical spacetime coordinate.

A generic verb equivalent to English “go” is unlikely to be enough for route operations.

### 6.2 Space and time as a combined coordinate

English separates “where” and “when.” HEL travel makes that separation dangerous. A language associated with the streams may instead treat a destination as one composite event-location.

Claude should consider grammatical or lexical distinctions among:

- same place, different time;
- same time, different place;
- same route node, different traversal;
- predicted destination;
- observed destination;
- intended destination;
- origin-relative time;
- traveler-relative time; and
- archive-relative chronology.

This is one of the most important canon-driven features.

### 6.3 Loop and path topology

Interlocking loops suggest a conceptual vocabulary based on:

- sequence;
- adjacency;
- junction;
- convergence;
- recurrence;
- inaccessible reverse edges;
- nested cycles;
- path dependence; and
- route completion.

If Claude develops a script, a topological or path-based writing system is an excellent fit. However, “circular glyphs because time loops” would be shallow. The writing system should encode direction, node relation, or traversal order in a functional way.

### 6.4 Evidentiality and uncertainty

HEL’s defining intellectual virtue is knowing the difference between observation and explanation. The language design should support precise distinctions among:

- directly observed;
- instrumentally measured;
- repeated but unexplained;
- inferred from route behavior;
- recovered from archive;
- translated with high confidence;
- translated provisionally;
- reported by an expedition;
- contradicted by later evidence; and
- ritually preserved without known meaning.

This may live in the nonhuman grammar, HEL’s annotation system, or both. It should be central rather than decorative.

### 6.5 Causality under constraint

The streams create uncertainty about intervention and consequence. The language should make room for:

- cause observed in ordinary sequence;
- cause inferred across a loop;
- an action whose consequence precedes the traveler’s departure frame;
- a precaution taken because of possible loop effects;
- a prohibited action with unknown consequences; and
- a stable correlation that HEL refuses to call causal.

Claude should not settle the physics, but the grammar and lexicon should reveal that its users need to discuss these distinctions.

### 6.6 Stewardship rather than mastery

HEL’s ethics favor protection, containment, documentation, and custodianship. Its institutional language should distinguish:

- possession;
- temporary custody;
- authorized access;
- responsibility for care;
- containment without ownership;
- observation without interference; and
- protection of an entity that retains agency.

If the working entity ontology is ratified, ordinary possessive constructions such as “my entity” may be considered colloquial or ethically disfavored inside HEL.

### 6.7 Secrecy and compartmentalization

HEL operates covertly. Its language ecosystem should plausibly include:

- clearance-limited terms;
- deliberately incomplete route names;
- neutral public cover terms;
- archival names that outlive their original explanations;
- code words whose literal meanings are intentionally mundane;
- formal warnings;
- compact field commands; and
- names that change when a hypothesis is disproven but operations must continue.

This does not require an elaborate “secret language.” Institutional vocabulary and controlled ambiguity are more credible.

### 6.8 Ritual without confirmed mysticism

Some HEL procedures can resemble ritual because inherited actions may work without understood mechanisms. Language may have formulae that are:

- phonologically conserved;
- semantically opaque;
- performed in a fixed order;
- retained because deviation correlates with failure; and
- analyzed differently by scientists, archivists, and field personnel.

The language should not prove that belief, magic, or prayer powers the system. It should preserve the possibility that ritual is degraded protocol—or that “protocol” is an inadequate modern label.

## 7. Recommended aesthetic and structural direction

**All of this section is Authority C — Recommendation, not canon.**

### 7.1 Overall character

The substrate should feel:

- ancient without sounding like faux-Latin;
- alien without becoming unpronounceable noise;
- precise about relation, path, observation, and state;
- poor at mapping neatly onto human ideas of tense and location;
- capable of terse operational forms;
- fragmentary in the corpus; and
- resistant to complete translation.

Avoid the generic conlang pattern of adding apostrophes, harsh consonant clusters, and a list of fantasy honorifics. The alien quality should arise from conceptual structure.

### 7.2 Human pronunciation

HEL field speech should be an approximation optimized for repeatability over authenticity. Claude can define:

- a limited transliteration inventory;
- stress rules chosen by HEL rather than recovered from evidence;
- symbols or features that have no agreed pronunciation;
- variant traditions inherited by different archive branches; and
- checksum-like repetition used to avoid route-command errors.

The distinction between written unit and spoken approximation should remain visible in the documentation.

### 7.3 Morphology and syntax

A strong design would make route relations grammatically primary. One possible architecture:

- roots identify states or nodes rather than concrete nouns;
- directional morphology encodes allowed traversal;
- evidential suffixes encode source and confidence;
- a coordinate phrase binds spatial and temporal reference;
- agent marking is optional or structurally secondary;
- commands distinguish simulation, preparation, authorization, and execution; and
- negation distinguishes impossible, forbidden, unavailable, and unverified.

This is a design proposal, not a demand. Claude should explain why its chosen system fits HEL better than conventional subject–verb–object grammar.

### 7.4 Script

A suitable script could use:

- directed strokes;
- junction marks;
- nested contours;
- terminal versus continuing nodes;
- orientation to indicate traversal order;
- annotations outside the primary path for evidence or confidence; and
- distinct forms for observed versus predicted connections.

The script must still be producible in practical game assets, UI, documents, props, and signage. Claude should deliver both a high-concept form and a simplified field hand.

The current Standard Egg asset must not be reverse-engineered into script canon merely because it exists. Any proposed visual language should be approved independently.

### 7.5 Naming

Names should reflect HEL’s layered epistemology:

- **English operational names:** practical and sometimes misleading, such as “Black Box” and “stream.”
- **Catalog identifiers:** stable codes that survive changing interpretations.
- **Recovered forms:** substrate strings whose status may be disputed.
- **Field names:** memorable names used by expedition personnel.
- **Historical names:** inherited labels from older HEL archives.

Claude should avoid assuming every place or artifact has a true proper name waiting to be translated.

## 8. Prohibited shortcuts and likely canon errors

Claude should not:

1. Name or describe the Black Box builders as established fact.
2. make the Black Box fully decipherable;
3. turn “Black Box” into a confirmed alien self-designation;
4. create unrestricted portals or reversible time travel;
5. let grammar erase the fixed, one-way network topology;
6. imply that a year alone identifies a destination;
7. convert HEL into a public galactic government or conquering military;
8. make HEL omniscient or uniformly correct;
9. import PNP lore or the Culture Codex;
10. treat Standard Egg as a canonical alien term or final form;
11. make the NFC pin an egg, soul container, summoning object, or deterministic genetic key;
12. assume entities are property;
13. establish an entity language before settling what the entities are;
14. borrow a real Indigenous language to imply it is extraterrestrial;
15. equate the Chicago burial-mound connection with proof of alien authorship;
16. use a simple English substitution cipher as the alien language;
17. use faux-Latin merely to communicate institutional age;
18. present every translation with equal certainty; or
19. explain every ritual as either definitely magical or definitely meaningless superstition.

## 9. Canon gaps Claude must surface, not silently fill

### 9.1 Required decision before final conlang production

The user should choose the primary target:

- recovered Black Box substrate;
- HEL operational argot;
- precursor/entity language; or
- a coordinated package containing the first two.

**Recommended default:** a coordinated package containing the recovered substrate plus HEL’s transliteration and operational register.

### 9.2 Black Box and substrate questions

- Is any visible or audible output from the Black Box confirmed?
- Are there inscriptions on the casing, external equipment, route nodes, or recovered artifacts?
- Does HEL know that all samples belong to one system?
- Can the Box receive commands, or does HEL only configure surrounding machinery?
- Is the substrate written, acoustic, electromagnetic, spatial, mathematical, or multimodal?
- How large is the recovered corpus?
- Has any translation been independently verified?
- Should the language be usable for dialogue, or mainly for inscriptions and field commands?

### 9.3 HEL institutional questions

- What does HEL call itself internally, and how old is the English name?
- What are its ranks, departments, archives, and field roles?
- Does it have regional branches or only Chicago cells?
- How does it preserve institutional knowledge across centuries?
- Are ritual formulae translated substrate, inherited human practice, or both?
- What level of secrecy applies to different terms?
- Does HEL have a formal motto, oath, warning, or expedition liturgy?

### 9.4 Entity questions

- Are precursors biologically alive, informationally alive, or something else?
- Do they communicate before differentiation?
- Do developed entities share a language?
- Is communication symbolic, emotional, behavioral, dreamlike, or verbal?
- Are entities indigenous to particular stream nodes?
- Are entities connected to the Black Box builders?
- Can they understand the substrate more naturally than humans?
- Does “adoption” remain the preferred public and internal term?

### 9.5 Production questions

- Does the language need to support spoken dialogue by actors?
- Does it need a Unicode-compatible font or only bespoke glyph assets?
- Where will it appear: UI, props, creature markings, maps, documents, voice, signage, or all of these?
- How many words and grammatical constructions are required for the first release?
- Must players be able to learn and solve it?
- Should correct interpretation affect route navigation or remain atmospheric?

## 10. Recommended Claude deliverables

Claude should not begin by generating a dictionary. It should deliver the following in order:

### Phase 1 — Canon alignment

1. State which language target it is designing.
2. List every new assumption required.
3. Separate assumptions that expose cosmology from assumptions needed only for production.
4. Present two or three viable language architectures.
5. Recommend one and wait for approval.

### Phase 2 — Language specification

1. Speaker/user model and evidence corpus.
2. Phonology or human approximation system.
3. Transliteration standard.
4. Morphology.
5. Syntax.
6. Evidentiality and confidence marking.
7. Spacetime-coordinate grammar.
8. Direction and traversal grammar.
9. Negation: impossible, forbidden, unavailable, and unverified.
10. Register differences between archive, laboratory, and field use.
11. Naming rules.
12. Script and simplified field hand.
13. Historical and competing HEL interpretations.

### Phase 3 — Applied corpus

Claude should create a small, controlled corpus before expanding vocabulary:

- a Black Box warning with two competing translations;
- a route label containing origin, directed edge, confidence, and observed arrival;
- an expedition authorization formula;
- a containment notice;
- a field command sequence;
- an archival passage whose old translation is now disputed;
- a custodianship phrase that avoids ownership;
- three node or artifact names across catalog, field, and recovered layers; and
- a short exchange showing how HEL personnel actually pronounce substrate terms.

Each example should include:

- native or reconstructed script;
- transliteration;
- morpheme-by-morpheme gloss;
- natural English translation;
- confidence rating; and
- canon note identifying new assumptions.

### Phase 4 — Production package

1. Core lexicon sized to actual first-release needs.
2. Naming generator rules rather than an uncontrolled list of names.
3. Pronunciation guide for performers.
4. Glyph construction grid and font/asset guidance.
5. UI legibility rules.
6. Canon ledger recording every approved term and meaning.
7. Deprecated interpretations retained for archival storytelling.

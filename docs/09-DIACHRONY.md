# The Diachronic Layer — two histories, both human

> **Authority C throughout.** A design proposal for approval, not canon. The code is
> `hel/diachrony.py`; every table below is printed by it, and `d1_check()`,
> `d2_check()` and `algonquian_check()` run on every build. Nothing here gives the
> substrate a phonology or a history: A §6.1 forbids it, and Phase 1 Q2 (amended) put
> the construction standard somewhere canon permits it.

## 1. The rule this layer obeys

Phase 1 Q2 asked for Tolkien's construction standard without his disclosure standard:
proto-forms, regular sound laws, every modern form derived and motivated, and still no
key for the reader. Tolkien's own vector — etymology you can see in place names — is
structurally unavailable here, because the substrate cannot name a place.

So the depth goes where canon permits it. **Every sound change in this document happens
in a human mouth or on a committee's table.** The substrate's forms are HEL's radio code
(A §6.1): *nothing about the sound of* harnuli *is evidence about anything.* What has a
history is HEL's handling of the substrate, and that history is regular enough to
reconstruct.

There are two of them.

- **D1, the code.** The 1908 Transcription Standard, its leaks and drift, and the
  Reformed List of 1988 that replaced it by five ordered laws.
- **D2, the recitation.** The Ch-4 formulae as two branch traditions carry them —
  the Chicago notebooks and the Second branch — and the *Common Recitation HEL's
  comparativists reconstruct behind both.

## 2. D1 — the code, 1908 to 1988

**Engine finding F-08 dates the list.** The closed forty-eight-syllable list of A §6.1b
cannot be 1908's: the retired 1908 strings are not buildable from it, and the
reconstruction below needs thirteen 1908 syllables the list does not have (*a, i, o,
la, ma, wa, we, lum, ku, te, se, ho, ne*). The printed list is the Reformed List's. The
1908 code was an open inventory with a coda-poor core.

### The laws, in order

| Law | Change | What it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **L1** | nasal-coda merger: coda *m* becomes *n* | *lum-pe-ki* → *lunpeki* |
| **L2** | *m* and *w* only in closed syllables: an open *mV*, *wV* takes a coda (*n* after *m*, *l* after *w*) | A §6.1b: /m/ and /w/ are licensed only in closed syllables |
| **L3** | an onsetless open syllable closes with the unit's heel — hiatus is a boundary cue | *a-he-si* → *arhesi*; *i-pe-lu* → *inpelu*; the four onsetless syllables of the list |
| **L5** | minimum coda rate: a unit of three or more syllables with no coda takes the heel on the first syllable that can bear it | A §6.1c; *ha-nu-li* → *harnuli* |
| **L4** | band accommodation: an unlisted syllable moves its vowel to the nearest in its consonant's band | *ru-kal-te* → *rukalti*; *ni-ku* → *niki* (then R4) |

L5 applies before L4; the order matters, and the engine applies them in this order to
every unit. The **heel** is the coda the 1988 re-collation recorded on a unit's first
notch cluster — a fact about the object that the 1908 committee had read as wear.

### The irregulars, each with a cause

| Class | Units | Cause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **R1** borrowing | six categories | The 1908 analysts' English and Latin mnemonics (A §6.1e), replaced wholesale in 1988 over the 1949 objection. Held in the deprecated register; not reproduced |
| **R2** collision | `hilun` | The regular output would have collided with another commanded unit on a bad line: the 1908 gap marker would have become *wal*, which is UNW |
| **R3** scribal error | `hekasni` | The 1908 form was a branch copyist's /h/–/k/ confusion (the pair the code cannot tell apart, A §6.1b), consolidated in 1908 and corrected against the object in 1988; the laws would have given *ke-* |
| **R4** analogy | `kihar pukal rolun nikur` | Two-syllable fillers are exempt from the coda rule; the 1988 committee closed them anyway so the coordinate paradigm would have one shape (A §6.1c) |
| **R5** re-collation | `sarkile` | The re-collation found a third notch cluster the 1908 committee had read as wear; the unit gained a syllable. The Second branch recites three |

**Result.** 52 units: 45 derive regularly, 7 are irregular with a stated cause, none is
unexplained. Of the 47 core roots and ritual units, 3 carried a coda anywhere in 1908
(*lunpeki*, *walsenti*, *nisarlu*) and all 47 do after 1988. (A §6.1c's *nine of
roughly ninety* counts the formatives too, whose 1908 strings are not reproduced; the
verifier lists it as calibration, not error.)

## 3. D2 — the recitation

The nine formulae survive in two traditions that have transmitted them separately for
an undetermined period. **The Chicago notebooks** learned from print after 1908 and say
what is printed. **The Second branch** never did. Their differences are regular, and a
comparativist can do with them what the substrate itself forbids: reconstruct.

### The Common Recitation

Behind both stands the starred *Common Recitation. It is the printed code everywhere but
four units, which have *f* where print has *p*:

| Code (1988) | *Common | Chicago | Second |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `-pel` BAR tail | \*fel | peɪl | fil |
| `pe-` BAR head | \*fe | peɪ | fi |
| `rosilpe` | \*rosilfe | roʊsilpeɪ | rosilfi |
| `inpelu` | \*infelu | inpeɪlu | infilu |
| `lunpeki` | \*lunpeki | lunpeɪki | lunpihi |

### The laws

**Second branch**, in order:

1. **S1** — initial stress retained. The branch never adopted the Standard's final
   stress.
2. **S2** — *k* becomes *h* before *i*.
3. **S3** — unstressed *e* becomes *i* and *o* becomes *u*; a bound formative is
   unstressed throughout.

**Chicago**:

1. **C1** — labiodentals replaced from print, 1908: *f* > *p*. The Standard has no
   labiodental (A §6.1), and crews who learned the formulae from print say what is
   printed.
2. **C2** — final stress, from the Standard.
3. **C3** — the Reading Room diphthongs, all positions: *e* > [eɪ], *o* > [oʊ]; no
   reduction, ever.

**H4** — the attrition rule Phase 1 imported from architecture B — applies in the Second
branch only. Head halves are spoken unstressed and fast, and the branch that never
learned from print has lost them: *ru-yuntiha* is recited *ˈyuntiha*.

### Relative chronology, from one form

The THRU tail \*ken is *kin* in the Second branch, not *hin*. Had S3 (raising) come
before S2 (palatalisation), the raised *e* would have fed S2 and produced *hin*. So S2
is older than S3 — Tredgold's argument of 1967, and the only relative date the branch
data supports.

### One irregular, with its cause

S3 predicts **sin** for the closure marker `-sen` (FRB). The Second branch says **sen**.
Pellingham (1953) filed it as the branch's one exception: its crews spoke English,
heard *forbidden* in *sin*, and did not say it. That is homophony avoidance, which real
languages do, and it lands on the one morpheme where the Archive's own residue makes the
same objection in committee language: English *forbidden* would supply a forbidder, and
the record names none.

**Engine finding F-27.** The irregular exists because the collision screen caught the
regular output. It is recorded in the build (`SECOND_IRREGULAR`), printed in the tables,
and carried by the page's engine.

Phase 1 §5 item 3b asked that at least one law be accepted by only two of the three
schools. Two are. **The *f*:** four recited units have Chicago *p* against Second *f*,
and one — *lunpeki* — has *p* in both. The comparativists reconstruct two sounds and
read Chicago's *p* as print; the Instrument school reads the Second branch's *f* as its
own lenition of *p* and *lunpeki* as a post-1908 loan from Chicago into the one formula
the Second branch adopted late. Both fit every form. **The heels:** the 1988 heels agree
with the recitations' codas unit for unit — two lines of evidence, or one: the
Palimpsest school notes that the re-collators knew the recitations and found the heels
they expected, which is the objection A-reserve §4.1 prints about unit boundaries.

The reconstruction bottoms out in a human mouth. It cannot be pushed further, and
Phase 1's P-CH4 keeps it that way: whether the formulae are translated substrate,
inherited practice, or both is not represented anywhere, including in the sealed key.

## 4. The constraint that governed every form

The recitation is a human tradition inside a Chicago-based institution, so its
reconstructed phonology must be *affirmatively* incompatible with Algonquian,
Anishinaabe, Potawatomi and Miami-Illinois phonology (prohibited shortcuts #14 and
#15; A §6.1a) — not merely different. `algonquian_check()` enforces it on every
reconstructed and branch form: no vowel length, no tautosyllabic cluster (above all
*-shk- -nd- -mb- -ng-*), no /tʃ/ or /ʃ/, and /r/ allowed.

**Engine finding F-25: two drafts of this layer were withdrawn on exactly that test.**
The first gave the Common Recitation voiced stops, and its daughters grew *-nd-*,
*-mb-* and *-ng-*. The second lost intervocalic /h/ in the Second branch, which
produced doubled vowels that read as Ojibwe double-vowel spelling of length, and a
word-final *-kai* that reads as Polynesian. What stands has /f/ — a labiodental, absent
from every Algonquian language, and precisely the kind of sound the 1908 committee
excluded — and no cluster, no length, no postalveolar.

## 5. How to use it

- `python3 -c "from hel import diachrony as d; print(d.d1_check(), d.d2_check())"` —
  both empty on a clean build.
- `d.recite(text, "chicago" | "second")` — any formula as a branch says it.
- The Reading Room's **Recitation** tab prints every formula in the 1949 Standard, the
  Chicago notebooks and the Second branch, the correspondence table, the D1 table, and
  a game: shown a starred Common form, predict what the Second branch says.
